# 73. Binding Report Language Style Rule

## 73.1 Preferred report voice

The governing reader-facing voice is:

> **Formal engineering narrative — technically rigorous, measured and explanatory, without sounding legalistic, forensic, bureaucratic or unnecessarily categorical.**

The preferred tonal benchmark is the explanatory style demonstrated by the accepted V0.2 Assembly Draft.

This style may be referred to during development as the **V0.2 softer engineering style**.

“Softened” refers to the **delivery of the engineering conclusion**, not to the strength of the evidence or the technical conclusion itself.

---

## 73.2 What “softened” means

Reader-facing narrative should:

1. explain the engineering reasoning progressively;
2. use ordinary professional engineering language wherever specialised control-language is not required;
3. read as an engineer explaining the observed condition, its significance and the recommended direction to the client;
4. allow conclusions to emerge from the evidence and reasoning rather than repeatedly announcing them as formal determinations;
5. use connected explanatory paragraphs with sufficient breathing room for a management, operations or maintenance reader to follow the logic;
6. weave evidence limitations and engineering qualifications naturally into the narrative;
7. retain clear cause-and-effect transitions such as:
   - “The evidence indicates…”
   - “The inspection identified…”
   - “The observed condition suggests…”
   - “Taken together, these findings indicate…”
   - “This is important because…”
   - “The significance of this condition is…”
   - “The recommended response is therefore…”
   - “Further measurement is required to establish…”
   - “The available evidence does not establish…”
8. generally follow the reader sequence:

> **What was observed → what pattern or mechanism is indicated → why it matters → what should be done / verified next.**

The reader should be guided to the engineering conclusion rather than confronted with a sequence of verdict-like statements.

---

## 73.3 What “softened” does NOT mean

Language harmonisation must **not**:

- hedge directly verified facts;
- weaken justified engineering conclusions;
- dilute recommendations;
- remove evidence qualifications;
- replace every definitive statement with “may”, “might” or “possibly”;
- obscure a defect or consequence that is directly demonstrated;
- make the report diplomatic at the expense of accuracy;
- remove required technical terminology from implementation, verification or structural-assessment sections.

Where evidence directly establishes a condition, the report may and should state it clearly.

Examples:
- a directly visible defect **is present**;
- active water escape **is occurring** where the video demonstrates it;
- mistracking **is occurring** where the evidence establishes it;
- competent structural assessment **is required** where the condition warrants it.

The surrounding narrative should explain those facts in measured professional engineering language.

---

## 73.4 Separation of narrative language from control-register language

Phase 7 intentionally introduced precise implementation and verification terminology. That terminology remains technically valuable, but it must be used in the correct layer of the report.

### Appropriate in reader-facing narrative
Prefer natural explanatory wording such as:

- evidence indicates;
- inspection identified;
- findings suggest / support;
- taken together;
- significance of the condition;
- recommended response;
- requires further measurement / verification;
- available evidence does not establish;
- should be assessed / checked / confirmed.

### Appropriate primarily in technical-control layers
The following vocabulary remains valid but should be used selectively outside Part XI, verification registers, acceptance criteria, structural-assessment requirements and technical appendices:

- strongest supportable mechanism;
- explicit verification requirement;
- functional close-out;
- acceptance gate;
- formal disposition;
- representative operating proof;
- evidence-strength classification;
- task-completion criterion;
- authority boundary;
- implementation dependency.

These terms are not prohibited. The rule is that **control-register vocabulary must not dominate the narrative voice**.

---

## 73.5 Client-positioning rule

The preferred report relationship with the client is:

> **“Here is what we observed, here is what the evidence indicates it means, and here is the engineering direction we recommend.”**

The narrative should avoid unnecessarily sounding like:

> **“Here is our determination, here is the evidentiary ruling, and here is what must be demonstrated to close it.”**

The second form remains appropriate inside implementation/verification controls where formal close-out requirements are genuinely being defined.

---

## 73.6 Sentence-construction rule

Where practical, reader-facing prose should favour:

> **Observation / context → interpretation → significance → recommended direction**

rather than compressing each paragraph into:

> **Finding → classification → consequence → verification requirement.**

Technical precision remains mandatory, but excessive compression should be avoided where it makes the report sound clinical or difficult to read.

---

## 73.7 Report-layer application

### Executive Summary, Parts I–IX, Part X and Conclusion
Use the **V0.2 softer engineering narrative** as the default voice.

### Part XI — Recommended Action Programme
Retain a readable narrative introduction and explanation, but more formal implementation terminology is appropriate within action definitions, dependencies, acceptance criteria and close-out requirements.

### Appendices / verification registers / structural assessment requirements
Precise technical-control language is appropriate and should not be artificially softened where it is required for unambiguous implementation or traceability.

### Figure captions and evidence callouts
Remain concise, factual and evidence-disciplined. They should not carry unnecessary argumentative or verdict-like language.

# 74. V0.4 Language Harmonisation Rule

The first controlled revision after the V0.3 compiled-report review is a **report-wide language harmonisation pass**.

The V0.3 technical substance remains the baseline.

The V0.4 pass should:

1. preserve Phase 7 engineering substance, evidence boundaries, verification requirements and action logic;
2. restore the V0.2 explanatory narrative voice across reader-facing sections;
3. reduce unnecessary forensic / governance / close-out vocabulary in narrative sections;
4. retain stronger control terminology where it is functionally required in Part XI and technical appendices;
5. avoid combining this pass with detailed inspector wording corrections, evidence-selection revisions or final styling unless a local change is necessary to maintain meaning;
6. create a stable language baseline before the inspector undertakes the fine-tooth section-by-section technical/content revision.

The purpose is to prevent double handling: detailed wording should be revised **after** the report-wide voice is aligned.

---

# 75. Binding Interpretation of “Bring This Back to the V0.2 Softer Style”

When instructed to bring a section back to the V0.2 softer style, interpret the instruction as:

> **Preserve all established technical substance, evidence qualifications and recommendations, but rewrite the reader-facing language into a more natural, explanatory and measured engineering narrative. Reduce clinical, forensic and control-register vocabulary except where technically necessary. Let the evidence and reasoning lead the reader to the conclusion rather than presenting the conclusion as a verdict.**

This instruction must **not** be interpreted as weakening the engineering conclusion or obscuring established facts.
