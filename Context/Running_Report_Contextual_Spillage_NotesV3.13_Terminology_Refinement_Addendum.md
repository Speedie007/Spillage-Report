# 73. Governing Terminology Rule — Use Direct Physical Language Before “Envelope”

The report should not use **“envelope”** as a general-purpose substitute for the physical material path, process path, carrying area, chute route, screening/washing process or available installation space.

The preferred reader-facing approach is to describe the physical condition directly.

Examples include:

- **material-handling path** or **intended carrying path** for product movement on conveyors;
- **usable carrying area / carrying width** where the available belt-carrying space is the point being discussed;
- **process stream**, **process path** or **chute/process route** where material is intended to remain within a transfer or discharge arrangement;
- **screening/washing process** or **screening/chute process** where process water and entrained fines are being discussed;
- **installation space** where available physical room for equipment is being discussed;
- **containment arrangement** or **containment geometry** where the physical containment configuration is being discussed.

The term **“envelope” is retained only where it has a specific engineering purpose in describing a defined geometric or operating range**.

Appropriate retained examples include:

- **normal belt operating envelope**;
- **tracking envelope**;
- **verified operating envelope** where the phrase refers to measured or established belt movement / clearance range.

This distinction is intended to make the narrative more concrete and easier for a client reader to follow without removing useful engineering terminology where it is genuinely precise.

---

# 74. Reader-Facing Application Rule

When revising narrative text, apply the following test:

> **If the sentence is describing where product or process water is supposed to travel or remain, describe that physical path/process directly. If the sentence is describing a measurable geometric movement, clearance or operating boundary, “envelope” may remain.**

This rule applies throughout:

- Executive Summary;
- explanatory narrative sections;
- case-study interpretation;
- conclusion;
- reader-facing tables and callout boxes.

Technical implementation sections may retain “envelope” where it refers to an actual design or operating boundary.

---

# 75. Engineering Meaning Is Not to Be Weakened

This terminology refinement is a communication change only.

It must not:

- weaken established findings;
- remove evidentiary qualification;
- convert a measured geometric requirement into vague prose;
- change recommendation scope;
- change verification or close-out requirements;
- alter the distinction between observed fact, engineering interpretation and unresolved verification.

The V3.12 language-style rule remains binding:

> **Formal engineering narrative — technically rigorous, measured and explanatory, without sounding legalistic, forensic, bureaucratic or unnecessarily categorical.**

The V3.13 terminology rule works underneath that style rule by favouring direct physical wording over abstract terminology where possible.
