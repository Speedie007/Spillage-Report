# 45. Phase 5 Prototype Selection — CV09

CV09 has been selected as the first photographic evidence-module prototype.

The selection is intentional. CV09 is complex enough to test the report architecture but sufficiently contained to allow careful iteration without introducing the multiple unrelated structural sub-stories present at cases such as Screens or L1.

CV09 provides a useful prototype because it contains:

- still photographic evidence;
- dynamic video evidence;
- a visible material-loss consequence;
- a meaningful cleaner/chute geometry relationship;
- a clear containment-boundary issue;
- a strong connection to the report's wider fines-control philosophy;
- a working pilot recommendation that must remain clearly separated from final design.

The prototype is therefore expected to test both **technical evidence discipline** and **reader-facing narrative form**.

# 46. CV09 Controlled Engineering Context

## 46.1 Core finding

CV09 demonstrates that belt cleaning and containment of the removed material must be treated as one engineering function.

The existing cleaner removes wet residual material from the return belt, but the cleaner/discharge arrangement is positioned such that the removed material is not inherently retained within the effective discharge/process containment envelope. The result is that fines may be removed from the belt while still escaping into the surrounding plant environment.

The functional problem is therefore not simply inadequate belt cleaning. It is inadequate retention of the removed material within the intended process route.

The preferred governing phrase remains:

> **Do not merely clean the belt. Keep the fines inside the process.**

## 46.2 Directly supported evidence position

The accepted evidence supports that:

- wet fines/slurry are present below and around the CV09 cleaner/discharge region;
- the local cleaner/discharge arrangement is open/non-contained relative to the effective process containment boundary;
- active wet-material fallout occurred during operation;
- the cleaner/chute relationship allows removed material to escape into the surrounding plant area rather than remaining inherently captured within the intended downstream fines-handling route.

# 47. CV09 Prototype Narrative Intent

The CV09 module should not read as a sequence of isolated defect records.

It should guide the reader through the following logic:

1. **Observed condition** — substantial wet-fines/slurry contamination is present below the cleaner/discharge region.
2. **Local mechanism** — residual material is being removed from the belt but is not adequately retained within the effective discharge/process containment boundary.
3. **Operating confirmation** — video evidence shows active wet-material fallout during operation.
4. **Engineering significance** — a cleaner that removes material from the belt but allows that material to fall outside the intended process path has only performed part of the required control function.
5. **Control philosophy** — belt cleaning and containment of the removed fines must be integrated.
6. **Action** — CV09 should be used as a pilot/design-development location to develop and trial that functional arrangement.
7. **System application** — if the pilot is successful, the functional philosophy may be assessed for location-specific use at other suitable discharges; identical hardware is not to be prescribed universally.

The module must retain the report's accepted voice:

> **Explain first, prove second.**

The evidence should support the narrative rather than force the narrative into a photograph-by-photograph rhythm.

# 48. CV09 Working Engineering Assessment and Recommendation Context

## 48.1 Engineering assessment baseline

The intended controlled meaning is:

> CV09 demonstrates that belt cleaning and material containment cannot be treated as separate functions. The existing arrangement removes wet residual material from the belt, but removed material is not inherently retained within the effective discharge/process containment envelope. The inspection evidence includes active wet-material fallout during operation and substantial wet-fines/slurry accumulation below the cleaner region. The evidence therefore supports development of an integrated cleaner-and-containment arrangement that removes residual material while directing it back into the intended fines-handling route.

The assessment must retain the qualification that final cleaner type, contact pressure, chute dimensions, support details and maintenance-access geometry require design verification.

## 48.2 Recommendation baseline

The intended controlled recommendation is:

> Develop and trial a CV09 cleaner/containment arrangement that maintains effective belt-cleaning contact and directs removed wet fines back into the intended chute/process stream. Verify the existing belt/discharge geometry, available containment envelope, cleaner mounting, wear compensation, access, maintainability, safety and downstream fines route before finalising the design. Following successful trial and performance verification, assess the functional cleaner/containment philosophy for location-specific application at other suitable conveyor discharges.

The report must make clear that the recommendation is to standardise the **required function**, not identical hardware.

U4 remains an explicit example where materially different local geometry requires separate assessment.

# 49. Prototype Evidence and Layout Principles

The CV09 prototype is to establish the reusable evidence-module grammar for Phase 6.

The working target is a two-page module, with flexibility to extend to three pages if required for legibility or technical clarity.

The prototype should test:

- narrative-first introduction;
- hero/support image hierarchy;
- efficient multi-photo layout;
- canonical evidence IDs below every figure;
- representative frames from video evidence without inventing new inspection-evidence IDs;
- grouped story-level metadata rather than repetitive per-image metadata;
- concise Observation / Engineering Assessment / Control Philosophy / Recommended Action presentation;
- visible but readable qualifications;
- deliberate page-break control;
- formal engineering-report appearance without reverting to a repetitive defect-sheet format.

Where a representative frame is extracted from a controlled video, the caption must identify the frame as originating from that video and the evidence register must retain the original `.mp4` source.

Example:

> **CV09-REF-006 — representative frame from inspection video showing active wet-material fallout during operation.**

The prototype should use `CV09-REF-005` only if the wider context materially improves the story. S classification means the item is available to strengthen the narrative; it does not create an obligation to include it.
