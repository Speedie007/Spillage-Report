# 1. Audit Goals and Assessment Philosophy

The audit objectives remain to:

1. identify causes and contributing factors associated with combined-product spillage in the specified product-transport and processing areas;
2. identify the locations and operating conditions under which spillage occurs;
3. determine whether individual spillage areas are associated with one cause or several interacting factors; and
4. define practical remedial actions, engineering controls, maintenance requirements, operating procedures or policies that may eliminate, reduce or control the identified spillage.

The audit is a qualitative engineering/mechanism-focused assessment rather than a complete quantified mass-loss study.

Visible extent may indicate severity or persistence, but must not be converted into unsupported tonnage, mass flow, volume, design capacity or measured deviation.

The governing analytical sequence is:

> **Observed spillage → material/spillage type → immediate mechanism → contributing condition(s) → probable initiating cause where supportable → consequence → existing control → control deficiency → remedial direction.**

An important governing principle is:

> **The location at which spilled material is observed is not necessarily the location at which the initiating failure occurred.**

# 2. Core Terminology

## 2.1 Combined product

Lump ore and fines conveyed together as a single material stream.

## 2.2 Fines

The finer ore fraction, including very fine particulate material/ore dust.

## 2.3 Belt tracking

The lateral alignment of the conveyor belt along its intended travel path.

- Correct tracking: belt remains centred through the conveyor structure.
- Mistracking: belt moves laterally away from its intended centred position.
- Tracking deviation: measurable lateral displacement from the intended centreline.

## 2.4 Belt carryback

Residual fines/product remaining adhered to the belt after the main product has discharged and travelling onto the return side.

## 2.5 Belt scraper / belt cleaner

A mechanical device positioned at or near a conveyor discharge to remove material adhering to the belt and, where correctly configured, deliver that removed material into the intended discharge/containment route.

## 2.6 Wet fines / slurry

Fines containing sufficient moisture/process water to behave as wet particulate material or slurry-like material.

## 2.7 Side containment / side skirting

A local material-retention arrangement intended to help keep product within the usable carrying width. Final geometry and construction remain location-specific engineering matters.

# 10. Material Journey and Main Mechanism Families

Combined product already contains fines from blasting and gains additional fines through crushing and subsequent handling. It is conveyed through the underground crusher/UC system, other conveyor sections, surface screening/washing and loading/sampling areas.

Fines are not inherently a defect. The audit concern is where fines, lump ore, combined product or process water leave the path/process intended to contain and transport them.

## 10.1 Fines / carryback

Recurring return-side/downstream fines accumulation is the strongest repeated system pattern.

Carryback develops when residual material remains on the belt after discharge. It can progressively detach along the return path and be further dislodged by return rollers/idlers, producing deposits away from the initiating head-discharge/cleaner area.

Wet/sticky fines are recorded as more persistent than dry fines.

## 10.2 Coarse / mixed-product transit loss

Local coarse/mixed product is present outside the intended carrying path at W10, the Sample Conveyors and other selected positions.

Relevant contributors can include feed quantity/product size, loading trajectory, limited usable belt width, mistracking, transfer geometry and insufficient local side containment.

## 10.3 Tracking / alignment

Tracking affects containment because lateral belt displacement reduces the usable carrying margin and can move product toward/over an edge.

Causal strength remains location-specific.

## 10.4 Water / wet fines / slurry

Process/cleaning water can mobilise fines and move contamination away from the initiating source.

Screens provide direct dynamic proof of active water escape, while other wet/slurry locations do not necessarily share the same water source.

## 10.5 Event-driven spillage

Product removed after trips/blockages/restart preparation is a separate event-driven mechanism and should not automatically be classified as chronic mechanical spillage at the laydown location.

## 10.6 Housekeeping

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence. Source control should reduce the recurrence.**

# 12. Source-Recorded Cleaner / Maintenance Context

Questionnaire V2 records the following relevant contextual information:

- carryback recurs across multiple conveyor sections;
- it is normally first visible downstream of head/discharge areas and progressively drops from return belts;
- return rollers/idlers can dislodge and redistribute additional adhered fines;
- wet fines are more persistent;
- carryback worsens when cleaner condition deteriorates;
- an installed cleaner can still allow carryback where the wear element has reduced sufficiently to create a belt/blade gap;
- the current conventional inspected designs do **not** employ the proposed spring/self-tensioning mechanism;
- observed/identified deficiencies include missing blades, excessive wear, incomplete blade coverage, inadequate contact pressure, reliance on manual adjustment, damaged/displaced support hardware, location outside discharge containment and failure to direct removed material into the intended discharge route;
- both design/arrangement deficiencies and maintenance deficiencies are material contributors, with relative importance varying by location.

Source-recorded maintenance context:

- plant operation was described as a four-days-operating / four-days-maintenance cycle;
- maintenance ownership was understood to sit with designated section artisans and section supervisors;
- formal cleaner inspection standards, blade replacement criteria and maintenance records were not reviewed;
- routine contact-pressure/adjustment checking and prompt replacement of damaged/missing components were not supported by the questionnaire response;
- the mine-wide existing/common cleaner standard was unclear; the source response believed cleaner design may be left to the conveyor/OEM supplier.

These items are contextual, not a complete audit of South32 maintenance governance.

# 13. Governing Engineering Control Philosophy

Higher-order objective:

> **Keep combined product — fines and coarse/lump ore — within the intended material-handling/process route throughout transit, and retain process water within the process route where it is intended to remain, using controls appropriate to the specific mechanism and local geometry.**

Practical hierarchy:

1. establish actual loss mechanism;
2. verify relevant operating condition/geometry;
3. correct primary source/contributor;
4. provide required primary containment/process routing;
5. add secondary containment for genuine residual duty;
6. retain housekeeping/recovery as consequence control;
7. prove representative operating performance where functional success is claimed.

Fines-control principle:

> **Do not merely clean the belt. Keep the fines inside the process.**

Standardise the required function; do not automatically standardise identical hardware.

# 14. CV09 — SOURCE-RECONCILED PROPOSED SCRAPER / CHUTE SOLUTION BASELINE

This section is the controlling starting point for the current CV09 solution-gap-closure workstream.

## 14.1 What is directly established

- CV09 is a direct cleaner/containment example rather than only a general carryback inference.
- The existing scraper operates outside the effective discharge-chute containment arrangement.
- Wet material successfully removed from the belt can fall directly into the surrounding plant area.
- Residual carryback remains on the return belt after the existing cleaner.
- Downstream return rollers dislodge additional wet fines/carryback, spreading deposits beyond the immediate discharge.
- Dynamic evidence verifies active wet-material fallout during operation.

## 14.2 Why CV09 is the preferred pilot

CV09 is preferred because:

- the failure mechanism is directly observed;
- the existing cleaner/chute relationship can be related to the visible consequence;
- physical space was source-recorded as appearing available for a chute extension/revision;
- maintenance access was source-recorded as capable of being retained;
- the location provides a practical place to prove the required cleaner/support/tensioning/containment function before considering wider applicability.

## 14.3 Current intended functional solution

The proposed design intent is **not merely to fit a new scraper blade**.

The current conceptual functional requirements are:

1. locate the scraper at the most suitable position relative to the CV09 head/discharge geometry so that it can remove residual wet/sticky carryback effectively;
2. place the scraper/removal point within a revised/extended discharge-chute containment arrangement so scraped material cannot simply fall into the open plant area;
3. direct scraper-removed material into the **CV09 discharge chute**, where it joins the fines stream being conveyed/collected toward the fines stockpile;
4. use a spring-loaded/self-tensioning principle that maintains positive scraper-to-belt contact as the wear element reduces in thickness;
5. provide a positive mechanical wear/travel limiter so the tensioning mechanism cannot continue driving the scraper toward the belt once the wear element reaches its allowable limit;
6. retain safe access for inspection, adjustment, blade/wear-element replacement and maintenance;
7. evaluate whether a primary-plus-secondary cleaner arrangement is warranted for the actual wet/sticky service;
8. prove functional performance at CV09 before deciding whether the same functional principle should be assessed elsewhere.

The questionnaire did **not** identify additional drainage as a mandatory feature of the revised enclosure. Nevertheless, the OEM/detailed design should account for the actual wet-material behaviour confirmed during site verification/pilot development.

## 14.4 Important correction to later report shorthand

Later report wording sometimes describes cleaner-removed material generically as being “returned to the product/process stream.”

For CV09 the more accurate source-reconciled statement is:

> **Scraper-removed fines should be captured inside the revised CV09 cleaner/chute arrangement and delivered into the CV09 discharge chute so that they follow the existing fines-discharge route toward collection/stockpiling, instead of becoming plant-area spillage.**

The report should use that more physically accurate description when the CV09 concept is next revised.

## 14.5 Recommendation / OEM detailed-design boundary — BINDING CURRENT RULE

The audit/recommendation should define the **engineering function and conceptual implementation requirements**, including:

- why the scraper is required;
- where, in principle, it should be positioned relative to the head/discharge/chute arrangement;
- why that position is preferred;
- how the cleaner and chute containment must function together;
- the requirement for positive contact through wear;
- the conceptual self-tensioning principle;
- the requirement for a positive travel/wear limit;
- maintenance/access requirements;
- the required physical route for removed fines;
- whether primary/secondary cleaning should be assessed;
- functional performance/success criteria for the pilot.

The audit **should not prescribe fabrication-level mechanical specifications that properly belong to the OEM/detailed designer**.

The OEM/detailed designer should take the verified functional requirements and site geometry forward to determine, as applicable:

- exact scraper/cleaner make and type;
- blade/wear-element material, profile, width, segmentation and grade;
- final contact angle/working position;
- detailed spring/tensioning arrangement and spring rate/preload;
- cross-shaft, pivot, bearing/bush and support details;
- component material grades;
- member sizes/thicknesses;
- fasteners and connection details;
- wear limits and replacement criteria;
- final mechanical stop/travel-limiter dimensions;
- final chute/enclosure geometry and wear-liner details;
- guarding and safety details;
- structural adequacy/fabrication detailing;
- final installation/commissioning instructions.

The audit can therefore be **specific about required behaviour and preferred conceptual location without pretending to be the OEM fabrication design**.

## 14.6 Wider cleaner standardisation

If CV09 proves successful:

- survey each applicable head-discharge location individually;
- confirm cleaner presence/configuration, physical space, chute geometry, material condition and maintenance access;
- assess whether the same functional concept is physically applicable;
- do not represent similar cleaner-design defects as directly observed where access was not available;
- do not automatically apply the common concept to U4 because U4 uses a different/custom silo-discharge cleaner arrangement.

A successful CV09 pilot supports a structured applicability review, not blind one-for-one replication.

# 23. V4.1 Change-Control Rule

V4.1 is now the living engineering-development context.

For each accepted solution clarification:

1. update V4.1 first;
2. preserve unresolved items explicitly rather than filling gaps by assumption;
3. preserve evidence qualification boundaries;
4. identify which parts remain OEM/detailed-design responsibility;
5. revise the conceptual figure if the physical concept changes;
6. only then revise the report.

The immediate objective is to close the technical understanding of the CV09 concept — particularly **where the scraper should be positioned, why that location is preferred, how positive contact/self-tensioning should function conceptually, how over-travel should be prevented, and how the scraped wet fines should enter the CV09 discharge chute** — while deliberately leaving fabrication-level component specification to the OEM/detailed designer.
