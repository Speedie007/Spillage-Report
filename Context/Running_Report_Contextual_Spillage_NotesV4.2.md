# 1. Audit Goals and Assessment Philosophy

The audit objectives remain to:

1. identify causes and contributing factors associated with combined-product spillage in the specified product-transport and processing areas;
2. identify the locations and operating conditions under which spillage occurs;
3. determine whether individual spillage areas are associated with one cause or several interacting factors; and
4. define practical remedial actions, engineering controls, maintenance requirements, operating procedures or policies that may eliminate, reduce or control the identified spillage.

The audit is a qualitative engineering/mechanism-focused assessment rather than a complete quantified mass-loss study.

Visible extent may indicate severity or persistence, but must not be converted into unsupported tonnage, mass flow, volume, design capacity or measured deviation.

The governing analytical sequence is:

> **Observed spillage → material/spillage type → immediate mechanism → contributing condition(s) → probable initiating cause where supportable → consequence → existing control → control deficiency → remedial direction.**

An important governing principle is:

> **The location at which spilled material is observed is not necessarily the location at which the initiating failure occurred.**

# 2. Core Terminology

## 2.1 Combined product

Lump ore and fines conveyed together as a single material stream.

## 2.2 Fines

The finer ore fraction, including very fine particulate material/ore dust.

## 2.3 Belt tracking

The lateral alignment of the conveyor belt along its intended travel path.

- Correct tracking: belt remains centred through the conveyor structure.
- Mistracking: belt moves laterally away from its intended centred position.
- Tracking deviation: measurable lateral displacement from the intended centreline.

## 2.4 Belt carryback

Residual fines/product remaining adhered to the belt after the main product has discharged and travelling onto the return side.

## 2.5 Belt scraper / belt cleaner

A mechanical device positioned at or near a conveyor discharge to remove material adhering to the belt and, where correctly configured, keep that removed material within the intended discharge route.

## 2.6 Wet fines / slurry

Fines containing sufficient moisture/process water to behave as wet particulate material or slurry-like material.

## 2.7 Side containment / side skirting

A local material-retention arrangement intended to help keep product within the usable carrying width. Final geometry and construction remain location-specific engineering matters.

# 10. Material Journey and Main Mechanism Families

Combined product already contains fines from blasting and gains additional fines through crushing and subsequent handling. It is conveyed through the underground crusher/UC system, other conveyor sections, surface screening/washing and loading/sampling areas.

Fines are not inherently a defect. The audit concern is where fines, lump ore, combined product or process water leave the path/process intended to contain and transport them.

## 10.1 Fines / carryback

Recurring return-side/downstream fines accumulation is the strongest repeated system pattern.

Carryback develops when residual material remains adhered to the belt after the main product has discharged. It can progressively detach along the return path and be further dislodged by return rollers/idlers, producing deposits away from the initiating head-discharge/cleaner area.

Wet/sticky fines are recorded as more persistent than dry fines, but the cleaner-control philosophy applies to **both wet and dry residual fines**.

The preferred control is to remove residual fines before the belt leaves the head/discharge region and enters the return run.

## 10.2 Coarse / mixed-product transit loss

Local coarse/mixed product is present outside the intended carrying path at W10, the Sample Conveyors and other selected positions.

Relevant contributors can include feed quantity/product size, loading trajectory, limited usable belt width, mistracking, transfer geometry and insufficient local side containment.

## 10.3 Tracking / alignment

Tracking affects containment because lateral belt displacement reduces usable carrying margin and can move product toward/over an edge.

Causal strength remains location-specific.

## 10.4 Water / wet fines / slurry

Process/cleaning water can mobilise fines and move contamination away from the initiating source.

Screens provide direct dynamic proof of active water escape, while other wet/slurry locations do not necessarily share the same water source.

## 10.5 Event-driven spillage

Product removed after trips/blockages/restart preparation is a separate event-driven mechanism and should not automatically be classified as chronic mechanical spillage at the laydown location.

## 10.6 Housekeeping

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence. Source control should reduce the recurrence.**

# 14. CV09 — LOCKED FUNCTIONAL SCRAPER / CLEANER SOLUTION BASELINE

**Status:** ACCEPTED / LOCKED AT FUNCTIONAL-CONCEPT LEVEL — OEM DETAIL DESIGN AND SITE VERIFICATION REMAIN OPEN BY DESIGN

This section supersedes the earlier V4.1 CV09 working solution baseline and controls future CV09 report wording, conceptual figures and pilot verification.

## 14.1 Existing condition and loss mechanism

The recurring fines problem is not simply that fines exist on the conveyors. Residual fines can remain adhered to the belt after the main combined product has discharged, pass around the head pulley onto the return run, and then be progressively lost downstream. Return rollers/idlers can dislodge additional adhered material, explaining why the eventual deposit location may be remote from the initiating head-discharge/cleaner point.

At CV09 specifically:

- the cleaner is present and removes some material;
- the current cleaner region is outside/open to the effective head-chute discharge containment arrangement;
- successfully removed wet material can fall into the surrounding plant area;
- residual carryback still remains on the belt after the existing cleaner;
- downstream return rollers/idlers can dislodge further fines;
- photographs show wet-fines/slurry accumulation and extensive contamination below/around the cleaner region;
- video verifies active wet-material fallout during operation.

The proposed functional solution applies to **both wet and dry residual fines**. Wet/sticky fines make the CV09 failure particularly visible but do not define the full scope of the cleaner function.

## 14.2 Governing functional objective

> **Remove residual fines before the belt enters the return run, and remove them at a location where the material naturally remains inside the existing head-chute discharge flow.**

The solution must prevent two related loss paths:

1. material successfully removed by the cleaner must not fall outside the chute into the surrounding plant; and
2. residual material must not remain adhered to the belt and pass onto the return run where it can be progressively deposited downstream.

The design objective is therefore more specific than “improve belt cleaning.”

## 14.3 Preferred physical cleaning location — pulley-supported belt

The cleaner stages should act against the conveyor belt **while the belt remains positively supported by the head pulley**.

The mechanical reason is that the head pulley provides a stable reaction surface behind the belt. Scraper contact force is reacted through the belt into the pulley rather than simply allowing the belt to deflect away from the cleaner.

Governing principle:

> **Location provides mechanical stability; the tensioning system maintains contact as wear occurs.**

The exact angular position(s) remain for OEM/detailed design and site verification, but both stages should, as far as practicable, operate on pulley-supported belt in the head/discharge region.

## 14.4 Two-stage cleaner arrangement — preferred/default requirement

The preferred/default conceptual arrangement is **two cleaner stages**.

- **Stage 1 / primary cleaner:** removes the principal residual material remaining after the main combined-product discharge.
- **Stage 2 / secondary cleaner:** provides a second cleaning opportunity for residual material remaining after Stage 1, before the belt leaves the pulley-supported discharge region and enters the return run.

Two stages are preferred because they provide two opportunities to remove residual fines at the optimum mechanically supported and contained location. The concept does not assume that one cleaner can never function effectively.

Where verified geometry, access, guarding, structural arrangement or another legitimate design constraint prevents two stages from being installed:

> **At least one effective pulley-supported cleaner stage is the minimum functional arrangement.**

Hierarchy:

- **preferred/default = two stages**;
- **constrained minimum = one effective pulley-supported stage**.

This supersedes the earlier report/context language that merely said a primary-plus-secondary arrangement should be “considered” or “evaluated.”

## 14.5 Cleaner arrangement within the existing head chute

The head pulley and both cleaner stages are intended to be located **within the head/discharge chute housing**.

The proposed concept is not a separate fines-recovery chute and is not a one-sided/cantilevered scraper entering from one chute wall.

Each cleaner consists conceptually of:

- a full-width scraper blade spanning the effective belt width;
- a transverse cleaner support/cross-arm spanning across the chute;
- the transverse support extending through **both chute side walls** and protruding externally on both sides.

Conceptual arrangement:

> **external tensioner ← chute wall | transverse cleaner support + full-width blade | chute wall → external tensioner**

The blade/support assembly acts as one transverse cleaning unit.

## 14.6 Chute-wall slots / guided wear-compensation movement

Each end of the transverse cleaner support passes through a **guided slot/opening in the relevant chute side wall**.

The slot is not merely a penetration opening. It forms part of the required guided movement path for the complete scraper support/blade assembly as blade wear is taken up.

As the wear element reduces:

- the scraper support/blade assembly moves toward the belt along the permitted guided travel path;
- the blade orientation/function is maintained by the support/guidance arrangement;
- the concept is **not** a small blade pivoting on a one-sided cantilever arm.

The final guide arrangement, slot shape/size, wear protection, sealing, contamination control and support detail remain OEM/detailed-design matters.

## 14.7 External spring/self-tensioning function

Spring/tensioning hardware is intended to be positioned **outside the chute**, acting on the externally protruding ends of the transverse scraper support.

Required function:

> **Maintain positive, controlled scraper-to-belt contact as the scraper wear element progressively reduces.**

The tensioning arrangement should maintain sufficiently stable and reasonably uniform cleaner contact across the effective belt width.

The audit does not prescribe whether the two external sides are mechanically linked, independently adjusted, balanced or controlled by another OEM arrangement. The requirement is functional rather than fabrication-specific.

## 14.8 Positive travel / wear limiter

A positive mechanical stop/travel limiter is required to restrict the total movement available to the self-tensioning system.

Its function is to prevent continued cleaner advancement toward the belt once the scraper wear element reaches its allowable wear/travel limit.

Conceptual sequence:

> **new/serviceable wear element → tensioning maintains contact**
>
> **wear element reduces → support advances along guided travel path while contact is maintained**
>
> **maximum allowable travel reached → positive stop prevents further advancement → inspection/replacement required**

The OEM determines actual allowable wear, travel distance, contact loading and detailed stop design.

## 14.9 Material-flow philosophy — no separate scraper-fines recovery route

This is a critical correction to earlier report/context shorthand.

The concept does **not** require a separate fines chute, dedicated scraper-fines tray or special external recovery route merely to return removed fines to the process.

The head chute already surrounds the discharge/head-pulley region. The main combined product discharges from the belt into this chute and continues toward the receiving conveyor/existing downstream material-handling route.

Residual fines that remain adhered after the main discharge continue around the head pulley and encounter the cleaner stages **while still inside that same head-chute housing**.

Intended sequence:

> **main combined product discharges into head chute**
>
> **residual wet/dry fines remain adhered to belt**
>
> **Stage 1 removes principal residual material**
>
> **Stage 2 removes remaining residual fines**
>
> **material removed by both stages falls naturally inside the existing head chute**
>
> **removed fines remain with the existing combined-product discharge flow toward the receiving/next conveyor or existing downstream process**
>
> **cleaned belt leaves the head pulley and enters the return run**

Key principle:

> **Residual fines are removed inside the discharge chute, so gravity keeps them within the intended material flow automatically.**

### Superseded shorthand

Earlier source/context wording sometimes referred to scraper fines being directed to the “fines-discharge / fines-stockpile route.” That wording reflected the historical understanding of the downstream destination but can imply a separate scraper-fines transport system.

For the cleaner design itself, the accepted V4.2 physical concept is:

> **No separate recovery route is required at the cleaner. Scraper-removed fines remain inside the existing head-chute discharge flow.**

The actual downstream plant destination of that discharge remains whatever the existing material-handling route provides and does not need to be redesigned merely because the cleaner is improved.

## 14.10 Chute modification / enclosure principle

The earlier idea of “extending the chute around the current scraper” is no longer the governing design logic.

The preferred sequence is:

1. select the correct pulley-supported cleaner location;
2. place both cleaner stages within the existing head-chute/discharge zone;
3. modify/localise chute side-wall openings, enclosure, access, guarding or geometry only as required to accommodate that correct cleaner arrangement and retain the removed fines inside the existing discharge flow.

Any chute modification supports the correct cleaner location and maintainability; it is not intended to create a new fines-transport system around a poorly positioned existing cleaner.

## 14.11 CV06 / CV09 / CV15 — comparative cleaner-function lessons

The three inspected locations illustrate different ways in which the overall cleaner function can fail.

### CV06 — mechanical integrity / maintenance failure

Direct evidence shows:

- displaced/protruding cleaner support bush/insert;
- cleaner support beam/cross-shaft present;
- scraper blades absent across the working belt width.

Functional lesson:

> **A cleaner system must retain mechanical integrity, blade coverage and serviceability through operation and maintenance.**

CV06 does not independently prove that its cleaner is geometrically misplaced relative to the head pulley. Post-CV09 applicability review should check location/geometry rather than assume the same local defect.

### CV09 — location / containment / cleaning-performance failure

CV09 demonstrates the combined problem most clearly:

- cleaner present;
- removed material can escape outside the effective head-chute discharge path;
- residual carryback remains on the return belt;
- active wet-material fallout and major wet-fines accumulation are evidenced.

Functional lesson:

> **Remove the fines effectively, and remove them at a location where gravity automatically keeps them inside the discharge flow.**

### CV15 — cleaner-effectiveness concern / downstream consequence

Direct still evidence shows extensive distributed dry/dried fines/carryback around E-3181 and surrounding structure. The cleaner was source-recorded as observed/judged ineffective, but the still set does not independently prove its exact operating failure mechanism.

Functional lesson:

> **Cleaner success must be judged by actual downstream performance, not merely by the physical presence of a cleaner.**

Post-CV09 applicability review should check cleaner location, number of stages, blade contact, wear compensation, support condition, material route and maintainability at CV15 without pre-assigning the exact CV09 failure mechanism.

## 14.12 Wider cleaner standardisation

The intended wider recommendation is **not** to install an identical CV09 hardware package everywhere.

Instead:

> **Establish a proven functional cleaner standard at CV09, then assess each applicable conveyor against that required function and adapt the OEM design to the local geometry and operating conditions.**

Applicable head-cleaner installations should be assessed for:

- pulley-supported cleaning location;
- effective number of stages;
- blade coverage/contact;
- wear compensation/self-tensioning;
- positive travel limitation;
- mechanical integrity/component retention;
- material remaining inside the local discharge path;
- access/maintainability;
- representative downstream carryback performance.

Cleaner presence/configuration at inaccessible/unverified locations must not be represented as directly observed.

U4 remains an explicit geometry-specific exception because its cleaner/discharge arrangement is materially different from the conventional head-cleaner arrangement.

## 14.13 Maintainability / access

The final arrangement must retain safe, practical means to:

- inspect scraper condition/contact;
- assess spring/tensioning function;
- inspect travel-limit position;
- adjust/reset the cleaner where the OEM design requires it;
- replace blades/wear elements;
- service support/guide components;
- manage local build-up without dismantling disproportionate sections of the chute.

Specific access doors, guarding and maintenance procedures remain detailed-design/OEM/South32 matters.

## 14.14 OEM / detailed-design boundary — BINDING

The audit/recommendation should define:

- why the cleaner is required;
- preferred pulley-supported location and why that location is preferred;
- two-stage default / one-stage constrained minimum;
- full-width transverse cleaner arrangement;
- support through both chute side walls;
- guided wear-compensation movement;
- external self-tensioning function;
- positive mechanical travel limitation;
- stable/reasonably uniform contact across the effective belt width;
- cleaner installation within the head chute;
- natural retention of removed fines in the existing discharge flow;
- maintainability, inspection and safe access;
- required pilot verification/performance.

## 14.15 CV09 pilot verification intent

Before final detailed design/installation, verify:

- actual head-pulley/belt/chute geometry;
- available space for two pulley-supported cleaner stages;
- structural/support locations;
- cross-chute access and side-wall arrangement;

Final acceptance criteria remain to be reconciled into VER-001/VER-002 and Part XI after the conceptual figure is corrected and the responsible engineer/OEM has reviewed the functional concept.

## 14.16 Locked consolidated technical statement

> **The preferred CV09 cleaner concept is a two-stage transverse cleaner arrangement installed within the head/discharge chute, with both cleaner stages acting against the conveyor belt while it remains positively supported by the head pulley. Each full-width scraper blade is carried on a transverse support extending through guided openings in both chute side walls. External self-tensioning arrangements maintain controlled cleaner contact as the wear elements reduce, while positive mechanical travel stops limit the available wear-compensation movement. Material removed by either cleaner stage falls naturally within the existing head chute and remains with the combined-product discharge flow, preventing residual fines from being carried onto the return side of the belt. Where verified installation constraints prevent two cleaning stages, at least one effective pulley-supported stage is required. Final mechanical design and component specification remain the responsibility of the OEM/detailed designer.**
