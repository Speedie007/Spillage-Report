# V4.2 Housekeeping / Recovery / Reintroduction — Operational-Control Closure

## Status

**HOUSEKEEPING / RECOVERY FUNCTIONAL CONTROL LOCKED / AREA-SPECIFIC GOVERNANCE CONFIRMATION REMAINS OPEN BY DESIGN / FINAL REPORT-LEVEL SOLUTION GAP CLOSED**

This closure supplements the live V4.2 context and the previously accepted trip/restart operational-control closure.

It addresses housekeeping, recovery and reintroduction as a supporting operational control. It does not treat housekeeping as a substitute for correcting the initiating spillage, carryback, tracking, containment, water-loss or structural mechanism.

---

# 1. Locked source-recorded / inspector-clarified baseline

The current evidence/context supports the following controlled understanding:

- manual shovelling and wheelbarrow-based removal were reported to the inspector as methods used for accumulated product in some areas;
- the inspector did **not** directly witness those activities taking place during the audit;
- water wash-down has been source-recorded as a cleaning method in some areas, but the audit did not establish a universal plant-wide wash-down practice or universal receiving drainage route;
- the Screens building has a collection sump at its base intended to receive at least some local cleanup water and fines/slurry from that area;
- that Screens sump arrangement is **local to the Screens area** and must not be represented as a universal recovery/drainage arrangement across the wider surface plant;
- for recovered solid product, the inspector's understanding is that material should generally be returned to the conveyor/process where practicable during cleanup, but the normal approved destination/reintroduction route for every area was not established;
- the audit did not establish a single fixed housekeeping frequency applicable across all inspected areas;
- the observed overall condition suggests that some areas receive relatively regular cleaning while other areas appear to be cleared when accumulation becomes significant enough to require intervention.

These points are operational/contextual observations and source-recorded information, not a complete audit of South32 housekeeping procedures.

---

# 2. Governing control principle

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence; source control should reduce the recurrence.**

A cleaned area demonstrates that accumulated material has been removed. It does **not** by itself demonstrate that the mechanism causing the accumulation has been corrected.

Housekeeping therefore has three legitimate functions:

1. maintain safe access, walkways, escape routes and equipment surroundings;
2. recover and manage material that has already left the intended process path; and
3. provide practical feedback on whether source-control measures are reducing recurring accumulation.

---

# 3. Locked functional housekeeping / recovery sequence

## Step 1 — Define area responsibility and housekeeping trigger/frequency

South32 should confirm the applicable responsibility and housekeeping basis for each relevant operating area.

The audit does not prescribe one universal fixed interval because the inspected context indicates that housekeeping demand varies by location and mechanism.

The area control may therefore include, as appropriate:

- scheduled/routine inspection and clearing;
- condition-based clearing when accumulation reaches an established local trigger;
- event-driven clearing after trips/blockages or maintenance work; or
- a combination of these.

The applicable frequency/trigger and responsibility remain South32 operational decisions.

## Step 2 — Recover accumulated solid product using the approved local method

Accumulated solid product should be recovered using the method appropriate to the location, access and material condition.

Reported/manual methods may include shovelling and wheelbarrows, but the audit does not prescribe these as the universal method.

Where recovered material remains suitable for reintroduction, it should be returned to an **approved material-handling/process route** in accordance with the applicable South32 procedure.

The inspector's understanding that recovered product is generally intended to be returned to the belt/process is retained as contextual knowledge, but the exact approved destination for each area remains to be confirmed.

## Step 3 — Control water wash-down and the receiving route

Where water wash-down is used, the receiving route should be known and suitable for the resulting water/fines/slurry mixture.

The control objective is to avoid simply transferring contamination from the cleaned area to another uncontrolled location.

For the Screens building specifically, the known local collection sump may form part of the cleanup-water/fines recovery route, subject to confirmation of its actual operating function and capacity by South32.

This local sump must not be assumed to represent the drainage/recovery arrangement at other surface-plant areas.

Where the receiving route is not known, wash-down should not be treated in the report as a complete recovery/control solution merely because the original surface appears clean afterwards.

## Step 4 — Maintain access and escape routes

Housekeeping priority should preserve safe access to equipment, walkways and escape routes, particularly at locations where product accumulation can materially obstruct movement or inspection access.

Restricted areas such as W10 remain particularly sensitive to this consequence.

## Step 5 — Keep event-driven and chronic accumulation analytically separate

Material arising from trip/restart unloading, blockages or other discrete operating events should be identifiable as event-driven where practicable.

This prevents temporary/manual handling deposits from being confused with chronic mechanically generated spillage during later inspection or trending.

## Step 6 — Use recurring housekeeping demand as source-control feedback

For locations with material recurring often enough to justify tracking, South32 should retain sufficient housekeeping/condition information to answer:

- is the same area repeatedly accumulating material?;
- is the amount/frequency increasing, stable or reducing?;
- did a source-control intervention reduce the housekeeping demand?; and
- is the remaining accumulation event-driven, chronic, or a mixture of both?

The audit does not require a plant-wide quantitative mass-accounting system. Qualitative or client-selected quantitative trending may be used where appropriate.

## Step 7 — Close housekeeping separately from source-control closure

A housekeeping task can be closed when the area has been recovered/cleared to the applicable local standard.

A spillage/source-control item should only be closed when the initiating mechanism has also been corrected or otherwise formally dispositioned and, where applicable, verified under representative operation.

# 4. Reader-facing report position

Preferred reader-facing wording:

> **Housekeeping remains necessary to recover accumulated product and maintain safe access while engineering and operational source controls are implemented. Reported practices include manual removal and, in some areas, water wash-down; however, the audit did not establish one universal housekeeping frequency, recovery route or wash-down drainage arrangement across the plant. The recommended response is therefore to confirm area-specific responsibility, clearing frequency or condition trigger, recovery/reintroduction route and any wash-down receiving route. Recurring housekeeping demand should then be used as supporting feedback on whether the relevant source-control measures are reducing accumulation.**

For the Screens area, a more specific note may be used where relevant:

> **A local collection sump is present at the base of the Screens building and may form part of the local cleanup-water/fines recovery arrangement. Its role should be confirmed as part of the area housekeeping/water-control procedure and should not be generalised to other plant areas.**

---

# 5. Binding limitations

The report must not state or imply that:

- manual shovelling/wheelbarrow cleanup was directly witnessed during the audit;
- one fixed housekeeping schedule applies across all areas;
- all recovered material is always returned to the same conveyor or destination;
- all wash-down water/fines report to the Screens sump or an equivalent sump;
- washing an area clean proves the initiating spillage source has been corrected; or
- housekeeping can replace the engineering/source-control actions developed elsewhere in the report.

---

# 6. Verification / governance relationship

The current VER-013 housekeeping/recovery governance concept remains appropriate, with later V0.6/Stage-B reconciliation to reflect this closure explicitly.

Area-level verification should confirm, as applicable:

- responsible owner/team;
- scheduled frequency and/or condition-based trigger;
- approved solid-product recovery/reintroduction route;
- wash-down receiving route where wash-down is used;
- access/escape-route standard;
- method of identifying event-driven deposits where material;
- recurring-accumulation record/trending where useful; and
- linkage between housekeeping demand and the applicable source-control action.

These are South32 operational-governance confirmation items rather than unresolved audit-level solution concepts.

---

# 7. Closure boundary

The following are now **CLOSED at audit/report functional-concept level**:

- housekeeping as consequence control rather than source-control substitution;
- reported manual recovery methods treated as source-recorded rather than directly witnessed;
- area-specific rather than universal housekeeping frequency/control;
- recovery/reintroduction to an approved process route where appropriate;
- wash-down receiving-route verification before wash-down is treated as complete recovery;
- Screens sump treated as a local arrangement only;
- access/escape-route preservation;
- distinction between event-driven and chronic accumulation; and
- use of recurring housekeeping demand as feedback on source-control performance.

The following remain **OPEN BY DESIGN**:

- South32's formal area responsibilities;
- exact scheduled frequencies/condition triggers;
- approved recovery/reintroduction route for each area;
- wash-down procedure and drainage/recovery route outside locations where it is specifically verified;
- Screens sump operating details/capacity where relevant;
- recordkeeping format; and
- final operational acceptance criteria.

These are site-governance and implementation details, not unresolved report-level engineering gaps.
