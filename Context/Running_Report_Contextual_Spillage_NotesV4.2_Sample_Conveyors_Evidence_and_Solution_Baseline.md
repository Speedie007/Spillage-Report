# 1. Governing engineering problem

The core Sample Conveyor problem is the interaction between:

1. **the quantity and distribution of product being fed into the Sample Conveyor system;**
2. **the size of the transported product relative to the narrow conveyor-belt carrying width;**
3. **the lateral position/tracking of the belt and therefore the usable carrying margin available at each side;** and
4. **the resulting ability of the conveyor to retain coarse/mixed product within the intended carrying path.**

The photographic evidence supports these physical relationships and the resulting containment loss. It does **not** establish live mass-flow rate, rated conveyor-capacity exceedance, a measured percentage of belt offset, or one universal initiating cause.

For reader-facing/report use, distinguish:

- **rated/design throughput capacity** — not established by the still photographs; from
- **geometric carrying/containment condition at the captured state** — directly supported by the photographs showing coarse product occupying a substantial proportion of the narrow belt and product outside the intended carrying path.

The report should therefore avoid saying simply that a Sample Conveyor is "overloaded" unless later quantitative verification supports that conclusion.

Preferred engineering framing:

> **The observed quantity, distribution and size of the conveyed product appear capable of challenging the geometric carrying/containment capacity available on the narrow Sample Conveyor belt, particularly where lateral belt offset reduces the usable carrying margin.**

---

# 2. Evidence interpretation principle

The Sample Conveyor body photographs are not to function as decorative examples of spillage. They are to be used as an **evidence-led reasoning sequence**:

> **Explain → Prove → Interpret → Act**

Each selected image should have a deliberate role in showing one or more of the following relationships:

- observed loading / geometric carrying condition;
- product size versus belt width;
- tracking / usable carrying margin;
- actual containment loss;
- accumulated consequence; and
- physical geometry relevant to the corrective arrangement.

Photographs may demonstrate the coexistence of several conditions without proving the chronological order in which those conditions developed.

# 3. Sample Conveyor 2 — revised evidence baseline

## 3.1 Major correction from the earlier Batch-B interpretation

The earlier Batch-B position that Sample Conveyor 2 mistracking was not visually established is superseded by the focused review.

The complementary `LoadoutStationSample2-REF-004` and `LoadoutStationSample2-REF-005` photographs show the same local area from different perspectives. A substantial portion of a roller remains visibly exposed rather than being covered by the belt, supporting a laterally displaced belt condition at the captured state.

The current evidence-controlled statement is therefore:

> **A lateral belt-offset condition is visually supported at the captured Sample Conveyor 2 condition by the complementary REF-004 / REF-005 image pair.**

This does not establish:

- offset magnitude;
- whether the condition is continuous, intermittent or load-dependent;
- whether it should be described as persistent dynamic mistracking during operation;
- the initiating mechanical cause; or
- the relative contribution of tracking, feed quantity, product size, product congestion or another mechanism.

## 3.2 REF-004 + REF-001 — opening visual hook

The inspector identified a deliberate reader-facing use for these two images:

> **REF-004 makes the reader ask “what is happening at this conveyor?”** by showing the narrow-belt/product-size relationship, displaced material and visible belt-offset context.
>
> **REF-001 immediately establishes “the consequence is substantial”** by showing extensive accumulated product around the installation.

This pairing is cause/effect-oriented at the level of reader understanding but does not claim that REF-001 proves which single mechanism produced the accumulation.

## 3.3 REF-004 + REF-005 — complementary product-size / belt-offset pair

### REF-004

The image supports several simultaneous relationships:

- coarse manganese lumps are large relative to the narrow belt width;
- smaller pieces remain on the belt while larger pieces are visible outside the intended carrying path;
- the belt is laterally displaced relative to the roller arrangement;
- the reduced usable carrying margin is relevant to the observed containment difficulty.

### REF-005

REF-005 shows the same local condition from another perspective and more clearly exposes the roller/belt-position relationship.

This complementary role materially increases REF-005's narrative value. Its controlled inclusion changes from **S to E**.

## 3.4 REF-006 — entry-guide congestion and interacting mechanisms

The orange covers in REF-006 are the Sample Conveyor entry-zone covers/guides associated with directing product from the preceding feeder/chute toward the belt carrying path.

REF-006 shows the entry area heavily congested with displaced product, with accumulated material occupying much of the space beneath/around the covers.

The image is important because it shows the physical zone in which **feed quantity/distribution, product size, belt position and accumulated displaced product interact**.

### Permissible engineering hypotheses

The photographed end condition is compatible with one or more interacting mechanisms, including:

1. lateral belt offset reducing usable carrying margin and allowing product to leave the belt;
2. feed quantity at times exceeding what can be geometrically contained for the actual product-size and belt-position condition;
3. larger lumps being more susceptible to displacement/escape than smaller fragments on the narrow belt;
4. escaped large pieces accumulating and compacting within the constrained entry zone;
5. sufficiently severe build-up potentially interfering with belt freedom/position and aggravating an existing offset; and
6. a possible adverse feedback interaction in which containment loss, congestion and belt offset reinforce one another.

### Evidence qualification

These sequences were **not witnessed dynamically** and are not to be reported as proven chronological events.

The correct conclusion is:

> **The observed end condition is compatible with several interacting feed / product-size / belt-position / containment mechanisms. The evidence is sufficient to require those relationships to be tested, but not to prescribe one unverified initiating cause.**

## 3.5 Sample 2 controlled image sequence

Preferred body sequence:

1. **REF-004 + REF-001** — abnormal carrying condition + substantial consequence;
2. **REF-005** — clarifies exposed-roller / lateral belt-position relationship;
3. **REF-007** — reinforces observed loading / product-size / limited carrying geometry;
4. **REF-006** — congested entry/guide zone and containment-failure end condition.

`REF-002` and `REF-003` remain A. `REF-008` is downgraded from S to **A** because it adds little unique value to the core reasoning chain.

# 6. Diagnostic corrective-action sequence — binding baseline

The evidence does not justify jumping directly to one hardware modification.

For both Sample Conveyors, the preferred sequence is:

1. **Establish representative feed quantity and variation** — including whether surge/periodic high loading occurs.
2. **Establish product-size distribution** relative to the narrow belt and usable carrying geometry.
3. **Quantify lateral belt offset/tracking** under representative loading.
4. **Identify and correct confirmed tracking/alignment/interference causes**, including head/tail/roller/tension/alignment conditions where applicable.
5. **Inspect and clear/assess displaced-product congestion** and determine whether accumulated product is interfering with belt freedom or position.
6. **Determine whether feed quantity, product size or loading distribution requires control/restriction** after the mechanical condition is understood.
7. **Reassess the conveyor under representative operating conditions** after primary corrections.
8. **Establish the residual containment duty.**
9. **Where required, install/adapt a belt-compatible side-skirt / spill-seal arrangement** based on the W10 functional principle but tailored to Sample Conveyor geometry.
10. **Verify performance under representative operation** and adjust the final system as required.

This sequence intentionally permits different final outcomes. The issue may require a combination of tracking correction, feed/product-size control and side skirting, or correction of the dominant primary condition may materially reduce the required secondary-containment duty.

# 7. Relationship to W10 side-skirt solution

The W10 solution provides a transferable **functional principle**, not an identical hardware prescription:

> **Where residual lateral product must be retained on a troughed conveyor, the active containment element should act at the belt carrying edge as a belt-compatible flexible skirt/spill seal rather than after material has already escaped.**

For Sample Conveyors 2 and 3, final skirt arrangement remains dependent on:

- actual usable carrying width;
- corrected belt tracking range;
- feed/loading distribution;
- product-size range;
- local cover/guide geometry;
- risk of product trapping;
- practical maintenance/removal/clean-out access; and
- selected OEM skirt/seal design.

Historical preliminary dimensional concepts, including approximate skirt height or belt-clearance values, remain **non-binding historical design-development ideas** and are not to be restored to the report without verification.

# 8. V0.6 integration requirement

The Sample Conveyor sections in V0.6 should use the photographs to lead the reader through the engineering reasoning rather than merely illustrate spillage.

The body should make clear:

- what each selected photograph proves;
- what relationship is inferred from multiple photographs;
- what remains unverified;
- why those uncertainties determine the investigation/action sequence; and
- why side skirting is a residual containment control rather than a substitute for resolving feed/loading or tracking contributors.

The final language should preserve the established measured style:

> **The evidence indicates…**
>
> **Taken together, these conditions suggest…**
>
> **The available evidence does not establish which condition initiated the event…**
>
> **Further measurement is therefore required to determine…**
>
> **The recommended response is therefore to verify and correct the primary contributors before finalising the secondary containment arrangement.**
