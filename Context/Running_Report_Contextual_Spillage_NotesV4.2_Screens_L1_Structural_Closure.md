# 1. Governing structural principle

For both Screens and L1, the audit has sufficient evidence to require competent structural follow-up, but not to determine structural capacity from photographs.

The report-level hierarchy is:

> **identify the observed condition → define the structural concern → quantify the actual condition/load path → determine remaining adequacy through competent assessment → define and implement the approved structural disposition → formally accept the as-left condition.**

Photographic severity is not a substitute for calculation, measurement or structural acceptance.

## 2.1 Screens cut-out history — internal context only

The inspector has **no direct information or record** establishing why the members were cut.

From prior industrial/site experience, the inspector notes only as an **unverified background possibility** that structural openings are sometimes introduced for convenience during equipment installation/removal or to accommodate replacement equipment with different dimensions.

This possibility is not evidence of what occurred at the Screens installation and must **not** be written into the reader-facing report as the cause, history or justification for the cut-outs unless drawings, modification records, site records or other reliable evidence later establish it.

The report should therefore retain:

> **The reason and modification history of the cut-outs could not be established from the available inspection evidence.**

## 2.2 Screens functional structural action sequence

1. identify each affected member and confirm its structural role/load-path function from drawings, site inspection and available records;
2. document the as-found member dimensions, cut/notch/opening geometry, corrosion/section loss, connections and any deformation/cracking/deterioration relevant to assessment;
3. review available modification, maintenance and equipment-replacement records where practicable to establish whether the alterations were authorised and what equipment/history may be associated with them;
4. complete a competent structural assessment of the current load path and remaining adequacy/capacity;
5. allow the competent structural authority to define any immediate/interim controls that are required before permanent disposition;
6. develop the approved repair, strengthening, replacement, reinstatement or other structural response based on the assessment rather than on photographic judgement;
7. implement the approved work under the applicable engineering/quality controls; and
8. complete final inspection and formal structural acceptance of the as-left arrangement.

Knowing why a member was cut does not by itself establish that the remaining structure is adequate; structural assessment remains required.

## 3.2 L1 historical belt-contact boundary

The inspector confirms that the wear slots appeared historical and that the belt, when examined during the latest operating condition, showed no visible offset/mistracking and was running clear of the affected supports.

Binding interpretation:

> **The wear slots support previous/intermittent belt-to-structure contact. The latest inspected operating condition did not show ongoing contact or visible mistracking. The earlier dynamic tracking condition or other cause that produced the wear was not witnessed and remains unverified.**

Accordingly, the report should not state that the belt is currently continuously rubbing the structure or that current mistracking is established.

Representative operating verification remains appropriate because one satisfactory observed condition does not prove that contact cannot recur under other loading/tracking states.

## 3.3 L1 functional action sequence

### Structural workstream

1. complete a competent structural inspection of the affected concrete, reinforcement, steel members, supports and connections;
2. measure/document concrete loss, reinforcement exposure/condition, steel section loss/perforation, relevant member dimensions, connections and repair-design inputs;
3. determine remaining structural adequacy/capacity and any required interim controls through the competent structural authority;
4. define the approved structural disposition, which may include concrete rehabilitation, reinforcement treatment/reinstatement, steel repair/strengthening/replacement or another engineered response as established by the assessment;
5. implement the approved work and obtain formal post-repair structural acceptance.

### Mechanical / belt-clearance workstream

6. record the existing wear-slot/contact geometry and relevant belt-to-structure clearance;
7. observe belt tracking/clearance under representative operating conditions sufficient to determine whether intermittent contact can still occur;
8. if a current tracking/alignment/clearance defect is identified, correct the verified mechanical contributor; if no current defect is demonstrated, retain the historical-contact qualification rather than inventing one;
9. verify the acceptable as-left belt-to-structure clearance/operating condition to the responsible engineering standard.

### Environmental / recurrence-control workstream

10. reduce recurring product/fines/moisture accumulation around repaired structural interfaces through the applicable source-control and housekeeping measures so repaired areas are not simply returned to the same accumulation environment; and
11. include the local post-intervention structural/accumulation condition in close-out monitoring where appropriate.
