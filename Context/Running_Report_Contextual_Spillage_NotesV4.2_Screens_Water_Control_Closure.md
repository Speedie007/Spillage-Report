# 1. Locked evidence interpretation

The Screens evidence directly proves that process water escapes the screen/chute containment during operation.

The principal dynamic evidence is:

> **Screen-REF-016 — direct proof of active water escape during operation.**

> **Screen-REF-017 — strongest evidence for tracing/localising the external point of water emergence/escape on the operating screen assembly.**

The inspector could not safely access the internal operating section while the screen was running. This is an appropriate inspection limitation rather than an evidence deficiency that should be filled by assumption.

Binding distinction:

> **The external escape point can be localised from REF-017, but the internal component, defect or mechanism producing that escape cannot be established safely from the operating external view alone.**

Therefore the current evidence does not justify naming one unverified internal cause such as a failed seal, incorrectly directed spray, joint opening, drainage restriction, overflow or chute defect.

---

# 2. Governing engineering question

The water-control investigation is to answer:

> **Where is the process water intended to travel, and what physical internal condition is allowing it to leave that intended screening/washing/chute process path at the external escape location identified during operation?**

The subsequent corrective action should be based on the verified internal mechanism rather than on a generic downstream catchment or an assumed chute modification.

---

# 3. Locked functional action sequence

## Step 1 — Use the operating evidence to mark/localise the external escape point

Use REF-016 and particularly REF-017, together with site observation, to identify the external region from which the water is emerging during operation.

Where practical, physically reference/mark the corresponding external location for follow-up during shutdown.

## Step 2 — Perform planned safe shutdown/isolation and inspect the corresponding internal area

Once the screen is stopped, isolated and safe access is established, inspect the internal area corresponding to the external escape point.

The objective is to identify the first internal location at which water leaves the intended process path.

## Step 3 — Establish the actual internal mechanism

Inspect for the verified local condition, which may include, as applicable:

- damaged, missing or ineffective sealing;
- an opening/gap/joint condition;
- spray-water direction or spray-component condition;
- local chute/screen containment geometry;
- overflow or drainage/discharge restriction;
- damaged/altered containment;
- local wear/deterioration; or
- another site-established mechanism.

These remain inspection targets until verified. They are not current report conclusions.

## Step 4 — Correct the primary water-containment defect

Repair, restore or reconfigure the verified failed condition so that process water remains within the intended screening/washing/chute process path.

The audit does not prescribe a generic chute extension, extra drain or seal until the actual mechanism is known.

## Step 5 — Check the intended process-water route and drainage/discharge function

Confirm that water can follow its intended route without being forced toward external openings or surrounding structure under the representative operating condition.

Where a drainage or discharge limitation is found, it should be corrected as part of the verified mechanism rather than assumed in advance.

## Step 6 — Recommission and verify under representative operation

After corrective work, operate the screen under representative conditions and verify that uncontrolled spray/runoff into surrounding plant areas is eliminated or materially reduced to an acceptable contained condition.

The verification should specifically re-check the previously identified external escape location.

## Step 7 — Inspect the repeatedly wetted surrounding area separately

Following control of the water escape, inspect adjacent steelwork, support interfaces, fixings and other repeatedly wetted components for deterioration requiring maintenance or structural follow-up.

This does not merge the water-control action with the separate structural-capacity assessment.

# 4. Corrosion relationship — binding qualification

The evidence shows both:

- active water escape/repeated wetting of the screen-support environment; and
- severe corrosion/deterioration in that environment.

The report may therefore state that controlling the active water escape is important to reduce continued wetting exposure of surrounding steelwork.

However:

> **The available evidence does not establish that the observed screen-water escape is the sole cause of all existing corrosion or deterioration.**

Historical exposure, other moisture sources, material retention and maintenance condition may also contribute and are not quantitatively separated by this audit.

Preferred corrosion evidence grouping:

- REF-002 + REF-003 — principal close/detail + wider-context pair;
