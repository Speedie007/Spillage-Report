# 1. Clarified site understanding

The inspector does **not** have direct knowledge of the mine's formal internal trip/restart procedure, approved work instruction, responsibility allocation or detailed best-practice controls.

The known operating context is limited to the following:

- trips, blockages or related operating events can occur during normal plant operation;
- this is a **possible/intermittent operating event**, not a condition that is established as occurring regularly;
- where a conveyor remains heavily loaded after such an event and repeated restart attempts are hindered by the retained product load, some product may be manually removed from the belt to reduce the restart load and facilitate restart; and
- to the best of the inspector's knowledge, product removed for this purpose is subsequently required to be manually returned to the conveyor belt during cleanup/recovery activities.

This source-recorded understanding is not treated as a substitute for the applicable South32 procedure.

---

# 2. Governing evidence / classification boundary

Product deliberately removed from a conveyor as part of a trip/restart recovery activity is an **event-driven handling condition**.

It must not automatically be classified as evidence that the conveyor chronically spills at the temporary placement location.

Binding distinction:

> **Event-driven product removal may create a temporary deposit as part of restart recovery. Chronic mechanical spillage is material that leaves the intended carrying/process path because of an ongoing or recurring loss mechanism. The two conditions should be distinguished where the operating history allows.**

The audit does not establish the frequency of trip-related off-loading, the quantity normally removed or the exact locations used for temporary placement.

---

# 3. Locked report-level operational objective

> **Where product has to be removed from a loaded conveyor to facilitate safe and reliable restart after a trip or blockage, the removed product should remain under controlled temporary handling and should be recovered/reintroduced to the approved process route rather than becoming an unmanaged secondary deposit.**

This is an operational-control requirement. It is not a mechanical-spillage remedy and does not replace correction of any underlying cause of a trip, blockage or chronic spillage condition.

---

# 4. Locked functional control sequence

## Step 1 — Confirm the applicable South32 procedure

South32 should identify the current written procedure, work instruction or accepted operating practice governing conveyor unloading/clearing after trips, blockages or restart difficulties.

The audit does not invent that procedure.

## Step 2 — Confirm when manual product removal is permitted/required

The applicable procedure should define the circumstances in which retained product is to be removed from a conveyor to facilitate restart, together with the required isolation, access and operating controls.

The current audit only records that manual off-loading may be used where the retained belt load hinders restart; it does not define the mine's authorised threshold or decision authority.

## Step 3 — Define authorised responsibility

The procedure should identify who is authorised/responsible to:

- direct the clearing activity;
- remove product;
- determine/approve temporary placement;
- confirm readiness for restart; and
- ensure subsequent recovery/reintroduction is completed.

The audit does not assign these roles because they were not verified.

## Step 4 — Control temporary placement

Where product has to be removed before restart, temporary placement should be deliberate and controlled so that it does not:

- obstruct access/escape routes;
- create an unmanaged accumulation around operating equipment;
- become confused with chronic mechanical spillage; or
- remain indefinitely without a defined recovery responsibility.

The exact containment/laydown method remains a South32 operational decision based on the local area and applicable safety requirements.

## Step 5 — Recover and reintroduce the removed product

The source-recorded understanding is that removed product is manually returned to the belt during cleanup/recovery.

The applicable South32 procedure should confirm the approved reintroduction method, location and timing so that material is returned safely without creating a new overloading, trapping or spillage condition.

## Step 6 — Restore the temporary area

After recovery, the temporary placement area should be cleared sufficiently to restore safe access and to avoid leaving an event-generated deposit that is later mistaken for ongoing conveyor spillage.

## Step 7 — Preserve event-versus-chronic distinction

Where material accumulation is reviewed for repeat-spillage trending, trip/restart deposits should be identifiable as event-driven where the history is known.

Repeated trip-related off-loading at the same asset may still be operationally important, but it should not be presented as proof of a chronic mechanical loss mechanism without separate evidence.

---

# 5. Reader-facing wording

Preferred report framing:

> **Product may occasionally need to be removed from a loaded conveyor following a trip or blockage where the retained load hinders restart. This is an event-driven operating condition rather than, by itself, evidence of chronic mechanical spillage. The applicable South32 procedure should therefore define authorised unloading, controlled temporary placement and subsequent recovery/reintroduction of the removed product so that restart activities do not create unmanaged secondary deposits.**

Where the recovery practice is discussed, retain the qualification:

> **The inspection context indicates that removed product is expected to be returned to the conveyor during cleanup; however, the formal procedure, responsibilities and approved reintroduction method were not reviewed as part of this audit.**

Avoid statements that:

- trip-related manual unloading occurs routinely or at a known frequency;
- the retained product exceeds conveyor design capacity;
- a specific role/person is responsible unless verified from South32 procedure;
- a particular temporary laydown location is approved unless verified; or
- the audit has reviewed or approved the mine's restart procedure.

---

# 6. Closure boundary

The following are now **CLOSED at audit/report functional-concept level**:

- trip/restart product removal is treated as a possible intermittent event-driven condition;
- manual off-loading may be used where retained belt load hinders restart;
- removed product should remain controlled and be recovered/reintroduced rather than left as unmanaged secondary accumulation;
- the known recovery understanding is manual return to the conveyor during cleanup;
- event-driven deposits must be distinguished from chronic mechanically generated spillage; and
- the report should require confirmation/formalisation of the applicable South32 control rather than invent one.

The following remain **OPEN BY DESIGN / TO BE CONFIRMED BY SOUTH32**:

- the applicable written procedure/work instruction;
- authorised responsibilities;
- isolation/access/restart decision controls;
- approved temporary placement/containment arrangements;
- approved recovery/reintroduction method and timing;
- any required event recordkeeping; and
- whether repeat trip/off-loading events at a particular asset warrant separate operational/root-cause investigation.

These are site operational-governance matters rather than unresolved audit-level concept gaps.
