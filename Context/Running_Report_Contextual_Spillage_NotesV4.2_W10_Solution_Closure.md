# 1. As-Found W10 Context

W10 remains characterised by:

- substantial dry coarse/mixed product and fines outside the intended conveyor carrying path;
- a restricted tunnel/access environment that increases the consequence and housekeeping difficulty associated with spillage;
- established lateral product loss from the conveyor;
- no single universal initiating cause established from the present evidence set.

The restricted access route is therefore relevant to the **consequence and significance of the observed spillage**, but it does not define the functional purpose of the proposed conveyor-side control.

# 3. Governing Functional Objective

> **Keep coarse/mixed product on the conveyor belt by providing side skirting / spill sealing along the conveyor sides within the carrying width.**

The side-skirt / spill-seal system is intended to create a local near-vertical product-containment boundary at each side of the troughed carrying surface so laterally displaced material is retained within the intended carrying path rather than escaping over the belt edge.

The function is therefore **product retention on the belt**, not interception after the product has already left the conveyor.

---

# 4. Physical Side-Skirting / Spill-Seal Principle

The accepted concept is consistent with conventional conveyor skirting / spill-seal practice at functional level:

- a flexible/soft, durable wear/sealing element is installed along each side of the conveyor where required;
- rubber or another suitable belt-friendly wear compound is a typical material family, but the report does not prescribe the final compound;
- the skirt/seal is supported or clamped from the conveyor/skirt structure;
- the active sealing/containment element descends on the **inside of the conveyor carrying width**;
- it runs longitudinally along the conveyor side rather than forming a remote freestanding barrier;
- its lower region follows the troughed belt edge closely and may operate in controlled belt-friendly contact or controlled close clearance depending on the selected OEM system;
- the arrangement creates a near-vertical containment surface substantially perpendicular to lateral product movement;
- the skirt/seal helps retain laterally displaced coarse/mixed product within the belt carrying area.

This is the key physical distinction from the superseded external-barrier concept.

---

# 5. Relationship to Primary Causes / Contributors

The side-skirt / spill-seal arrangement is a direct containment control, but it does not eliminate the need to investigate and correct primary contributing conditions where present.

W10 should still be assessed for relevant:

- loading / feed distribution;
- belt tracking;
- head/tail and conveyor geometry;
- usable carrying width;
- local transfer or product-trajectory conditions;
- structural/support conditions affecting belt behaviour.

Governing hierarchy:

> **Investigate and correct primary contributors where present → provide effective side skirting / spill sealing to retain residual lateral product within the carrying path → verify performance under representative operation.**

The report should not imply that skirting alone corrects severe mistracking, poor loading or other root mechanical conditions.

---

# 6. Belt Relationship / Wear-Control Principle

The final side-skirt arrangement must be compatible with normal troughed-belt movement and tracking.

Required functional outcomes include:

- sufficiently close sealing/containment to prevent unacceptable lateral product escape;
- no rigid external element acting as a belt guide;
- no uncontrolled rubbing condition that damages the belt;
- no geometry that traps coarse product between the skirt/seal and belt in a manner likely to cause gouging, jamming or accelerated wear;
- practical adjustment/replacement as the skirt/seal wears.

The exact contact/clearance philosophy remains an OEM/detailed-design matter because different commercial skirt/seal systems use different controlled-contact, floating, self-adjusting or close-clearance arrangements.

---

# 7. Maintainability / Access

The final arrangement should retain practical means to:

- inspect skirt/seal condition;
- inspect belt-edge/tracking behaviour;
- adjust the skirt/seal where required by the selected system;
- replace worn skirt/seal elements;
- clean trapped/local build-up where it occurs;
- access clamps/supports safely;
- avoid creating inaccessible accumulation zones.

The W10 tunnel remains relevant here because local access/working space is constrained.

---

# 8. OEM / Detailed-Design Boundary — BINDING

The audit/report should define:

- the purpose of retaining product on the belt;
- side skirting/spill sealing on the conveyor sides where required;
- installation on the inside of the carrying width;
- belt-friendly flexible containment/sealing behaviour;
- compatibility with normal belt tracking/troughing movement;
- maintainability and inspection requirements;
- operating verification intent.

The audit should **not** invent fabrication-level skirt design.

The OEM/detailed designer should determine, as applicable:

- exact skirt/seal product/technology;
- rubber/polymer/other material specification and hardness;
- skirt/seal profile and thickness;
- longitudinal extent and height;
- clamp/support system;
- adjustment method;
- contact force or controlled-clearance setting;
- wear allowance/replacement criteria;
- belt-edge/tracking accommodation;
- structural brackets/attachments;
- guarding/access details;
- dimensions/tolerances;
- commissioning/maintenance requirements.

1. **Observation:** coarse/mixed product and fines have escaped the intended carrying path at W10; the tunnel/access setting increases consequence and clean-up difficulty.
2. **Interpretation:** lateral product loss is established, while the exact contribution of loading, tracking and local geometry remains location-specific and requires verification.
3. **Primary action:** inspect/verify and correct material contributors such as loading/tracking/geometry where present.
4. **Containment action:** provide suitable side skirting / spill sealing inside the carrying width to help retain residual lateral product on the belt.
5. **Illustration:** use DRAW-0010 and DRAW-0011 as complementary conceptual figures.
6. **Detailed design:** leave final skirt/seal system, dimensions, materials, contact/clearance and support details to OEM/detailed design.
7. **Verification:** confirm representative operation does not show unacceptable lateral product escape, harmful belt interaction, trapping or maintainability problems.

# 11. Transferable Principle for Sample Conveyor Refinement

The W10 closure establishes a useful general side-containment principle that may inform later Sample Conveyor 2/3 development:

> **Where product must be retained laterally on a troughed conveyor, the containment element belongs at the belt carrying edge as a belt-compatible skirt/seal, not as a remote external barrier after product has already escaped.**

This principle may be reused where technically applicable, but the Sample Conveyor feed quantity/product size, usable carrying width and tracking issues remain location-specific and must still be refined independently.

---

# 12. W10 Verification Intent

Before final design/installation, verify:

- actual belt width/troughing geometry;
- representative belt tracking movement;
- available carrying-edge width for the skirt/seal system;
- actual loading/feed behaviour;
- local support structure and available mounting positions;
- required longitudinal extent;
- maintenance/access constraints;
- any potential trapping or belt-wear interaction.

After installation/commissioning, representative operation should establish that:

- coarse/mixed product remains materially better contained on the belt;
- unacceptable lateral escape is reduced;
- the skirt/seal remains stable and serviceable;
- no harmful belt-edge rubbing/gouging is introduced;
- product is not trapped in a damaging manner;
- tracking remains acceptable;
- clean-out/maintenance remains practical.

# 13. Locked Consolidated Technical Statement

> **The preferred W10 containment concept is a conveyor side-skirt / spill-seal arrangement installed along the sides of the troughed carrying belt, with the flexible containment elements positioned within the conveyor carrying width to help retain coarse and mixed product on the belt. The skirt/seal forms a near-vertical lateral containment boundary and is arranged close to, or in controlled belt-friendly contact with, the belt surface as appropriate to the selected OEM system. The tunnel/access setting remains relevant to the consequence of W10 spillage, but the proposed control is not an external walkway barrier. Loading, tracking and local geometry should still be investigated and corrected where they contribute to product loss. Final skirt/seal technology, materials, dimensions, contact/clearance, supports and installation detail remain the responsibility of the OEM/detailed designer.**
