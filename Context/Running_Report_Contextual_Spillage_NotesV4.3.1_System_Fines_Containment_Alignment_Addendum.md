# 1. Inspector Clarification — Fines Presence Across the Inspected Conveyor Ecosystem

The inspector confirms from the full-route physical walkdown that **fines spillage / fines accumulation was present throughout the inspected conveyor ecosystem**.

The governing report wording is:

> **Fines spillage was observed throughout the inspected conveyor ecosystem, with the extent varying considerably between locations. No inspected conveyor section was completely free of fines accumulation.**

This must be immediately qualified as follows:

> **The presence of fines was therefore system-wide, although the severity, local condition and immediate mechanism were not necessarily the same at every location. Carryback remains the strongest recurring mechanism identified across the system, but the report does not assume that every individual fines deposit arose from the same sole cause.**

Important evidence distinction:

- it is acceptable to state that **fines spillage was observed at every inspected conveyor section** based on the inspector’s direct full-route observation;
- do **not** convert this into the stronger and unsupported statement that **carryback was proven as the sole mechanism at every conveyor**;
- some local fines may also reflect transfer fallout, cleaner discharge arrangement, wet-material movement, housekeeping history or other location-specific contributors;
- severity varies significantly and some locations remain low-severity / clean-condition comparators in the sense that they did not show **significant** spillage, even though some fines were still present.

This clarification supersedes older generic wording that recurring fines were observed only across “multiple” conveyor areas where that wording could be read as implying that some inspected conveyor sections were completely free of fines.

# 2. End-to-End Fines Material-Control Philosophy

Fines form part of the combined product from the underground start of the inspected material-handling route. The control objective is therefore end-to-end:

> **Keep fines within the intended conveyor/process stream from one conveyor and transfer stage to the next, prevent residual fines from progressively escaping as carryback, and retain cleaner-removed fines within the intended downstream process route until the process reaches the point at which the fines are deliberately separated, collected and handled.**

This strengthens, but does not replace, the existing V4.3 principles:

- fines are not themselves a defect;
- the defect/concern is loss of fines from the intended product/process route;
- residual fines should be removed before progressing onto the return run where practical;
- cleaner-removed material should remain within the discharge/process route; and
- the cleaner function and containment of removed material are one connected control function.

# 3. CV09 — Downstream Fines-Handling Reference Point

The inspector confirms the following process context:

> **Within the surface screening/washing process, fines separated during washing are conveyed by CV09 to the downstream fines collection/handling point, where that fines stream is intentionally collected and handled for the downstream process.**

This gives the wider fines-containment philosophy a logical end point:

> **Fines remain within the material/process flow → fines are separated where the process intends separation → the separated fines stream is conveyed by CV09 → the fines reach the designated downstream collection/handling point.**

CV09 is therefore not merely an arbitrary local cleaner pilot. It is a useful demonstration location because it combines:

- an observable cleaner/discharge containment deficiency;
- active wet-material fallout;
- downstream carryback consequence;
- a clear opportunity to keep cleaner-removed fines within the process; and
- a known downstream fines-handling destination.

The final report must not describe CV09 as the universal handling endpoint for every material stream. The controlled statement is that **CV09 conveys the fines separated by the screening/washing process to the downstream fines collection/handling point**.

# 4. CV09 Pilot → System Functional Standard → Progressive Application

The intended long-term position is stronger than optional wider consideration.

Governing principle:

> **CV09 proves the function → the function becomes the system reference standard → every relevant head/discharge location is systematically assessed against that standard → the hardware is adapted to local conditions → a geometry-specific exception/deviation requires a technical reason.**

Once the CV09 pilot has demonstrated the required cleaning-and-containment function under representative operation:

> **The relevant conveyor head/discharge locations across the inspected material-handling route should be systematically reviewed against the demonstrated functional standard and progressively brought to that standard where the local geometry, operating duty, material condition and maintenance/access requirements confirm applicability.**

The required common function is:

1. effective removal of residual fines before the return run where applicable;
2. reliable cleaner-to-belt contact through service, including appropriate wear compensation/adjustment;
3. adequate mechanical support, component retention and positive travel control;
4. retention of cleaner-removed fines within the intended downstream chute/discharge/process route;
5. practical inspection, maintenance, replacement, guarding and clean-out access; and
6. representative operating evidence that downstream carryback has been materially reduced.

This is a **system functional standard**, not a mandate to install identical CV09 hardware at every conveyor.

The physical arrangement remains location-specific and should be adapted to:

- local head-pulley/chute geometry;
- belt condition and operating duty;
- cleaner stage availability;
- access and guarding;
- maintenance requirements; and
- verified local carryback/containment condition.

U4 remains the known geometry-specific exception and must continue to receive separate local engineering review rather than automatic duplication of the conventional CV09 arrangement.

Older wording such as “other applicable conveyors can then be assessed” or “wider application may be considered” is now too weak where it describes the fines-control programme. The intended programme is **systematic review followed by progressive adaptation/implementation where applicability is confirmed**.

# 5. W10 Side-Skirting Principle — Future Wider Applicability

The W10 philosophy remains location-specific rather than system-wide.

The governing distinction is:

- **fines cleaner/containment:** system-wide functional standard after successful CV09 demonstration; versus
- **side skirting/spill sealing:** location-specific residual containment applied where comparable lateral coarse/mixed-product loss is identified.

After the W10 side-skirt/spill-seal principle has demonstrated satisfactory representative operating performance:

> **The same functional principle may be adapted at other underground or surface conveyor locations where future inspection or operating trends identify a comparable residual lateral-product-loss condition, subject to confirmation of the local loss mechanism, corrected primary contributors, carrying geometry, belt position/tracking, belt-compatible contact/clearance, clean-out and maintenance requirements.**

Do not imply automatic side-skirt installation throughout the conveyor system.
