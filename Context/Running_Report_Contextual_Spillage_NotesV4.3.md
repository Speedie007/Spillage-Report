# 1. Audit Goals and Assessment Philosophy

The audit objectives remain to:

1. identify causes and contributing factors associated with combined-product spillage in the specified product-transport and processing areas;
2. identify the locations and operating conditions under which spillage occurs;
3. determine whether individual spillage areas are associated with one cause or several interacting factors; and
4. define practical remedial actions, engineering controls, maintenance requirements, operating procedures or policies that may eliminate, reduce or control the identified spillage.

The audit is a qualitative engineering/mechanism-focused assessment rather than a complete quantified mass-loss study.

Visible extent may indicate severity or persistence, but it must not be converted into unsupported tonnage, mass flow, volume, design capacity, percentage deviation, remaining structural capacity or OEM limit.

The governing analytical sequence is:

> **Observed condition → material/spillage type → immediate mechanism → contributing condition(s) → probable initiating cause where supportable → consequence → existing control → control deficiency → remedial direction → verification.**

A governing principle remains:

> **The location at which spilled material is observed is not necessarily the location at which the initiating failure occurred.**

# 7. Material Journey and Main Mechanism Families

Combined product already contains fines from blasting and gains additional fines through crushing and subsequent handling. It is transported through the underground crusher/UC system, other conveyor sections, surface screening/washing and loading/sampling areas.

Fines are not inherently a defect. The audit concern is where fines, lump ore, combined product or process water leave the route/process intended to contain and transport them.

## 7.1 Fines / carryback

Recurring return-side/downstream fines accumulation remains the strongest repeated system pattern.

Carryback develops when residual material remains adhered to the belt after the main product has discharged, passes onto the return run, and then progressively detaches. Return rollers/idlers can dislodge and redistribute additional adhered fines, so the eventual deposit may be remote from the initiating head-discharge/cleaner region.

Wet/sticky fines are recorded as more persistent, but the cleaner-control philosophy applies to **both wet and dry residual fines**.

## 7.2 Coarse / mixed-product transit loss

Local coarse/mixed product is present outside the intended carrying path at W10, the Sample Conveyors and selected other locations.

Relevant contributors can include feed quantity/variation, product-size distribution, loading position/trajectory, usable belt width, lateral belt position/tracking, transfer geometry and insufficient local side containment.

## 7.3 Tracking / alignment

Tracking affects containment because lateral belt displacement reduces usable carrying margin and may move carried product toward/over an edge.

Causal strength remains location-specific and must not be transferred automatically between assets.

## 7.4 Water / wet fines / slurry

Process/cleaning water can mobilise fines and move contamination away from the initiating source.

Screens provide direct dynamic proof of active process-water escape. Other wet/slurry areas do not automatically share the same water source or mechanism.

## 7.5 Event-driven product handling

Product deliberately removed after a trip/blockage or to facilitate restart is an event-driven handling condition and should not automatically be classified as chronic mechanical spillage at the temporary placement location.

## 7.6 Housekeeping

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence; source control should reduce the recurrence.**

# 10. Governing Engineering Control Philosophy

Higher-order objective:

> **Keep combined product — fines and coarse/lump ore — within the intended material-handling/process route throughout transit, and retain process water within the process route where it is intended to remain, using controls appropriate to the specific mechanism and local geometry.**

Practical hierarchy:

1. establish the actual loss mechanism;
2. verify relevant operating condition/geometry;
3. correct primary source/contributor;
4. provide required primary containment/process routing;
5. add secondary containment only for genuine residual duty;
6. retain housekeeping/recovery as consequence control; and
7. prove representative operating performance where functional success is claimed.

Fines-control principle:

> **Do not merely clean the belt. Keep the fines inside the process.**

Cleaner standardisation principle:

> **Standardise the required function; do not automatically standardise identical hardware.**

# 11. CV09 — Locked Functional Cleaner / Fines-Containment Concept

**Status:** CLOSED / LOCKED AT FUNCTIONAL-CONCEPT LEVEL — OEM DETAILED DESIGN AND SITE VERIFICATION REMAIN OPEN BY DESIGN.

## 11.1 Existing mechanism

At CV09:

- residual fines remain after the main combined-product discharge;
- the existing cleaner removes some material but is outside/open to effective head-chute containment;
- successfully removed wet material can fall into the surrounding plant area;
- residual carryback can remain on the belt and pass onto the return run; and
- return rollers/idlers can dislodge further fines downstream.

The cleaner concept applies to both wet and dry residual fines.

## 11.2 Governing objective

> **Remove residual fines before the belt enters the return run, and remove them at a location where the material naturally remains inside the existing head-chute discharge flow.**

## 11.3 Accepted physical concept

- cleaner stages act against belt while it remains positively supported by the head pulley;
- **two stages are the preferred/default arrangement**;
- where verified constraints prevent two stages, **at least one effective pulley-supported stage is the constrained minimum**;
- head pulley and cleaner stages are located inside the existing head/discharge chute;
- each cleaner uses a full-width scraper blade and transverse support/cross-arm spanning the chute;
- the support extends through guided openings/slots in both chute side walls;
- the guided path permits wear-compensation movement while retaining the intended blade orientation/function;
- external spring/self-tensioning acts on the protruding support ends to maintain controlled cleaner-to-belt contact as the wear element reduces;
- positive mechanical travel/wear limitation prevents uncontrolled continued advancement after the allowable wear/travel limit is reached;
- scraper-removed fines fall naturally inside the existing head chute and remain with the combined-product discharge flow;
- no separate scraper-fines recovery chute is required at the cleaner; and
- maintainability, inspection, adjustment, replacement, guarding and safe access must remain practical.

Governing principles:

> **Location provides mechanical stability; the tensioning system maintains contact as wear occurs.**

> **Residual fines are removed inside the discharge chute, so gravity keeps them within the intended material flow automatically.**

## 11.4 Wider cleaner standardisation

CV09 is to establish a proven **functional cleaner standard**, not an identical hardware package.

After the pilot, each applicable installation should be assessed for:

- pulley-supported cleaning location;
- effective stage count;
- blade coverage/contact;
- wear compensation/self-tensioning;
- positive travel limitation;
- mechanical integrity/component retention;
- removed material remaining inside the local discharge path;
- maintainability/access; and
- actual downstream carryback performance.

U4 remains a geometry-specific exception requiring separate local engineering review.

# 12. W10 — Locked Side-Skirting / Spill-Seal Concept

**Status:** CLOSED / LOCKED AT FUNCTIONAL-CONCEPT LEVEL.

## 12.1 Governing correction

The historical interpretation of a remote external walkway-protection barrier is superseded.

> **Keep coarse/mixed product on the conveyor belt by providing side skirting / spill sealing along the conveyor sides within the carrying width.**

The tunnel/restricted access route remains relevant to consequence and housekeeping difficulty, not the defining purpose of the skirt/seal system.

## 12.2 Accepted physical principle

- flexible/soft, durable belt-compatible skirt/spill-seal elements on both conveyor sides where required;
- active skirt/seal positioned on the **inside of the conveyor carrying width**;
- near-vertical lateral containment boundary along the troughed belt edge;
- lower seal region close to, or in controlled belt-friendly contact with, the belt according to the selected OEM system;
- not a freestanding external barrier and not a rigid belt guide;
- avoid harmful rubbing/gouging, damaging product trapping and inaccessible build-up zones;
- retain practical inspection, adjustment, replacement and clean-out access; and
- loading, feed distribution, tracking and local geometry still require investigation/correction where they contribute.

Hierarchy:

> **Correct primary contributors where present → retain residual lateral product on the belt using side skirting/spill sealing → verify representative operation.**

# 19. Trip / Restart Product Handling — Locked Operational-Control Concept

**Status:** CLOSED AT REPORT FUNCTIONAL-CONCEPT LEVEL — FORMAL SOUTH32 PROCEDURE AND RESPONSIBILITY CHAIN REMAIN TO BE CONFIRMED.

The inspector does not have direct knowledge of the mine's formal internal trip/restart procedure.

Source-recorded operating understanding:

- trips, blockages or related operating events can occur during normal plant operation;
- this is a possible/intermittent event, not an established regular condition;
- where a conveyor remains heavily loaded and retained product hinders restart, some product may be manually removed from the belt to reduce restart load; and
- to the best of the inspector's knowledge, product removed for this purpose is expected to be manually returned to the conveyor during cleanup/recovery.

Binding distinction:

> **Event-driven product removal may create a temporary deposit as part of restart recovery. Chronic mechanical spillage is material that leaves the intended carrying/process path because of an ongoing or recurring loss mechanism. The two conditions should be distinguished where operating history allows.**

Locked report-level objective:

> **Where product has to be removed from a loaded conveyor to facilitate restart after a trip or blockage, the removed product should remain under controlled temporary handling and should be recovered/reintroduced to the approved process route rather than becoming an unmanaged secondary deposit.**

The report should require South32 to confirm:

- applicable trip/restart/belt-clearing procedure;
- conditions in which removal is authorised;
- isolation/access/restart controls;
- authorised responsibility;
- controlled temporary placement;
- approved recovery/reintroduction method; and
- how event-driven deposits are distinguished from chronic source-generated spillage.

The audit must not invent the mine's procedure, thresholds, roles or laydown arrangements.
