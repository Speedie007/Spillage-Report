# 2. Governing Report Identity and Reader Journey

The report remains a **hybrid engineering audit report** with three integrated layers:

1. **Formal shell** — issue control, metadata, scope, limitations, references, sign-off and controlled appendices.
2. **Narrative core** — a connected engineering explanation of the material-loss and process-water-control problem across the inspected system.
3. **Technical evidence backbone** — controlled photographs/videos, canonical evidence IDs, qualifications, verification requirements, technical registers and conceptual figures.

The evidence supports the engineering story; it is not the story by itself.

The accepted reader sequence remains:

> **Explain → Prove → Interpret → Control → Act → Verify → Close**

The report should work for multiple reader levels:

- operations / maintenance readers should understand the physical condition and practical action;
- engineering readers should be able to follow mechanism, evidence, qualification, measurement/design-development need and technical control;
- management readers should be able to understand the overall problem, priority programme and engineering philosophy without reading the entire evidence register.

# 3. Binding Reader-Facing Language Rule

The preferred report voice is:

> **Formal engineering narrative — technically rigorous, measured and explanatory, without sounding legalistic, forensic, bureaucratic or unnecessarily categorical.**

The accepted tonal benchmark is the V0.2 explanatory engineering style.

“Softened” means **softening the delivery, not weakening the evidence or the engineering conclusion**.

Reader-facing narrative should generally follow:

> **What was observed → what pattern / mechanism is indicated → why it matters → what should be done or verified next.**

Preferred narrative transitions include:

- “The evidence indicates…”
- “The inspection identified…”
- “The observed condition suggests…”
- “Taken together, these findings indicate…”
- “This is important because…”
- “The significance of this condition is…”
- “The recommended response is therefore…”
- “Further measurement is required to establish…”
- “The available evidence does not establish…”

Precise implementation / close-out language remains appropriate in Part XI, verification registers, acceptance criteria, structural-assessment requirements and technical appendices, but should not dominate the reader-facing narrative.

Directly verified facts remain direct. Language harmonisation must not obscure defects, weaken justified recommendations, remove evidence qualifications or convert established facts into unnecessary hedging.

# 4. Binding Terminology Rule — Describe the Physical Path Directly

The report should not use **“envelope”** as a general-purpose substitute for the physical path or process being discussed.

Prefer direct wording such as:

- **material-handling path** / **intended carrying path** for conveyor product movement;
- **usable carrying area / carrying width** where available belt space is relevant;
- **process stream**, **process path** or **chute/process route** for transfer/discharge material;
- **screening/washing process** or **screening/chute process** for process water and wet fines;
- **installation space** for available physical room;
- **containment arrangement / containment geometry** for the actual physical control arrangement.

Retain **“envelope” only where it has a specific engineering purpose describing a defined geometric or operating range**, for example:

- normal belt operating envelope;
- tracking envelope;
- verified operating envelope where belt movement / clearance range has actually been established or is to be established.

Reader-facing test:

> **If the sentence describes where product or process water should travel or remain, describe the physical path/process directly. If it describes a measurable movement, clearance or operating boundary, “envelope” may remain.**

# 6. Evidence and Engineering Discipline

The report must continue to distinguish:

1. **Direct observation** — condition recorded directly during inspection.
2. **Visual evidence-supported condition** — materially demonstrated by supplied photograph/video.
3. **Source-recorded information** — operating, maintenance or historical information from questionnaire / inspection records.
4. **Engineering interpretation / hypothesis** — reasoned explanation consistent with evidence but not fully proven.
5. **Working remedial concept** — engineering direction requiring verification and design development.
6. **Unresolved / requires verification** — magnitude, mechanism, capacity or geometry that cannot be responsibly closed from current evidence.

Binding principles:

- observed deposit location is not automatically the initiating cause location;
- visible severity must not be converted into unsupported mass/tonnage/loss rate;
- still photographs can prove physical condition without proving complete dynamic mechanism or causal history;
- no unmeasured dimension, tolerance, feed rate, structural capacity or OEM limit is to be introduced as fact;
- similar-looking physical conditions do not automatically justify identical causal conclusions;
- conceptual solutions are not fabrication designs;
- source control should precede secondary containment where practicable;
- representative operating performance is required where functional success is being claimed.

# 7. Material Journey and Governing Audit Principle

The inspected system handles combined product comprising coarse/lump ore together with fines. Fines are already part of the product stream and additional fines are generated through crushing and subsequent handling.

The audit concern is not the presence of fines or lump ore. It is the loss of product or process water from the physical path or process intended to carry, transfer, screen, wash or discharge it.

The central audit principle is:

> **The point at which spilled material is observed is not necessarily the point at which the initiating failure occurred.**

Examples:

- residual fines can remain on the return belt and detach progressively downstream;
- water can mobilise fines away from an original escape point;
- mistracking can reduce available carrying margin before product is lost later;
- product removed during trip/restart clearing can produce a secondary deposit away from the initiating event.

Assessment sequence:

> **Observed deposit → material type → immediate mechanism → contributing condition → initiating cause (where supportable) → consequence → control.**

Where initiating cause cannot be established, it remains a verification item rather than an assigned conclusion.

# 8. Consolidated Mechanism Hierarchy

The evidence supports several interacting mechanism families rather than one universal mine-wide spillage defect.

## 8.1 Fines / carryback

Recurring fines accumulation along return-side and downstream conveyor areas is the strongest repeated system pattern.

Control direction:

- remove residual material effectively at appropriate discharge/cleaner locations;
- maintain the cleaner function through wear/adjustment;
- keep cleaner-removed material inside the intended process stream;
- treat downstream accumulation as a consequence that may originate at an upstream discharge/cleaner location.

## 8.2 Coarse / mixed product transit loss

Local coarse or mixed product is present outside the intended carrying path at W10, Sample Conveyors and selected other positions.

Control direction:

- understand feed/product/trajectory and usable carrying width;
- verify tracking/alignment where supported;
- correct primary contributors;
- apply secondary side containment only where a residual duty remains.

## 8.3 Alignment / tracking as a material-containment factor

Belt tracking is both a mechanical condition and a material-containment control because lateral displacement reduces usable carrying margin.

The causal strength differs by location:

- U4 has a supported relationship between mistracking and one-sided product loss;
- Sample Conveyor 3 has supported lateral offset/mistracking plus significant product loss, but magnitude/root cause remain unmeasured;
- L3 has a supported offset/alignment concern without an equivalent proven significant solid-product causal chain;
- Sample Conveyor 2 significant mistracking remains unconfirmed.

## 8.4 Water / wet fines / slurry

Process/cleaning water can mobilise fines and spread contamination away from source points.

The Screens provide direct dynamic evidence of active process-water escape. CV01 and CV04 demonstrate wet/slurry conditions but do not establish one common water source.

Water containment and structural condition are related but must not be collapsed into unsupported single-cause statements.

## 8.5 Event-driven operational spillage

Product deliberately removed during a trip, blockage or restart intervention is a separate event-driven mechanism and should not automatically be classified as chronic mechanically generated spillage at the deposit location.

Control direction is procedural: authorised removal, temporary placement/containment, recovery/reintroduction and accountability.

## 8.6 Housekeeping

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence. Source control should reduce the recurrence.**

Housekeeping remains necessary for access, recovery and safe operation, but repeated cleaning of the same area is not evidence that the initiating source has been corrected.

# 10. Governing Engineering Control Philosophy

The higher-order engineering objective is:

> **Keep the combined product — fines and coarse/lump ore — within the intended material-handling and process path throughout transit, and retain process water within the screening/washing or other process route where it is intended to remain, using controls appropriate to the specific failure mechanism and local geometry.**

Practical hierarchy:

1. identify the actual loss mechanism;
2. verify the relevant operating condition / geometry;
3. correct the primary source or contributor where practicable;
4. provide the required primary containment / process routing;
5. add secondary containment only where a residual duty remains;
6. use housekeeping/recovery as consequence control;
7. prove the result under representative operation where functional performance is claimed.

The guiding fines-control principle remains:

> **Do not merely clean the belt. Keep the fines inside the process.**

Standardise the **required engineering function**, not automatically identical hardware.

# 19. V4 Change-Control Rule Going Forward

V4 is a living engineering-development context.

For each future solution-refinement session:

1. capture the accepted technical clarification in V4;
2. record unresolved items explicitly rather than filling gaps by assumption;
3. retain evidence qualification boundaries;
4. reference the affected asset / CTD figure / report section where practical;
5. use Git history as the audit trail of contextual development.

A new V4.x/V5 context should only be created when a meaningful contextual baseline freeze is required, rather than for every incremental clarification.

The immediate objective is therefore not to rewrite the report again yet. It is to **close the engineering understanding gaps in the proposed solutions, capture the agreed solution intent in V4, and then feed the mature technical context back into the report in a controlled revision.**
