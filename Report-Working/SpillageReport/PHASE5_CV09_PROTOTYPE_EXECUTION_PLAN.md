## 2. Why CV09 Is the Prototype Case

CV09 is sufficiently involved to test the report architecture without becoming unnecessarily strenuous as a first layout case.

It contains:

- a clear physical cleaner / discharge arrangement;
- visible wet-fines/slurry consequence;
- direct dynamic evidence of active fallout during operation;
- a meaningful containment-boundary issue;
- an identifiable engineering-control philosophy;
- a working pilot recommendation that must remain distinct from final design;
- both still photographs and video evidence;
- enough complexity to test narrative/evidence integration without the multiple unrelated structural sub-stories present at Screens or L1.

CV09 therefore provides an effective first test of the principle:

> **Do not merely clean the belt. Keep the fines inside the process.**

## 4. Evidentiary and Wording Controls

The prototype must distinguish clearly between what is directly demonstrated and what remains design-development work.

### Directly supported

The evidence supports that:

- wet fines/slurry accumulated below and around the CV09 discharge/cleaner region;
- the local cleaner/discharge arrangement is open/non-contained relative to the effective process containment boundary;
- wet-material fallout was active during operation;
- the cleaner/chute relationship allows removed material to escape into the surrounding plant environment rather than remaining inherently captured within the intended downstream fines-handling route.

### Do not overstate

The prototype must not claim that the current evidence establishes:

- final chute-extension dimensions;
- final cleaner type;
- quantified scraper contact pressure;
- final blade geometry or wear compensation arrangement;
- final support/bracket design;
- final maintenance-access solution;
- a completed pilot design;
- that every particle visible below CV09 originated from one single instantaneous mechanism.

These remain engineering/design-development items.

## 5. Prototype Narrative Objective

The reader should understand the following sequence without having to decode a defect register:

1. residual wet fines are present at the discharge/cleaner region;
2. the cleaner removes material from the belt, but removal alone is not the complete control function;
3. because the removed material is not adequately retained within the effective discharge/process containment envelope, it can fall into the plant area;
4. dynamic evidence confirms that material fallout was active during operation;
5. the engineering response must therefore integrate **belt cleaning and containment of the removed material**;
6. CV09 is an appropriate pilot location to develop and prove that functional cleaner/containment philosophy before considering location-specific application elsewhere.

The module should lead the reader from observation to mechanism to consequence to control.

## 6. Working Prototype Page Architecture

The target is a **two-page technical narrative module**, with flexibility to become three pages if legibility or evidence integrity requires it. Page count is subordinate to clarity.

### Page / Sequence 1 — What is happening and why it matters

Purpose:
- establish CV09 visually and explain the containment-boundary problem before presenting the recommendation.

Proposed content:

- short narrative lead paragraph;
- `CV09-REF-003` as the principal cleaner/arrangement still;
- representative frame from `CV09-REF-008` where it adds clearer containment-boundary / geometry understanding;
- `CV09-REF-002` as the principal consequence image;
- concise story-level observation text rather than repetitive per-image defect metadata.

Reader takeaway:

> The cleaner arrangement is part of the material-loss mechanism because removed wet fines are not adequately retained within the effective process containment envelope.

### Page / Sequence 2 — Operating proof and engineering response

Purpose:
- prove the condition was active during operation and convert the evidence into a qualified engineering-control philosophy.

Proposed content:

- representative frame or controlled frame sequence from `CV09-REF-006` showing active fallout;
- `CV09-REF-005` only if the wider extent materially improves the story and page balance;
- compact grouped block containing:
  - Observation;
  - Engineering Assessment;
  - Control Philosophy;
  - Recommended Next Action;
- explicit statement that the CV09 arrangement is a **pilot/design-development recommendation**, not a completed final design.

Reader takeaway:

> Effective control requires both removing residual fines from the belt and keeping the removed fines inside the intended process stream.

## 8. Caption and Traceability Standard to Test

Every body image must retain:

- canonical evidence ID;
- asset/location identification where not already obvious from the story heading;
- concise description of what the image demonstrates;
- qualification where the image could otherwise invite over-interpretation.

Captions should explain **why the image matters**, not merely restate what is visible.

Avoid repeating full finding metadata beneath every photograph. Shared finding/evidence metadata should be grouped at story level.

---

## 9. Working Engineering Assessment Baseline

The prototype should work from the following controlled meaning, subject to wording refinement during drafting:

> CV09 demonstrates that belt cleaning and material containment cannot be treated as separate functions. The existing arrangement removes wet residual material from the belt, but the cleaner is positioned such that removed material is not inherently retained within the effective discharge/process containment envelope. The supplied evidence includes active wet-material fallout during operation and substantial wet-fines/slurry accumulation below the cleaner region. The evidence therefore supports development of an integrated cleaner-and-containment arrangement that removes residual material while directing it back into the intended fines-handling route.

Qualification to remain visible:

> The inspection evidence does not define final cleaner type, scraper contact pressure, chute-extension dimensions, structural support details or maintenance-access geometry. These require design verification.

---

## 10. Working Recommendation Baseline

The prototype should present CV09 as a pilot / design-development action:

> Develop and trial a CV09 cleaner/containment arrangement that maintains effective belt-cleaning contact and directs removed wet fines back into the intended chute/process stream. Verify the existing belt/discharge geometry, available containment envelope, cleaner mounting, wear compensation, access, maintainability, safety and downstream fines route before finalising the design. Following successful trial and performance verification, assess the functional cleaner/containment philosophy for location-specific application at other suitable conveyor discharges.

Important:

- standardise the **required function**, not identical hardware;
- U4 remains an explicit geometry-specific exception requiring separate assessment;
- final implementation elsewhere follows local verification, not automatic replication.

## 11. Layout Stress Tests

The CV09 prototype must test the following reusable report behaviours:

1. **Narrative-first entry** — reader understands the issue before reading technical metadata.
2. **Hero + support hierarchy** — not every image receives equal visual weight.
3. **Still + video integration** — representative frames remain traceable to controlled video evidence.
4. **Multi-photo efficiency** — avoid one-photo-per-page rhythm.
5. **Grouped metadata** — evidence traceability without repeated clinical blocks.
6. **Qualification visibility** — uncertainty is preserved without making the prose unreadable.
7. **Observation → Assessment → Action flow** — engineering logic remains obvious.
8. **Page-break discipline** — technical story is not split arbitrarily.
9. **Readable captions** — canonical ID visible without dominating the page.
10. **Formal narrative identity** — module feels like part of an engineering audit, not a slideshow or defect database.

### Step 5 — Render and visual QA
Render the prototype and inspect every page for:
- image legibility;
- caption readability;
- evidence-ID visibility;
- awkward white space;
- broken image/caption relationships;
- excessive metadata density;
- page-break problems;
- overall engineering-document polish.

### Step 6 — Inspector review
Review the prototype for:
- engineering accuracy;
- narrative clarity;
- whether evidence proves the statements being made;
- whether any E item is visually underused;
- whether the S item adds enough value to occupy body space;
- whether the module feels too clinical or too informal;
- whether the recommendation remains appropriately qualified.

### Step 7 — Lock the evidence-module grammar
After approval, record:
- approved page hierarchy;
- caption convention;
- metadata convention;
- video-frame convention;
- Observation / Assessment / Action convention;
- acceptable evidence density;
- layout rules to propagate into Phase 6.

## 13. Prototype Acceptance Criteria

Phase 5 CV09 prototype is accepted only when the inspector agrees that:

- the story is understandable without reading an evidence register;
- the technical proof remains auditable and traceable;
- E evidence receives appropriate visual priority;
- S evidence is used only where it genuinely improves the story;
- video-derived figures remain clearly identified as video evidence;
- direct observation, visual evidence and engineering inference are not blurred together;
- the recommendation reads as a controlled pilot/design-development action;
- the module looks formal and professional without reverting to a repetitive defect-sheet format;
- the same design grammar can reasonably be adapted to Screens, L1, U4, Sample Conveyor 3 and simpler assets.
