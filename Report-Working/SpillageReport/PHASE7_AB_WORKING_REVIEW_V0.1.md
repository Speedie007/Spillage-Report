# 1. Pass 7A — Narrative Continuity / Duplication Review Framework

The purpose of Pass 7A is to tighten the approved report, not to rebuild it.

The following checkpoints are now established for the V0.2 wording review.

## 7A-01 — Executive Summary vs later mechanism sections
The Executive Summary should state the complete audit conclusion once at management level. Later sections may explain and prove the mechanisms, but should not repeatedly reproduce the same full list of carryback, mistracking, water, loading/containment and structural issues.

**Review test:** retain repetition only where it changes reader function from summary → mechanism → proof → action.

## 7A-02 — Cause-location vs deposit-location principle
The principle that the visible deposit location is not necessarily the initiating cause location is central and should be established strongly near the start of the report.

**Review test:** later references should normally be short callbacks rather than full re-explanations unless a case materially demonstrates the principle.

## 7A-03 — Source control vs housekeeping
The governing principle remains:

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence. Source control should reduce the recurrence.**

**Review test:** use as a report-level control philosophy and in the operational section where needed; avoid repeating the full phrase mechanically in every asset module.

## 7A-04 — CV09 story/evidence separation
CV09 remains the principal cleaner/containment proof case.

**Review test:**
- story layer explains the control problem;
- evidence module proves it;
- interpretation states what remains unmeasured;
- action section defines the pilot.

Do not allow the evidence captions/assessment tables to restate the entire preceding narrative.

## 7A-05 — Sample Conveyor 2 vs Sample Conveyor 3
The two conveyors share the feed/product-size/usable-width question, but their evidence status is different.

- **Sample 2:** significant mistracking is not established; feed/loading containment is the primary verified concern.
- **Sample 3:** lateral offset/mistracking is visually supported in addition to feed/loading containment concerns.

**Review test:** combine common explanation where efficient, then separate the evidence status and corrective sequence clearly.

## 7A-06 — U4 vs L3 evidence-discipline contrast
U4 is the strong local tracking/product-loss case. L3 is deliberately weaker and remains a verification case.

**Review test:** do not flatten them into one generic alignment statement. U4 should demonstrate confirmed mistracking/one-side loss; L3 should demonstrate why offset does not automatically establish a solid-product causal chain.

## 7A-07 — Screens: water escape and structure are related but separate stories
The report should keep distinct:
1. active process-water loss of containment;
2. corrosion/deterioration;
3. cut/notched support members.

**Review test:** avoid wording that silently converts correlation into proof that the water escape caused every structural defect.

## 7A-08 — L1: consequence, belt interaction and structure
Keep separate but connected:
1. extensive accumulation;
2. previous/intermittent belt contact;
3. concrete deterioration/exposed reinforcement;
4. steel corrosion/section deterioration.

**Review test:** the evidence supports previous/intermittent contact; it does not prove continuous present contact.

## 7A-09 — Recommendation sections should not become a second findings chapter
The recommendation programme should convert evidence into controlled action rather than repeating all local observations.

**Review test:** each recommendation should answer:
- objective;
- action type;
- prerequisite verification;
- dependency;
- acceptance criterion;
- evidence traceability.

## 7A-10 — Conclusion discipline
The Conclusion should close the engineering argument and priority programme without introducing new mechanisms or new corrective concepts.

**Review test:** every conclusion statement must already be established in the body.

# 2. Pass 7B — Recommendation Maturity Register

## 2.1 CV09 — Cleaner / containment pilot

### Engineering objective
Reduce recurring wet-fines carryback while ensuring material actually removed by the cleaner remains inside the intended process/fines-discharge stream rather than becoming environmental spillage below the conveyor.

### Action type
**Pilot + mechanical design development + controlled performance verification.**

### Required inspection / measurement
Before final design:
- record existing cleaner type/position and blade coverage;
- verify cleaner-to-belt geometry and available adjustment/travel;
- verify chute/enclosure geometry around the cleaner;
- identify the intended route for removed wet fines;
- confirm maintenance/inspection access;
- document representative wet/sticky operating condition sufficiently to judge the pilot;
- establish a pre-trial carryback/spillage baseline suitable for before/after comparison.

### Design-development requirement
Develop the cleaner and containment function as one system. The working concept may include:
- cleaner located within the effective containment envelope;
- self-tensioning/spring-loaded contact principle;
- positive wear/travel limit;
- primary/secondary cleaning stages if justified by service condition;
- controlled route for removed fines back into the intended process stream;
- safe inspection, adjustment and replacement access.

The concept remains **not for fabrication until geometry and design inputs are verified**.

### Implementation dependency
- site measurements / access;
- mine operational and safety review;
- final cleaner selection and wear criteria;
- detailed mechanical design before fabrication/installation;
- confirmation that the proposed arrangement creates no guarding, trapping or maintenance conflict.

### Success / acceptance criterion
Success is functional, not cosmetic:
- downstream carryback materially reduced relative to the pre-trial condition;
- removed wet fines remain in the intended process stream;
- stable cleaner-to-belt contact through the agreed wear range;
- no unacceptable belt damage or abnormal cleaner wear;
- maintainable inspection/adjustment access retained;
- no new trapping/guarding conflict introduced;
- monitoring period and records sufficient to make a defensible rollout decision.

### Evidence / case traceability
Principal case: **CV09**.
Controlled evidence includes `CV09-REF-002`, `CV09-REF-003`, video `CV09-REF-006`, with `CV09-REF-008` available as supplementary dynamic evidence.

### Client / OEM / competent-person decisions
- final cleaner make/type and OEM requirements;
- acceptable blade/contact/wear limits;
- final chute modification details;
- operational trial duration and acceptance authority;
- final decision on wider standardisation.

## 2.2 U4 Tripper Car — tracking / alignment

### Engineering objective
Restore and maintain a centred belt path through the full tripper operating range so that the confirmed one-sided product-loss condition is removed or materially reduced.

### Action type
**Mechanical inspection + quantitative alignment/tracking verification + corrective adjustment/repair + operating proof.**

### Required inspection / measurement
- establish fixed/repeatable geometry reference points;
- measure relevant tail-pulley / support geometry;
- record as-found belt position/centring;
- verify adjustment provisions and identify the mechanical/structural source of any misalignment;
- remove trapped accumulated material that could distort tracking assessment;
- observe tracking under representative operating condition through the full tripper travel range;
- document side clearance / carrying margin at critical positions.

### Design-development requirement
No new design should be assumed necessary until the source of the pulley/tracking error is established. If existing adjustment provisions or support geometry are defective/inadequate, develop the required repair/modification from measured geometry.

The U4 cleaner arrangement is a separate local issue and must not automatically receive the CV09 cleaner design.

### Implementation dependency
- safe representative operating test;
- verified adjustment/repair method;
- suitable fixed reference/datum points;
- local mechanical/structural condition confirmed before final correction.

### Success / acceptance criterion
- relevant pulley/alignment geometry measured and corrected as required;
- centred/stable tracking demonstrated under representative operation across full tripper travel;
- adequate side clearance/carrying margin demonstrated;
- the previously observed one-sided product-loss condition materially reduced or removed;
- as-left geometry/reference points recorded for future trending;
- U4 custom cleaner configuration assessed separately.

### Evidence / case traceability
Principal case: **U4 Tripper Car**.
Key evidence includes `UGU4-REF-009` for mistracking, `UGU4-REF-007` for one-side product-loss consequence, video `UGU4-REF-010` for alignment context, and `UGU4-REF-011/-012` for the custom cleaner arrangement.

### Client / OEM / competent-person decisions

- repair/replacement decision for defective adjustment/support hardware;
- operational acceptance after correction.

## 2.3 Sample Conveyor 3 — feed / tracking / containment

### Engineering objective
Reduce coarse/mixed-product loss by first correcting the primary feed/tracking contributors and only then finalising any secondary side-containment arrangement.

### Action type
**Feed verification + mechanical tracking/alignment investigation + corrective work + secondary containment design if still required.**

### Required inspection / measurement
- measure usable belt carrying width;
- document representative product size distribution/condition relevant to containment;
- identify and quantify the actual upstream feed-control / metering arrangement;
- observe representative feed quantity/trajectory;
- measure the lateral belt offset/mistracking condition;
- inspect head/tail alignment, belt tensioning and support/idler condition;
- remove trapped accumulated product before final tracking assessment;
- establish the required belt-edge operating clearance for any future skirt/barrier.

### Design-development requirement
Correct/optimise feed and tracking first. Then determine whether secondary side containment is still required and, if so, develop:
- required length/location;
- support geometry;
- flexible retention/skirt element if appropriate;
- non-contact belt clearance through the expected tracking envelope;
- clean-out and maintenance access;
- trapping/wear safeguards.

### Implementation dependency
- representative operating observation;
- actual feed-control mechanism identified;
- mistracking root contributors investigated;
- belt operating envelope established before containment geometry is frozen.

### Success / acceptance criterion
- usable belt width documented;
- representative feed/product-size condition verified;
- lateral offset quantified and root mechanical contributors checked;
- feed/tracking corrected or controlled to the agreed condition;
- coarse-product loss materially reduced;
- any remaining side-containment system retains required belt clearance and maintainability without becoming a normal belt-contact surface.

### Evidence / case traceability
Principal case: **Sample Conveyor 3**.
Key evidence includes `LoadoutStationSample3-REF-013`, `-005/-006`, `-002` and `-012`.

### Client / OEM / competent-person decisions
- acceptable feed rate/throughput and process-control set point;

## 2.5 W10 — side-containment feasibility / source control

### Engineering objective
Reduce recurring mixed/coarse-product migration into the confined tunnel/access route while ensuring that any secondary barrier does not become a normal belt-contact surface or create a new trapping, wear or maintenance hazard.

### Action type
**Root-contributor investigation + geometry survey + source-control review + secondary containment feasibility/design.**

### Required inspection / measurement
- establish the normal belt tracking envelope;
- record trough/belt/support geometry;
- measure available side clearances and structural attachment envelope;
- inspect likely source locations and distinguish carryback-related fines from coarse/mixed-product loss mechanisms;
- assess access/clean-out requirements in the restricted tunnel;
- identify locations at which a barrier could retain escaped material without interfering with belt operation.

### Design-development requirement
Do not treat a side barrier/rail as the primary cause correction by default. First investigate initiating contributors and available source controls.

If a secondary barrier remains justified, develop it so that:
- it remains outside the verified normal belt tracking envelope;
- it does not act as a routine rubbing/contact surface;
- trapped material can be safely removed;
- belt inspection and maintenance access remain practical;
- wear, impact and structural reactions are considered.

### Implementation dependency
- measured operating envelope;
- initiating contributors reviewed;
- confined/restricted-access maintenance requirements incorporated;
- structural attachment adequacy verified where relevant.

### Success / acceptance criterion
- normal tracking envelope established;
- trough/belt/support/clearance geometry verified;
- initiating contributors investigated and corrected where practicable;
- any secondary barrier demonstrated to remain non-contact in normal operation;
- access, trapping, wear and maintenance risks assessed;
- tunnel-side product accumulation materially reduced without introducing a new belt interaction hazard.

### Evidence / case traceability
Principal case: **W10 Conveyor — Primary Feed**.
Evidence pool includes `W10-REF-002`, `W10-REF-010/-011`, with `W10-REF-006/-009` available for mechanism/design context.

### Client / OEM / competent-person decisions
- acceptable belt tracking/clearance envelope;
- final barrier type/material/structural support;
- confined-space/access and maintenance controls;
- final source-control versus secondary-containment scope.

## 2.6 Screens — water escape + structural assessment

### Engineering objective
Stop process water/wet fines escaping the intended screening/chute process path and separately establish the structural condition, remaining capacity and repair/replacement requirement of damaged/corroded/cut support members.

### Action type
**Safe-shutdown mechanism investigation + containment design development + competent structural assessment.**

### Required inspection / measurement
For water containment:
- inspect during safe shutdown and, where safely possible, correlate with representative operating evidence;
- identify the actual escape path(s);
- inspect seals, chute interfaces, screen movement/clearance, local enclosure geometry and maintenance condition;
- record geometry required for any containment modification.

For structure:
- competent structural inspection of cut/notched/corroded members;
- record relevant section loss, dimensions, connection condition and support geometry as required by the structural engineer;
- establish whether installation/maintenance clearance has driven previous modifications without assuming the reason.

### Design-development requirement
Water containment modification follows mechanism confirmation; it is not to be designed from assumption alone.

Structural repair/replacement must be developed by the responsible competent structural authority. Any geometry change to provide future installation clearance requires calculation/approval before implementation.

### Implementation dependency
- safe shutdown/access;
- mechanism confirmed;
- structural engineer / competent person appointed;
- repair or containment geometry measured and checked against surrounding equipment clearance.

### Success / acceptance criterion
- actual water escape mechanism/path identified;
- containment modification addresses that verified mechanism;
- representative operation confirms water/wet fines remain within the intended process envelope to the agreed practical standard;
- competent structural assessment completed;
- condition, remaining capacity and required repair/replacement scope defined;
- structural recommendations remain separate from unproven assumptions about the original cause of damage.

### Evidence / case traceability
Principal case: **Screens / Screen Chutes**.
Key evidence includes video `Screen-REF-016/-017`, photos `Screen-REF-004/-008` for water/corrosion context and `Screen-REF-006/-010` for support condition.

### Client / OEM / competent-person decisions
- final screen/chute modification design;
- structural adequacy / remaining capacity determination;
- repair/replacement method and priority;
- operational acceptance after modification.

## 2.8 Trip / restart and housekeeping governance

### Engineering objective
Separate event-driven product removal and unavoidable residual housekeeping from recurring source-generated spillage, and ensure material removed during trips/restarts is controlled, recovered and reintroduced without creating unmanaged secondary deposits.

### Action type
**Operational control / procedure verification + responsibility assignment + recovery/housekeeping governance.**

### Required inspection / verification
- obtain/confirm the current written trip/restart belt-unloading procedure;
- identify who authorises and performs unloading/recovery;
- document where removed product is placed temporarily;
- document how/when material is returned to the process;
- confirm housekeeping responsibilities and normal clearing frequency;
- distinguish chronic recurring deposits from event-driven deposits where practical.

### Design-development requirement
Normally procedural rather than mechanical. Defined temporary laydown/containment points may require local engineering/safety review if introduced.

### Implementation dependency
- South32 operational/risk ownership;
- existing procedures reviewed;
- safe temporary containment/recovery method agreed where required;
- responsibilities communicated to affected personnel.

### Success / acceptance criterion
- current trip/restart unloading and recovered-product procedure is documented and controlled;
- responsibilities are explicit;
- removed product is placed in an agreed controlled location/method rather than uncontrolled plant areas;
- recovery/reintroduction route is defined;
- housekeeping frequency/responsibility is defined;
- recurring source-generated spillage is tracked separately from event-driven deposits so housekeeping is not used as a substitute for source correction.

### Evidence / case traceability
Cross-system operational case. Sample Conveyor 3 records trip/restart clearing as applicable; broader procedure evidence remains source-recorded and requires confirmation against mine operating documents/practice.

### Client / competent-person decisions
- operating procedure content and approval;
- temporary laydown/recovery locations;
- role ownership and escalation criteria;
- housekeeping frequency and operational priorities.

# 3. Pass 7B Cross-Case Control Rules

The following rules apply to all recommendation wording:

1. **Do not state an unmeasured value as fact.** Unknown dimensions/tolerances remain verification tasks.
2. **Separate action classes:** immediate maintenance; inspection/measurement; design development; structural assessment; operational control; implementation.
3. **Do not promote inference to proof.** Especially preserve the current qualification boundaries for Sample 2, Sample 3, L3, U4, Screens and L1.
4. **Correct primary contributors before finalising secondary containment** where the evidence supports that sequence.
5. **Concept drawings are explanatory, not fabrication drawings.** Dimensions and final geometry must be verified.
6. **Acceptance must be functional.** A modification is not complete merely because it was installed; representative operating proof and close-out evidence are required.
7. **Keep client/OEM/competent-person authority explicit** where the audit cannot appropriately make the final design, structural or operational decision.
8. **Every material recommendation must trace to evidence or an explicitly qualified engineering need.**
