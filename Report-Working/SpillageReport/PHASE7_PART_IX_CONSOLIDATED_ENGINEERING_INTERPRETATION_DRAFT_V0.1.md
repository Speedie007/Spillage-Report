Parts IV to VIII deliberately separated the principal mechanisms so that each could be assessed against the correct evidence and uncertainty boundary. Part IX now recombines those findings at system level.

Its purpose is not to repeat the detailed case studies. It answers a different question:

> **What does the combined evidence mean when the inspected material-handling system is considered as one connected process?**

The interpretation retains three distinctions throughout:

1. **systemic / recurring mechanisms** versus **local / geometry-specific mechanisms**;
2. **source-control failures** versus **consequence-management activities**;
3. **proven conditions** versus **items that still require measurement, shutdown inspection, competent assessment or detailed design.**

# PART IX — WHAT THE COMBINED EVIDENCE MEANS

## 22. Consolidated Engineering Interpretation

The combined inspection and evidence review does not support a conclusion that spillage across the inspected Wessels Mine areas is caused by one isolated defect, one failed cleaner type or one common local maintenance issue.

The evidence instead supports a connected set of **material- and process-containment failures occurring through different mechanisms at different stages of the handling route**.

The significance of this conclusion is that one universal physical remedy would be technically inappropriate. The system requires a common control philosophy, but the physical intervention must respond to the mechanism and local geometry actually present at each location.

### 22.1 The dominant recurring system mechanism — fines carryback

Recurring fines accumulation is the strongest system-wide pattern identified through the audit.

The inspection record identifies carryback as a major recurring mechanism, supported by distributed fines accumulation beneath and alongside return-belt/support areas across multiple underground and surface conveyor sections.

The detailed Part IV review also established why the cleaner issue cannot be reduced to one hardware defect:

- **CV06** demonstrates loss of the cleaning function through a materially compromised cleaner condition;
- **CV09** demonstrates that material removal from the belt is still an incomplete control if the removed wet fines can escape the effective discharge containment.

Taken together, these conditions support a system-level conclusion:

> **Effective fines control requires both belt cleaning and containment of the removed material within the intended process stream.**

This conclusion is broader than either CV06 or CV09 individually, but it does not mean that the exact same defect exists at every conveyor. Cleaner condition, geometry, accessibility and material service differ by location and must be verified before local action is defined.

### 22.2 Coarse / mixed-product loss is a separate containment family

The audit also identified material-loss conditions that cannot be explained by return-side carryback.

At W10 and the Sample Conveyors, coarse/lump or mixed product has left the carrying envelope during transit. The correct engineering response is therefore concerned with preserving the **usable carrying envelope**, rather than only cleaning the return belt.

The Part V cases show that this carrying-envelope problem can arise through different combinations of:
- product size relative to available belt width;
- feed quantity and trajectory;
- belt position/tracking;
- transfer or local geometry;
- available lateral containment margin;
- and site-specific access/containment constraints.

Again, the visible consequence does not justify one universal causal statement.

- **W10** proves significant coarse/mixed-product loss into a restricted tunnel, while the initiating contributors remain to be verified.
- **Sample Conveyor 2** proves a loading/product-size/containment concern, while significant mistracking remains unconfirmed.
- **Sample Conveyor 3** proves a similar loading/containment concern and additionally supports lateral mistracking/offset, although the magnitude and root mechanical cause remain unmeasured.

The combined interpretation is therefore that coarse-product containment must follow a hierarchy of **feed and geometry verification, tracking correction where applicable, and secondary containment only where a residual containment duty remains.**

### 22.3 Tracking is a real material-loss mechanism where the evidence chain supports it

Part VI deliberately contrasted U4 and L3 to establish how tracking/alignment findings should be interpreted.

At **U4**, visually supported lateral mistracking occurs together with a directional one-sided coarse/lump-product accumulation pattern. That combination supports a local tracking-to-product-loss relationship, while the tail-pulley/support geometry and exact alignment magnitude remain to be measured.

At **L3**, a tail-end offset/alignment concern is supported, but the current evidence does not establish a comparable causal chain to significant solid-product loss.

This contrast is important to the consolidated assessment because it demonstrates that the audit has not classified every alignment concern as a proven spillage cause.

The correct interpretation is:

> **Tracking becomes a material-containment conclusion only where the evidence supports a relationship between belt position and loss of carrying margin or material.**

Where that relationship is not established, alignment remains a genuine maintenance/verification requirement without being promoted to a stronger causal category.

### 22.4 Water introduces a separate process-containment and transport mechanism

The Screens provide the strongest direct evidence of process-water loss of containment.

Dynamic video confirms active water spray/runoff leaving the screen/chute operating envelope. This can mobilise fines, spread wet material away from the point of escape and repeatedly wet surrounding plant areas and structures.

The exact equipment-level escape mechanism remains unresolved and must be established through safe shutdown inspection before detailed containment modification.

The same area also contains severe corrosion/deterioration and physically cut/notched support members. These conditions are directly observed, but the audit does not claim that the active water escape explains the full history of every structural defect or that the reason for the cut-outs is known.

The consolidated interpretation therefore separates:

1. **verified process-water loss of containment requiring mechanism identification and correction;** and
2. **verified structural damage/deterioration requiring competent structural assessment.**

They occur in the same engineering environment and both require action, but they remain separate workstreams until further investigation establishes more.

### 22.5 L1 shows why consequence, interaction and structural condition must remain distinct

L1 combines several significant conditions in one physical area:
- extensive product/fines accumulation;
- wear geometry consistent with previous/intermittent belt-to-structure contact;
- severe concrete deterioration/spalling with exposed reinforcement;
- severe local steel deterioration/section loss.

The running belt is clear of the affected supports in the captured operating view. The report therefore does not claim continuous present contact.

Likewise, while fines and moisture can contribute to an aggressive deterioration environment, the structural capacity of the affected concrete and steel cannot be inferred from the visual record.

L1 consequently requires two linked but distinct responses:
- verify representative belt tracking/clearance and address any active mechanical contributor;
- obtain competent structural assessment and repair/replacement definition.

### 22.6 Event-driven material handling is not the same as chronic spillage

Trip/restart preparation and authorised manual product removal introduce another important distinction.

Material deliberately removed from a loaded belt during a controlled intervention may produce a deposit that visually resembles ordinary spillage, but the initiating mechanism is operational rather than an uncontrolled failure during normal conveying.

The appropriate control is therefore procedural: defined responsibility, controlled temporary placement, containment, recovery and reintroduction.

This does not remove the material from the audit. It ensures that event-driven deposits are not incorrectly used as evidence of a chronic local mechanical defect.

### 22.7 Housekeeping remains necessary, but it is consequence control

Across the inspected system, housekeeping is necessary to restore access, recover product and maintain workable operating areas.

However, repeated accumulation at the same source-generated locations demonstrates why cleaning cannot be treated as the principal long-term control.

The engineering distinction is:

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence. Source control should reduce the recurrence.**

Housekeeping records can also become useful performance feedback. If an engineering correction is effective, the frequency and quantity of recurring accumulation should reduce under comparable operating conditions.

## 22.8 Systemic Versus Local Mechanism Hierarchy

The combined evidence is most clearly understood through the following hierarchy.

### Systemic / recurring themes

**Recurring fines carryback**
- repeated across multiple conveyor sections;
- variable visible severity by location;
- requires improved source control at applicable discharges and cleaner locations.

**Variable cleaner performance / condition**
- CV06 proves direct mechanical/maintenance loss of cleaning function;
- CV09 proves that cleaning and removed-material containment must be considered together;
- similarity at inaccessible or unverified locations remains an engineering inference pending local inspection.

**Recurring consequence-management burden**
- housekeeping/recovery is repeatedly required where source loss continues;
- consequence management alone does not demonstrate that the initiating mechanism is controlled.

### Local / geometry-specific mechanisms and conditions

**W10**
- coarse/mixed-product loss into a restricted tunnel;
- initiating contributors require verification;
- secondary barrier/rail remains a feasibility concept.

**Sample Conveyor 2**
- product-size/feed/limited-width containment issue established;
- significant mistracking not established.

**Sample Conveyor 3**
- product-size/feed/limited-width containment issue established;
- lateral offset/mistracking additionally supported but unmeasured.

**U4**
- mistracking and one-sided product-loss relationship supported;
- alignment geometry/magnitude requires quantitative verification.

**L3**
- alignment/offset concern supported;
- significant solid-product causal chain not established.

**Screens**
- active process-water escape verified;
- exact escape mechanism unresolved;
- severe structural deterioration and cut/notched supports verified separately.

**L1**
- extensive accumulation, previous/intermittent belt contact and serious structural deterioration established;
- continuous present contact and remaining structural capacity not established.

**Trip/restart handling**
- separate event-driven operational mechanism requiring controlled procedure and recovery rather than being merged into chronic spillage causation.

## 22.9 The Higher-Order Engineering Conclusion

Taken together, the evidence supports a conclusion that is broader than any individual asset finding:

> **The inspected system has a material- and process-containment problem with several mechanisms, not one universal spillage defect.**

The recurring system problem is most evident in fines/carryback and the resulting housekeeping burden. Local geometry, feed, tracking, water-containment and structural conditions then increase or change the consequence at specific assets.

The correct response is therefore neither:
- one standard scraper everywhere;
- one standard side barrier everywhere;
- nor increased housekeeping alone.

The response must instead apply one common engineering objective through mechanism-specific controls.

That objective is developed in Part X.

### Part IX reader takeaway

> **One process, several failure mechanisms, one governing containment objective — with the physical solution selected according to evidence, local geometry and verified operating condition.**
