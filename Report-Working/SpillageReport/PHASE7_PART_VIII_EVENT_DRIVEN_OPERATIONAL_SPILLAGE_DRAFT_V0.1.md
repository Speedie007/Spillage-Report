# South32 Wessels Combined Product Spillage Audit — Phase 7 Part VIII Draft V0.1

## Status
**REPORT-READY CONTENT DRAFT — PASS 7A / 7B ALIGNMENT**

## Purpose
This draft develops **PART VIII — EVENT-DRIVEN AND OPERATIONAL SPILLAGE** within the inspector-approved V0.2 report architecture.

The preceding report sections deal primarily with recurring or condition-driven loss mechanisms. Part VIII establishes an important boundary:

> **not every material deposit beside a conveyor is evidence of a chronic mechanical or containment failure at that location.**

Where material is intentionally removed from a loaded conveyor to support a controlled trip/restart or other authorised operating intervention, the resulting deposit is an **event-driven material-handling condition**. It still requires controlled placement, recovery and accountability, but its corrective mechanism is primarily procedural rather than the same source-control response applied to chronic carryback, mistracking or process-water escape.

Housekeeping is addressed separately as an essential consequence-control activity that should also provide feedback on recurring source conditions.

# PART VIII — EVENT-DRIVEN AND OPERATIONAL SPILLAGE

## 20. Trip / Restart Product Handling — A Separate Event-Driven Mechanism

A conveyor can remain loaded when an operating interruption occurs. Where material must be removed from the belt or local process area before safe restart or recovery, that product may temporarily leave the normal material-handling envelope by deliberate intervention rather than by uncontrolled mechanical escape.

This distinction matters to the audit because the final deposit may resemble ordinary spillage when viewed after the event.

The engineering interpretation should therefore ask:
- was the material lost unintentionally during normal conveying?
- or was it deliberately removed as part of an authorised operating/restart activity?

The answer changes the appropriate control.

### 20.1 Event-driven material remains part of the product-control problem

Classifying a deposit as event-driven does not make the material irrelevant to the audit.

Removed product still needs:
- a controlled temporary location;
- suitable containment so that it does not obstruct access or spread into surrounding areas;
- defined responsibility;
- a recovery/reintroduction route;
- and a clear distinction from chronic source-generated spillage.

The objective is not to eliminate all necessary intervention-related handling. It is to prevent a necessary operating activity from creating unmanaged secondary deposits.

### 20.2 Procedure verification rather than assumed practice

The report should not invent a trip/restart procedure or presume that the same local practice applies throughout the mine.

The required next step is to confirm the actual South32 procedure and operating practice, including:
- the current written trip/restart or belt-unloading instruction, where applicable;
- who is authorised/responsible for product removal;
- where removed material may be placed temporarily;
- how the temporary placement is contained or controlled;
- how and when the material is recovered and returned to the process;
- what conditions must be met before restart;
- and how event-driven deposits are distinguished from recurring mechanically generated spillage for inspection/trending purposes.

Any site-specific safety, isolation, restart or recovered-product requirements remain governed by South32 procedures and authorised operational controls.

### 20.3 Temporary placement should be deliberate, not ad hoc

Where local operating conditions require product to be removed before restart, the preferred control philosophy is a defined temporary containment or laydown arrangement rather than uncontrolled placement at the nearest convenient location.

The appropriate physical arrangement may vary by location and must be compatible with:
- safe access and escape routes;
- equipment clearances;
- manual/mechanical recovery method;
- quantity and size of material normally handled during the event;
- local housekeeping capability;
- and the required route for returning product to the process.

The report does not prescribe one standard container, bin or laydown design without site verification.

### 20.4 Functional close-out for trip/restart handling

The operational-control item should be considered satisfactorily addressed when:
- the applicable procedure is documented/confirmed;
- authorised responsibilities are explicit;
- temporary product placement is controlled rather than ad hoc;
- the recovery/reintroduction route is defined;
- access and operating safety are maintained;
- and event-driven deposits can be distinguished from chronic source-generated spillage.

### Trip/restart reader takeaway

> **Event-driven product removal may be necessary, but the material should remain controlled throughout removal, temporary placement and recovery so that an operating intervention does not become unmanaged secondary spillage.**

## 21. Housekeeping and Product Recovery — Necessary Consequence Control, Not Source Correction

Housekeeping remains necessary across the inspected system.

Accumulated product can:
- obstruct walkways and restricted access routes;
- reduce visibility of equipment condition;
- hinder inspection and maintenance;
- retain moisture against structures;
- create repeated manual-recovery work;
- and obscure whether an engineering correction is actually reducing recurrence.

The report therefore does not treat housekeeping as unimportant. The distinction is that housekeeping generally addresses **material already outside the intended process envelope**.

### 21.1 The key distinction

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence. Source control should reduce the recurrence.**

This principle prevents two opposite errors.

The first error would be to treat repeated cleaning as sufficient close-out while the initiating material-loss mechanism remains active.

The second would be to delay necessary cleaning on the basis that an engineering modification is planned. Existing accumulations still require safe management while source-control work is being developed and verified.

Both controls are therefore necessary, but they perform different functions.

### 21.2 Housekeeping as feedback on source-control performance

Where recurring accumulation is significant, housekeeping records can become useful engineering feedback.

A location that requires frequent repeat clearing after a corrective action may indicate that:
- the source mechanism has not been fully corrected;
- the operating condition used for verification was not representative;
- the intervention has degraded or moved out of adjustment;
- or an additional mechanism remains active.

Conversely, a sustained reduction in repeat accumulation can support the functional close-out evidence for a source-control intervention.

The report does not prescribe a universal clearing interval because operating conditions and local consequence vary by location. Frequency should be defined by South32 according to operating condition, access/safety needs and the role of the record in monitoring repeat accumulation.

### 21.3 Restricted-access areas require particular attention

W10 demonstrates why housekeeping consequence can vary by location.

Product accumulated in a restricted tunnel consumes limited access space and increases the burden of manual recovery. Until source-control and any justified secondary containment are implemented and proven, maintaining the access route remains an important operational requirement.

The same logic applies anywhere accumulated material interferes materially with safe access, inspection or maintenance.

### 21.4 Housekeeping / recovery governance

The recommended governance elements are:
- defined responsibility for inspection and clearing;
- clearing/recovery frequency appropriate to the local operating condition;
- controlled recovery and product disposition/reintroduction where applicable;
- retention of simple records where repeat accumulation is material to trend assessment;
- differentiation between event-driven deposits and chronic operating loss;
- escalation where recurring accumulation indicates source-control performance is inadequate.

### 21.5 Functional close-out

Housekeeping governance should provide confidence that:
- access and working areas are maintained to the accepted condition;
- recovered material is handled through a controlled route;
- repeat accumulation can be recognised rather than repeatedly normalised;
- event-driven and chronic source-generated deposits are distinguishable;
- and housekeeping information feeds back into engineering/maintenance review where recurrence persists.

## 21.6 Part VIII — Operating-Control Hierarchy

Part VIII can be summarised through the following hierarchy:

| Situation | Primary control | Supporting control | Close-out evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product deliberately removed during trip/restart preparation | Approved operational procedure and controlled temporary placement/recovery | Housekeeping / product recovery | Procedure, responsibility and recovery route confirmed |
| Chronic carryback / containment loss | Correct initiating material-loss mechanism | Housekeeping during interim and after unavoidable residual events | Representative operating proof + reduced recurrence |
| Accumulated material obstructing access | Safe clearing / recovery | Source investigation where recurrence is present | Access restored + recurrence tracked |
| Repeat accumulation after an engineering intervention | Reassess source-control performance | Maintain safe housekeeping | Trend / inspection evidence identifies whether action is effective |

The table reinforces that the same visible consequence — material outside the process envelope — can arise through different routes and therefore requires different primary controls.

## 21.7 Transition to Part IX — Bringing the Mechanisms Back Together

Parts IV to VIII have now separated the principal loss mechanisms so that they can be understood on their own terms:

- **fines/carryback and cleaner containment**;
- **coarse/mixed-product loss during transit**;
- **tracking/alignment-related loss of carrying margin**;
- **process-water escape and wet-fines transport**;
- **structural conditions requiring dedicated assessment**;
- **event-driven operational product removal**;
- **housekeeping and recovery as consequence controls**.

The next step is to bring those mechanisms back together without flattening their differences.

Part IX therefore consolidates the evidence into the report's system-level interpretation:

> which conditions are recurring across the material-handling ecosystem, which are local exceptions, and what common engineering principle connects the different physical solutions?
