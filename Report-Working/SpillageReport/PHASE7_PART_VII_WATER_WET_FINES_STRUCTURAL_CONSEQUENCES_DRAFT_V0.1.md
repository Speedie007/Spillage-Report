## Purpose
This draft develops **PART VII — WATER, WET FINES AND STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCES** within the inspector-approved V0.2 report architecture.

Parts IV to VI addressed fines/carryback, coarse-product containment and tracking/alignment. Part VII introduces a different process-containment failure:

> **process water leaving its intended operating envelope, mobilising fines and repeatedly wetting surrounding plant areas and structures.**

The section deliberately separates four evidentiary questions:

1. is water escape directly observed?
2. is structural deterioration directly observed?
3. is physical alteration/damage to structural members directly observed?
4. does the available evidence establish why those structural conditions developed or what capacity remains?

At the Screens, the first three are directly supported; the fourth requires further investigation and competent structural assessment.

At L1, the report likewise separates:
- extensive material accumulation;
- previous/intermittent belt-to-structure contact;
- concrete deterioration;
- steel deterioration;
- and the unresolved dynamic/mechanical and structural questions that require verification.

No remaining structural capacity, exact corrosion rate, exact water-origin mechanism or continuous current belt-contact state is inferred from photographs.

# PART VII — WATER, WET FINES AND STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCES

## 17. Water Changes Both the Transport Mechanism and the Consequence

Water introduces a different form of process-containment problem from the dry carryback and coarse-product mechanisms discussed earlier in the report.

When process or other water escapes the intended equipment envelope, it can:
- combine with fines and create wet fines/slurry;
- transport fine material away from the original escape point;
- spread contamination through lower structural levels and access areas;
- increase the persistence of adhered deposits;
- increase housekeeping and access burden;
- and repeatedly wet structural steel, concrete and interfaces.

This means a wet deposit does not automatically identify its own source. Water and entrained fines can move away from the point at which containment was first lost, reinforcing the report-wide principle that **deposit location is not automatically cause location**.

The audit must also avoid the opposite error: not every wet deposit across the inspected plant should be assigned to one common process-water source. Water may be present for different reasons at different locations, including process operation, wash-down, maintenance activity or other local sources.

The Screens are the strongest direct case because the dynamic evidence shows active process-water escape during operation. The exact equipment-level escape mechanism, however, was not safely resolved during the inspection.

### Part VII water-control principle

> **Where process water is visibly escaping, first identify the actual escape path and mechanism; then develop the containment correction around the verified mechanism rather than an assumed cause.**

## 18. Screens — Confirmed Water Loss of Containment and Separate Structural Concerns

The Screens area contains two important engineering stories that occur in the same physical environment but must remain analytically separate:

1. **active process-water loss of containment**; and
2. **structural deterioration and physically cut/notched support members**.

The proximity of these conditions makes them relevant to one another, but proximity alone does not establish the complete causal history of the structural defects.

### 18.1 Active water escape is directly verified

The screen/chute videos provide direct dynamic evidence of water leaving the intended operating envelope.

`Screen-REF-016` shows active spray/droplets escaping from the underside/side of the operating screen/chute region into the surrounding plant area.

`Screen-REF-017` provides a wider operating view showing substantial water spray/runoff around the screen/chute structure and wetting surrounding steelwork and areas.

These videos are important because they establish that the condition was **active during operation**, rather than only inferred from historical wet staining or accumulated slurry.

The evidence therefore supports the statement:

> **process-water loss of containment at the Screens is verified.**

### 18.2 The exact equipment-level escape mechanism remains unresolved

The dynamic evidence does not isolate one specific internal component or geometry defect as the cause of the escape.

The current record does not establish whether the dominant mechanism is associated with:
- a seal/interface condition;
- chute geometry;
- screen movement or operating clearance;
- spray direction;
- an opening/gap in the local containment envelope;
- a maintenance/condition issue;
- or another equipment-level mechanism.

The report should therefore not design the corrective modification from assumption alone.

The next engineering step is a **safe-shutdown mechanism investigation** correlated against the existing operating video evidence. That inspection should identify the actual escape path, relevant local geometry and the containment interface requiring modification.

### 18.3 Water-containment action sequence

The recommended sequence is:

1. complete a close inspection under safe shutdown/access conditions;
2. correlate the static shutdown geometry with `Screen-REF-016/-017` and any other representative operating evidence;
3. identify the actual water escape path(s);
4. inspect relevant seals, chute interfaces, screen movement/clearance and surrounding enclosure geometry;
5. record the dimensions/geometry required for design;
6. develop the containment modification against the verified mechanism;
7. implement the approved modification;
8. prove performance under representative operation.

### 18.4 Functional close-out requirement

The Screens water action should not be considered closed simply because a plate, seal, deflector or chute modification has been installed.

Close-out should demonstrate that:
- the actual water escape mechanism was identified;
- the installed modification addresses that verified mechanism;
- representative operation shows materially improved retention of process water and entrained fines within the intended screening/chute process envelope;
- and the change has not created an unacceptable new access, maintenance or operating conflict.

The final acceptable operating condition remains a South32 / responsible-engineer decision.

### 18.5 Severe structural deterioration is directly verified

The Screens photographic set separately verifies significant corrosion and deterioration of surrounding support/deck structural elements.

Representative evidence includes:
- `Screen-REF-004` — severe corrosion and local deterioration at the screen support/deck interface;
- `Screen-REF-008` — extensive corrosion and deterioration over a broader length of the support interface;
- other supporting views showing the wider wet/corrosive environment.

These photographs establish the physical condition. They do not determine the remaining load-carrying capacity of the affected steelwork.

Repeated wetting is relevant to the observed corrosive environment, but the report should not state that the currently observed process-water escape is proven to be the sole cause of every area of corrosion or deterioration. The complete exposure and maintenance history has not been established.

### 18.6 Cut / notched support members are a separate structural finding

The evidence also directly verifies physical cut-outs/notches in support members around the screen installation.

Key examples include:
- `Screen-REF-006` — multiple cut-outs/notches together with corrosion;
- `Screen-REF-010` — substantial opening/cut-out through a support member.

The photographs establish that the geometry of the members has been physically altered.

They do **not** establish:
- why the cuts were made;
- when they were made;
- whether they formed part of an approved modification;
- the original design intent or load path;
- or the remaining structural capacity.

Any working suggestion that the members may have been altered to facilitate installation, removal or maintenance of screen equipment must therefore remain explicitly unverified unless supporting records are produced.

### 18.7 Structural assessment must be independent of the assumed history

The structural question is not whether a plausible reason can be proposed for the cuts. The engineering question is whether the current altered/corroded structure remains adequate for its required duty and, if not, what controlled repair or replacement is required.

The recommended structural sequence is:

1. appoint a competent structural authority;
2. inspect and record the affected members, connections and surrounding load path;
3. measure relevant member dimensions, cut/notch geometry and section loss as required;
4. establish current structural status / remaining capacity;
5. define repair, replacement or operating restriction as required;
6. formally design and approve any revised geometry where future installation/maintenance clearance is required;
7. document the completed structural intervention and acceptance.

### 18.8 Screens evidence and action summary

| Question | Evidence position | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Is water escaping during operation? | **Yes — directly verified by video** | Identify exact escape path, then correct containment |
| Is the exact equipment-level cause known? | **No** | Safe-shutdown investigation + geometry verification |
| Is severe corrosion/deterioration present? | **Yes — directly verified** | Competent structural assessment |
| Are support members physically cut/notched? | **Yes — directly verified** | Record geometry and assess structural adequacy |
| Is the reason for the cuts known? | **No** | Review records if available; do not infer motive as fact |
| Is remaining structural capacity known from the photographs? | **No** | Engineering measurement / calculation required |

### Screens reader takeaway

> **Water loss of containment is proven; structural deterioration and physical modification are proven; the detailed water mechanism, structural history and remaining capacity are not. These must be resolved by targeted inspection and competent engineering rather than assumption.**

## 19. L1 — Accumulation, Previous Belt Contact and Structural Deterioration

L1 contains several significant findings within the same tunnel/conveyor environment. As with the Screens, the report should connect the conditions without collapsing them into one unsupported causal chain.

The evidence directly supports:

1. extensive fines and coarse/lump-product accumulation;
2. wear slots/notches in angular supports consistent with previous/intermittent belt contact;
3. severe concrete deterioration/spalling with exposed reinforcement;
4. severe local steel corrosion/section loss and perforation.

These conditions require both mechanical and structural follow-up.

### 19.1 Extensive material accumulation

The L1 evidence directly confirms extensive product and fines accumulation throughout the area.

`LoadoutStationL1-REF-013` is a strong overall consequence image, showing a large area of accumulated lump product and fines across the conveyor/access zone. `LoadoutStationL1-REF-017` provides useful supporting context that accumulation is also present on/within surrounding structural surfaces rather than being limited to one floor pile.

This establishes a substantial material-containment and housekeeping burden at L1. It does not, on its own, establish that every deposit arose from one mechanism.

### 19.2 Previous / intermittent belt-to-structure contact is supported

`LoadoutStationL1-REF-007` is the principal evidence for the belt-interaction condition.

The photograph shows:
- the operating belt passing between angular steel supports;
- smooth localised wear slots/notches in the supports adjacent to the belt path;
- and the belt running clear of the supports at the instant captured.

The evidence therefore supports the carefully qualified conclusion:

> **previous/intermittent belt-to-structure contact has occurred, but continuous present contact was not demonstrated in the captured operating condition.**

This distinction must remain explicit throughout the report.

Belt mistracking is a plausible and inspection-supported mechanism for the previous contact, but the still photograph does not establish the complete dynamic history or current tracking envelope. Representative operating verification is therefore required.

### 19.3 Concrete deterioration is independently significant

`LoadoutStationL1-REF-003` and `LoadoutStationL1-REF-005` directly show severe concrete deterioration/spalling with exposed reinforcement and corrosion staining.

The photographs establish the physical structural condition. They do not establish the remaining capacity of the affected concrete/reinforcement or define the required repair method.

This must therefore be treated as a structural engineering item in its own right rather than merely as a housekeeping observation.

### 19.4 Steel deterioration is independently significant

`LoadoutStationL1-REF-008` and supporting views directly show severe local steel corrosion, section loss and perforation.

Again, the visual evidence establishes deterioration but cannot quantify remaining structural capacity.

The presence of recurring material and moisture around the structure is relevant to the local deterioration environment, but the report should not assign a quantified corrosion contribution or one exclusive moisture source without supporting investigation.

### 19.5 L1 requires a combined mechanical and structural response

The L1 corrective programme should proceed on two coordinated but separately accountable tracks.

#### Mechanical / tracking and clearance verification

- record the existing wear-slot/contact geometry;
- establish suitable fixed references where required;
- inspect relevant belt, support and alignment condition;
- verify belt tracking and clearance under representative operation;
- establish whether present operation maintains suitable clearance through the relevant operating condition;
- retain the distinction between historic/intermittent evidence and any newly verified current contact.

#### Structural assessment and repair

- competent structural inspection of affected concrete, reinforcement, steel members and connections;
- record relevant dimensions, section loss and deterioration geometry;
- establish current structural status / remaining capacity;
- define repair or replacement scope;
- implement the approved repair under the competent authority;
- control recurring fines/moisture accumulation around repaired interfaces as part of the local deterioration-control strategy.

### 19.6 L1 functional close-out

L1 should not be closed merely by cleaning the area or repairing visible damage.

Close-out should demonstrate:
- competent structural assessment completed;
- repair/replacement requirement defined and completed or formally controlled;
- representative belt tracking/clearance verified at the affected supports;
- no unsupported statement of continuous current belt contact introduced;
- recurring accumulation and moisture exposure around repaired interfaces materially reduced or controlled;
- as-left condition recorded sufficiently for future inspection/trending.

### 19.7 L1 evidence and action summary

| Finding | Evidence position | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Extensive product/fines accumulation | **Directly verified** | Source-control + recovery/housekeeping + local mechanism review |
| Previous/intermittent belt contact | **Supported by wear slots and local geometry** | Representative tracking/clearance verification |
| Continuous current belt contact | **Not proven** | Do not claim unless dynamically verified |
| Concrete spalling / exposed reinforcement | **Directly verified** | Competent structural assessment and repair definition |
| Steel corrosion / section loss | **Directly verified** | Competent structural assessment and repair definition |
| Remaining structural capacity | **Not established by photographs** | Measurement / structural calculation required |

### L1 reader takeaway

> **L1 is not one defect. It is a location where significant accumulation, evidence of previous belt interaction and serious structural deterioration coexist. Each requires its own verification and close-out path, while the combined local environment must be improved to reduce recurrence and further deterioration.**

## 19.8 Transition to Part VIII — Not Every Deposit Is a Chronic Mechanical Failure

Parts IV to VII have developed recurring or condition-driven material-loss mechanisms:
- carryback and cleaner/containment performance;
- coarse-product loss from the carrying envelope;
- belt tracking/alignment;
- water/process containment;
- and structural consequences requiring dedicated assessment.

The audit also records another category of deposit that must not be confused with these chronic mechanisms: **material deliberately removed from a loaded conveyor during trip/restart preparation or other controlled operating intervention.**

The resulting material still requires containment and recovery, but its source and control are different.

Part VIII therefore separates **event-driven operational spillage** and **housekeeping/recovery** from the chronic source-control mechanisms established in the preceding sections.
