## Purpose
This draft develops **PART VI — ALIGNMENT AND LOCAL PRODUCT-LOSS MECHANISMS** within the inspector-approved V0.2 report architecture.

Part V established that coarse/mixed-product containment depends partly on preserving the usable carrying envelope. Part VI now isolates one mechanism that can reduce that envelope:

> **belt tracking / alignment.**

The section deliberately contrasts two cases with different evidence strength:

1. **U4 Tripper Car** — lateral mistracking and one-sided product loss are visually supported strongly enough to establish a local material-loss relationship, while the magnitude and detailed source geometry still require measurement;
2. **L3** — a tail-end offset/alignment concern is visually supported, but the current evidence does not establish a comparable causal chain to significant solid/lump-product loss.

The purpose of the contrast is not to downgrade L3. It is to demonstrate a key audit principle:

> **similar-looking mechanical conditions do not automatically justify the same causal conclusion.**

No unmeasured alignment value, tracking percentage, belt-offset dimension or OEM tolerance is introduced as fact.

# PART VI — ALIGNMENT AND LOCAL PRODUCT-LOSS MECHANISMS

## 14. Belt Tracking Is a Material-Containment Requirement

Part V established that coarse or mixed product remains contained only while the belt, loading condition and local geometry collectively preserve an adequate carrying margin. Belt tracking is therefore more than a mechanical-maintenance condition. It is also a material-containment control.

A conveyor belt can only use its intended carrying width when it remains suitably positioned relative to the idlers, pulleys, structure and any surrounding containment system. Where the belt moves laterally, the available margin on one side reduces. Depending on the loading condition and local geometry, product can then approach or cross the edge of the usable carrying envelope.

The consequence is not identical at every conveyor. Lateral offset may:
- reduce usable carrying width;
- bring product closer to the belt edge;
- increase interaction with local skirts, guides or surrounding structure;
- create one-sided product loss where the belt is loaded heavily enough;
- create abnormal belt-to-structure interaction;
- or remain primarily a mechanical/alignment concern without a proven solid-product loss mechanism.

For this reason, the report does not treat the presence of an offset belt as automatic proof of spillage causation. The engineering question is instead:

> **What does the available evidence establish about the belt position, the material distribution and the resulting containment consequence at that specific location?**

This distinction is demonstrated clearly by U4 and L3.

### 14.1 Relationship to the Sample Conveyor 3 case

Sample Conveyor 3, discussed in Part V, already demonstrates why belt position matters to carrying margin. The photographs support lateral offset/mistracking in addition to the narrow-belt loading/containment concern, but the magnitude and root mechanical cause remain unmeasured.

Part VI does not repeat that full case. Instead, Sample Conveyor 3 acts as the transition from the general coarse-product containment problem into two alignment-focused examples:
- U4, where the evidence supports a stronger tracking-to-one-sided-loss relationship;
- L3, where the evidence supports offset but not an equivalent solid-product causal chain.

### Part VI alignment principle

> **Tracking is relevant to containment when it materially changes the usable carrying margin or creates interaction with surrounding equipment; the strength of the causal conclusion must match the strength of the evidence.**

## 15. U4 Tripper Car — Strong Local Tracking / Product-Loss Case

### 15.1 What the evidence establishes

U4 provides one of the strongest local examples in the audit of a tracking condition associated with a directional product-loss consequence.

The controlled evidence directly or strongly supports three related conditions:

1. **lateral belt mistracking / offset** relative to the carrying-idler/support geometry;
2. **coarse/lump-product accumulation concentrated predominantly along one side** of the conveyor/tripper area;
3. **substantial fines accumulation** around the tripper structure.

`UGU4-REF-009` is the preferred still for the tracking condition. The belt is visibly displaced laterally relative to the carrying-idler/support geometry. The image does not provide a dimensional reference and must therefore not be used to assign a percentage or millimetre offset.

`UGU4-REF-007` provides the strongest selected consequence image. Lump/solid product is accumulated predominantly along one side of the conveyor/tripper area. The still shows the accumulated consequence rather than the instant of material falling, but when read together with the verified mistracking condition it provides strong evidence for a local one-sided material-loss relationship.

`UGU4-REF-006` can be used where an additional severity/context image is required to demonstrate the associated fines accumulation.

### 15.2 What remains unmeasured

The inspection record identified a tail-pulley alignment concern. `UGU4-REF-010` provides operating context and visually supports an asymmetric belt/pulley condition, but the available media does not contain the fixed survey references required to quantify pulley alignment relative to the tripper structure.

The report must therefore maintain the distinction:

> **U4 belt mistracking and one-sided product loss — visually supported / verified.**

versus:

> **tail-pulley / supporting alignment geometry and magnitude — requires quantitative verification.**

The evidence also does not establish the complete reason for the alignment condition. Potential contributors such as pulley/support geometry, adjustment condition, trapped material or other mechanical factors should remain inspection targets until measured and verified.

### 15.3 Why U4 is stronger than a generic “misalignment” finding

The significance of U4 is the combination of evidence, not the presence of an offset belt alone.

The case contains:
- a clearly supported lateral belt-position problem;
- a directional product-loss pattern on the corresponding local route;
- substantial accumulated material;
- and a moving tripper configuration in which the tracking condition must remain acceptable over an operating range rather than at one fixed position.

This makes U4 a suitable example of how a mechanical geometry condition can reduce the practical carrying margin and contribute to local product loss.

It does **not** mean that every conveyor showing some visual offset should automatically receive the same causal classification. L3 demonstrates why that distinction matters.

### 15.4 U4 requires dynamic verification, not a static adjustment alone

Because U4 is a tripper-car installation, a satisfactory static belt position at one location is not sufficient close-out evidence.

The corrective process should establish:
- fixed and repeatable geometry reference points;
- as-found pulley/support/belt-path geometry;
- the actual mechanical or structural source of any confirmed error;
- the available adjustment provisions;
- the effect of trapped accumulated material on the tracking assessment;
- and the belt position under representative operation through the **full tripper travel range**.

The objective is not simply to make one pulley or one belt view appear centred. The objective is to restore a stable operating path that preserves adequate carrying margin throughout the relevant tripper movement.

### 15.5 U4 cleaner arrangement remains a separate local issue

U4 also contains a distinct/custom transverse cleaner/scraper arrangement, directly supported by `UGU4-REF-011` and `UGU4-REF-012`.

This is materially different from the conventional cleaner/discharge arrangements used to develop the CV09 pilot philosophy. The U4 tracking/alignment correction should therefore not be used as a reason to copy the CV09 cleaner hardware into U4 without a separate geometry and functional assessment.

This preserves the report-wide rule:

> **standardise the required material-control function, not identical hardware.**

### 15.6 U4 engineering response

The required response is a controlled alignment/tracking correction rather than an assumed component replacement.

The action sequence should be:

1. establish fixed/repeatable survey references;
2. measure the relevant tail-pulley, support and belt-path geometry in the as-found condition;
3. identify the mechanical or structural source of any confirmed error;
4. remove trapped material that could influence tracking or measurement;
5. correct the confirmed contributor using the appropriate adjustment/repair method;
6. verify centred/stable tracking under representative operation through the full tripper travel range;
7. confirm adequate side carrying margin at critical operating positions;
8. retain the as-left geometry/reference data as the future maintenance baseline;
9. assess the U4 cleaner/containment configuration separately.

### 15.7 Functional close-out

U4 should only be regarded as satisfactorily closed when the operating evidence demonstrates that:
- the relevant geometry has been measured and corrected as required;
- tracking remains stable through representative operating conditions and full tripper travel;
- adequate carrying margin/clearance is maintained;
- the previously observed one-sided product-loss condition is materially reduced or removed;
- as-left geometry/reference points are recorded for future trending;
- and any cleaner-related issue has been addressed through its own local assessment.

The final acceptable tracking/alignment tolerance remains a South32 / responsible conveyor-engineering decision and should not be invented by the audit report.

### U4 reader takeaway

> **U4 provides a strong local evidence chain: mistracking is supported, the one-sided product-loss consequence is supported, and quantitative geometry is the next step required to convert the finding into controlled correction and operating proof.**

## 16. L3 — Alignment Concern Without a Proven Solid-Product Causal Chain

### 16.1 What the evidence establishes

L3 presents a deliberately different evidence position.

The photographic record supports a lateral/asymmetric condition at the tail-end belt/pulley area. `LoadoutStationL3-REF-001` is the strongest image for this finding and is consistent with the inspector-recorded tail-end offset.

The evidence also directly shows fines/material accumulation at L3, including:
- fine-material contamination on side guarding and adjacent structure (`LoadoutStationL3-REF-007`);
- fines/small fragments accumulated on lower surfaces beneath the conveyor area (`LoadoutStationL3-REF-008`).

These conditions justify retaining L3 as an alignment/tracking investigation item and as a local fines/carryback finding.

### 16.2 What the evidence does not establish

Unlike U4, the current L3 evidence does **not** show a comparable directional accumulation of significant coarse/lump product associated with the offset condition.

The record therefore does not establish that the visually supported tail-end offset is directly causing significant solid-product spillage.

It also does not provide a reliable dimensional basis for any earlier approximate offset description. Perspective, camera position and the absence of fixed references prevent a defensible percentage or millimetre value being assigned from the photograph.

Accordingly, the technically controlled statement is:

> **L3 tail-end offset/alignment concern — visually supported and requires quantitative verification.**

It should **not** be promoted to:

> **L3 offset proven to be causing significant solid-product loss.**

That causal conclusion is not supported by the current evidence.

### 16.3 Why the distinction from U4 matters

U4 and L3 are useful when read together because they demonstrate how the audit treats evidence strength.

| Evidence question | U4 | L3 |
|---|---|---|
| Lateral belt/geometry concern supported? | Yes | Yes |
| Quantitative alignment magnitude established? | No | No |
| Significant coarse/lump-product loss visible? | Yes, one-sided local accumulation strongly supported | No comparable solid-product causal pattern established |
| Tracking-to-product-loss relationship | Strongly supported locally | Not established from current record |
| Required response | Quantify, correct and prove full-travel operation | Quantify, inspect/correct as required and verify operating effect |

This comparison is important because the report should not use visual similarity as a substitute for causal evidence.

### 16.4 L3 engineering response

L3 should remain a controlled alignment/tracking verification and corrective-maintenance item.

The next steps should include:
- establish suitable fixed references;
- measure the tail-end belt/pulley/support relationship;
- inspect the relevant mechanical adjustment, support, idler and tension condition;
- identify and remove trapped material where it could distort the assessment;
- correct any confirmed alignment/tracking deficiency;
- verify the belt path under representative operating conditions;
- retain the fines/carryback condition as a separate local material-control issue;
- only strengthen the solid-product causal conclusion if later operating evidence demonstrates that relationship.

### 16.5 L3 close-out principle

The L3 finding should be closed on the basis of verified mechanical condition and representative operation, not on an assumed spillage cause.

A satisfactory close-out record should demonstrate:
- the relevant geometry measured;
- confirmed alignment/tracking deficiencies corrected as required;
- representative belt operation checked after correction;
- any material interaction or product-loss consequence documented as actually observed;
- no unsupported offset percentage carried into the final engineering record.

### L3 reader takeaway

> **The presence of a supported offset justifies engineering verification and correction where required; it does not, by itself, prove the same solid-product-loss mechanism demonstrated at U4.**

## 16.6 What U4 and L3 Demonstrate Together

The combined purpose of these cases is broader than the two assets themselves.

They establish a report-wide method for interpreting tracking/alignment findings:

1. **observe the belt / pulley / support relationship;**
2. **identify what material consequence is actually visible;**
3. **separate the observed consequence from the suspected mechanical source;**
4. **measure the geometry before assigning magnitude or tolerance;**
5. **correct confirmed contributors;**
6. **prove the result under representative operating conditions.**

This approach prevents two opposite errors:
- ignoring a genuine material-loss mechanism because the exact alignment magnitude has not yet been surveyed; and
- overstating causation simply because a belt or pulley appears offset in a photograph.

The U4 evidence is sufficiently strong to justify a local mistracking/product-loss conclusion while still requiring quantitative alignment verification. L3 remains appropriately one step earlier in the evidence chain: the offset is supported, but the solid-product consequence has not been established.

## 16.7 Transition to Part VII — Water, Wet Fines and Structural Consequences

Parts V and VI have focused on material leaving the carrying envelope through loading, geometry, containment and tracking mechanisms.

The inspection also identified a different form of loss of process control: **water escaping the intended screening/process envelope and interacting with fines and surrounding structure.**

This introduces a different engineering problem because water can:
- mobilise fines and produce slurry;
- transport contamination away from its point of origin;
- repeatedly wet structural components;
- increase housekeeping/access burden;
- and coexist with structural deterioration that requires separate competent assessment.

The next part therefore moves from belt-position and product-containment mechanisms to **process-water loss, wet-fines transport and structural condition**, beginning with the Screens and then L1.
