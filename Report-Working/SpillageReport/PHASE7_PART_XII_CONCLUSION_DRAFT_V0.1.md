# PART XII — CONCLUSION

## 29. Conclusion

The Combined Product Spillage Audit indicates that spillage across the inspected Wessels Mine material-handling route is **not the result of one isolated defect or one universal equipment failure**.

The combined evidence supports a broader engineering conclusion: the inspected system presents a **material- and process-containment problem expressed through several different mechanisms at different locations**.

The dominant recurring system-level pattern is fines/carryback. Distributed fines accumulation across multiple underground and surface conveyor sections, together with the detailed cleaner cases, shows why this problem cannot be reduced to housekeeping or to one standard scraper defect.

CV06 demonstrates a directly compromised cleaner condition. CV09 demonstrates a different but equally important limitation: material can be removed from the belt while still becoming environmental spillage if the removed fines are not retained inside the intended discharge/process envelope. Effective fines control therefore requires both **effective belt cleaning** and **controlled return of the removed material to the process**.

The audit also identified material-loss mechanisms that are local rather than system-wide.

Coarse/mixed-product loss at W10 and the Sample Conveyors requires control of the usable carrying envelope through the appropriate combination of feed, geometry, tracking and local secondary containment. The evidence does not justify one universal cause or barrier arrangement. Sample Conveyor 2 retains a feed/loading/containment concern without proven significant mistracking, while Sample Conveyor 3 additionally supports lateral offset/mistracking with unmeasured magnitude.

U4 provides the strongest local tracking-to-product-loss case because lateral mistracking and directional one-sided coarse/lump-product accumulation are both supported. L3 provides an important contrast: an alignment/offset concern is supported, but the current evidence does not establish an equivalent causal chain to significant solid-product loss. This distinction reflects the report-wide requirement that the strength of the causal conclusion must match the strength of the evidence.

At the Screens, active process-water loss of containment is directly verified, while the exact equipment-level escape mechanism remains to be established through safe shutdown investigation. Severe corrosion/deterioration and cut/notched support members are also directly verified, but their remaining structural capacity and the historical reason for the physical alterations require competent structural assessment rather than assumption.

L1 similarly requires mechanical and structural issues to remain distinct but coordinated. Extensive accumulation, previous/intermittent belt-to-structure contact, concrete deterioration and steel deterioration are supported by the evidence. Continuous present belt contact is not established, and remaining structural capacity cannot be inferred from the photographic record.

The audit also distinguishes chronic source-generated spillage from event-driven material handling. Product deliberately removed during an authorised trip/restart intervention may still require containment, accountability and recovery, but it should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of a chronic mechanical failure at the deposit location.

Across all these mechanisms, the governing engineering principle is the same:

> **Keep the combined product — fines and coarse/lump ore — within the intended process/material-handling envelope throughout transit, and keep process water within its intended operating envelope, using controls appropriate to the specific failure mechanism and local geometry.**

This means the long-term response should move beyond repeated consequence management toward a hierarchy of:

1. identify and verify the actual loss mechanism;
2. correct the primary source or contributor where practicable;
3. provide suitable primary containment;
4. add secondary containment only where a residual duty remains and the local geometry justifies it;
5. retain housekeeping/recovery as necessary consequence control;
6. prove the corrected condition under representative operation before close-out.

The audit therefore does **not** recommend one identical cleaner, skirt, barrier or mechanical modification throughout the system. What can be standardised is the required engineering **function and close-out philosophy**; the physical solution must be adapted to the local belt, material duty, geometry, access, maintenance, guarding and applicable South32/OEM/engineering requirements.

The CV09 cleaner-and-containment pilot provides the preferred opportunity to prove the principal fines-control philosophy before wider applicability is considered. U4, the Sample Conveyors, W10, the Screens and L1 require targeted actions because their mechanisms and physical constraints differ. CV06 requires restoration and verification of the directly compromised cleaner function, while L3 requires quantitative alignment/tracking verification without promoting the condition into a stronger product-loss conclusion than the evidence supports.

Housekeeping remains essential for access, recovery and safe operation, but it is not evidence that the initiating mechanism has been corrected.

> **Housekeeping controls the consequence. Source control should reduce the recurrence.**

The recommended programme should therefore be closed through an evidence-based cycle:

> **As-found evidence → verification / measurement → corrective design or control → implementation → representative operating proof → as-left record → monitoring / closure.**

The ultimate objective is not simply a cleaner plant floor. It is a material-handling system in which product and process media remain controlled through the intended process, local exceptions are addressed according to verified mechanism and geometry, and unavoidable losses or interventions are contained, recoverable and deliberately managed.

## Phase 7 conclusion reconciliation check

This conclusion closes only themes already established in the refined report body and Part XI programme:
- systemic fines/carryback;
- cleaner function plus removed-material containment;
- coarse/mixed-product carrying-envelope control;
- evidence-qualified tracking/alignment;
- process-water containment;
- separate structural assessment requirements;
- event-driven material handling;
- housekeeping as consequence control;
- mechanism-specific physical solutions under one common containment philosophy;
- representative operating proof before close-out.

No new engineering mechanism or unsupported quantitative criterion is introduced here.
