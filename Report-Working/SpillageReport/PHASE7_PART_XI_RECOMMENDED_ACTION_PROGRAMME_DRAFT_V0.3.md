## Purpose
This draft is the reconciled **PART XI — RECOMMENDED ACTION PROGRAMME** following completion of the refined Parts IX and X.

Part IX establishes what the combined evidence means. Part X establishes the governing engineering control philosophy. Part XI therefore performs one task only:

> **convert the established evidence and control philosophy into a staged, verifiable implementation programme.**

Each action is structured around:
- engineering / operational objective;
- prerequisite verification;
- required action sequence;
- functional acceptance / close-out criteria;
- evidence traceability;
- explicit authority boundaries where final design, structural or operational acceptance lies outside the audit.

No unmeasured dimension, alignment tolerance, feed rate, structural capacity or OEM limit is introduced as fact.

Two evidence-to-action gaps identified during reconciliation are closed in this version:
- **CV06** now has an explicit cleaner-function restoration and verification action;
- **L3** now has an explicit quantitative alignment/tracking verification action while retaining that significant solid-product causation is not established.

# PART XI — RECOMMENDED ACTION PROGRAMME

## 24. Programme Structure and Implementation Principle

The programme follows the control hierarchy established in Part X and should be closed on **functional performance**, not task completion alone.

The five implementation priorities are:

1. **prove and restore the principal fines-control functions;**
2. **correct or verify confirmed/strongly supported local material-loss mechanisms;**
3. **resolve process-water containment and structural conditions through dedicated investigation/assessment;**
4. **extend proven functional controls only where local applicability is established;**
5. **strengthen operational and housekeeping governance as supporting controls.**

Where measurement, shutdown inspection, structural assessment or design development remains necessary, the recommendation is intentionally staged. Conceptual arrangements are not fabrication drawings and require site verification and responsible-engineer/OEM/South32 acceptance as applicable.

### 24.1 Priority 1A — Prove the Cleaner / Fines-Containment Function at CV09

#### Engineering objective
Demonstrate a practical cleaner-and-containment arrangement that reduces downstream wet-fines carryback **and** keeps material removed from the belt within the intended process/fines-discharge stream.

The pilot must prove the cleaning and containment functions together.

#### Required action sequence

**1. Establish the as-found baseline.** Before modification:
- record the existing cleaner arrangement, position and blade/contact condition;
- verify the local chute/enclosure geometry and available installation envelope;
- identify the intended route by which removed wet fines should return to the process stream;
- record maintenance, inspection and adjustment access;
- establish a practical pre-trial record of downstream carryback and local wet-fines fallout under representative operating conditions.

**2. Complete detailed design development.** Develop the pilot from verified site geometry. Evaluate:
- positive cleaner-to-belt contact through the agreed wear range;
- suitable wear/travel limitation;
- cleaner location within an effective containment envelope;
- controlled routing of removed wet fines into the intended process stream;
- primary/secondary cleaning stages where justified by the actual wet/sticky service condition;
- safe inspection, adjustment, guarding and replacement access.

A spring-loaded/self-tensioning cleaner remains a **design concept to be evaluated**, not an unqualified final equipment selection.

**3. Install and commission the pilot.** Fabrication/installation should proceed only after geometry, cleaner selection, safety requirements and maintenance access have been verified and accepted by the responsible South32 / engineering authorities.

**4. Monitor performance before wider rollout.** Operate the pilot for an agreed monitoring period and retain sufficient inspection records to compare the post-modification condition with the as-found baseline.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
The CV09 pilot should only be regarded as successful when the available operating record demonstrates that:
- downstream carryback is materially reduced relative to the pre-trial condition;
- wet fines removed by the cleaner remain within the intended process stream rather than falling into the plant area;
- cleaner-to-belt contact remains stable through the agreed wear range;
- no unacceptable belt damage or abnormal cleaner wear is introduced;
- inspection, adjustment and replacement remain practical;
- no unacceptable new guarding, trapping or access conflict is created;
- the monitoring record is sufficient to support a defensible decision on wider applicability.

#### Evidence traceability
Principal case: **CV09**. Controlled evidence includes `CV09-REF-002`, `CV09-REF-003`, video `CV09-REF-006`, with `CV09-REF-008` available as supplementary dynamic evidence.

#### Authority boundary
Final cleaner make/type, OEM wear limits, detailed chute modification, trial duration and final rollout approval remain South32 / responsible-engineer / OEM decisions as applicable.

### 24.2 Priority 1B — Restore and Verify CV06 Cleaner Function

#### Engineering objective
Restore the directly compromised CV06 head-cleaner function and verify that the cleaner is complete, mechanically supported and operating as intended before considering any broader redesign.

#### Required action sequence
1. Inspect the complete CV06 cleaner assembly and confirm the as-found condition of the support housing, bush/insert, cross-shaft and blade system.
2. Establish why the support bush/insert is displaced and why scraper blades are absent from the working belt width, where maintenance/history records permit.
3. Restore or replace the defective/missing cleaner components through the applicable maintenance/OEM process.
4. Verify cleaner position, support integrity, blade coverage/contact and adjustment after restoration.
5. Observe the restored cleaner under representative operation and record the local downstream carryback condition.
6. Use the CV09 pilot findings, once available, to determine whether any further cleaner-plus-containment upgrade is justified at CV06; do not presume an identical hardware solution.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- displaced/defective support condition corrected;
- required cleaner blade elements present across the intended working width;
- cleaner support and adjustment function verified;
- representative operation confirms the cleaner is performing its intended cleaning duty to the accepted local standard;
- any remaining carryback/containment concern is recorded for mechanism-based follow-up rather than attributed to an assumed design cause.

#### Evidence traceability
Key evidence: `CV06-REF-004` — displaced support bush/insert; `CV06-REF-005` — cleaner support beam/cross-shaft with scraper blades absent from the working belt width.

#### Authority boundary
Final component disposition, cleaner settings, OEM requirements and any later redesign remain South32 / responsible-maintenance / conveyor-engineering / OEM decisions as applicable.

## 25. Priority 2 — Correct or Verify Local Material-Loss / Alignment Mechanisms

### 25.1 U4 Tripper Car — Tracking / Alignment Correction

#### Engineering objective
Restore and maintain a centred belt path through the full tripper operating range so that the verified one-sided product-loss condition is removed or materially reduced.

#### Required action sequence
1. Establish fixed and repeatable survey/reference points.
2. Quantitatively verify the relevant tail-pulley, support and belt-path geometry in the as-found condition.
3. Identify the mechanical or structural source of any confirmed geometry error and verify the available adjustment provisions.
4. Remove trapped accumulated material that could distort tracking assessment.
5. Correct the confirmed alignment/tracking contributor as required.
6. Verify belt tracking under representative operating conditions through the **full tripper travel range**, not at one static position only.
7. Record the as-left geometry and reference points for future trending.
8. Assess the U4 cleaner arrangement separately; do not automatically apply the CV09 pilot hardware because the local geometry is materially different.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- relevant pulley/alignment geometry measured and corrected as required;
- stable/centred tracking demonstrated under representative operation through full tripper travel;
- adequate side carrying margin/clearance demonstrated at the critical operating positions;
- the previously observed one-sided product-loss condition materially reduced or removed;
- as-left geometry/reference data retained as the maintenance baseline;
- U4 cleaner/containment requirements addressed as a separate local assessment.

#### Evidence traceability
Key evidence includes `UGU4-REF-009` for visually supported mistracking, `UGU4-REF-007` for the one-sided product-loss consequence, video `UGU4-REF-010` for alignment context and `UGU4-REF-011/-012` for the custom cleaner arrangement.

#### Authority boundary
Final tracking/alignment tolerances, repair disposition of defective support/adjustment hardware and operational acceptance remain the responsibility of the applicable South32 / conveyor-engineering authority.

### 25.2 Sample Conveyor 3 — Feed, Tracking and Secondary Containment

#### Engineering objective
Reduce coarse/mixed-product loss by addressing the feed and tracking contributors before finalising any secondary side-containment system.

#### Required action sequence
1. Measure the usable carrying width of the belt.
2. Document representative product-size and feed conditions relevant to containment.
3. Identify the actual upstream feed-control/metering mechanism and the practical variable available for controlling incoming product quantity.
4. Observe representative feed trajectory and loading condition.
5. Quantitatively establish the lateral offset/mistracking condition.
6. Inspect head/tail alignment, belt tensioning and relevant support/idler condition.
7. Remove trapped accumulated material before the final tracking assessment.
8. Correct or control the identified feed/tracking contributors.
9. Reassess whether secondary side containment is still required after the primary contributors have been addressed.
10. If required, develop the side-containment system from the verified belt operating envelope, with suitable clearance, clean-out access and protection against trapping or abnormal belt contact.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- usable belt width documented;
- representative feed/product condition verified;
- lateral offset quantified and root mechanical contributors checked;
- feed and tracking controlled to the agreed operating condition;
- coarse/mixed-product loss materially reduced;
- any remaining side-containment arrangement retains the required belt clearance through the verified operating envelope and remains maintainable;
- the secondary containment does not become a normal belt-contact surface.

#### Evidence traceability
Principal evidence includes `LoadoutStationSample3-REF-013`, `-005/-006`, `-002` and `-012`.

#### Authority boundary
Acceptable feed rate/control set point, tracking tolerance, final side-containment geometry/material and guarding requirements remain South32 / responsible-engineer decisions.

### 25.3 Sample Conveyor 2 — Feed / Loading Verification Before Secondary Containment

#### Engineering objective
Confirm and control the relationship between incoming product, representative product size and the available belt carrying/containment width before attributing the condition to mistracking or committing to final side-containment geometry.

#### Required action sequence
1. Measure usable belt carrying width.
2. Document representative product size and incoming feed condition.
3. Identify the actual upstream feed-control/metering mechanism.
4. Observe representative feed quantity and trajectory.
5. Verify head/tail alignment and live belt tracking as a check, without presuming significant mistracking from the still evidence.
6. Establish the normal belt-edge operating clearance.
7. Determine whether feed control alone materially improves containment.
8. Only after the feed/tracking condition is established, develop any required side support/skirt/rail as a secondary control.

Any previously discussed approximate side-support height or belt clearance remains conceptual until site-measured and design-verified.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- usable carrying width and representative product/feed condition documented;
- the feed can be controlled to a condition the conveyor can practically contain;
- significant mistracking is either ruled out during live verification or separately corrected if identified;
- visible containment loss is materially reduced;
- any secondary side containment retains suitable belt clearance and maintenance access.

#### Evidence traceability
Key evidence includes `LoadoutStationSample2-REF-004`, `-006/-007`, with `-001/-003` available for broader context.

#### Authority boundary
The final feed-control operating basis, side-containment design and operating acceptance remain South32 / responsible-engineer decisions.

### 25.4 W10 — Source-Control Review and Side-Containment Feasibility

#### Engineering objective
Reduce recurring mixed/coarse-product migration into the restricted W10 tunnel/access route without creating a secondary barrier that routinely contacts the belt or introduces new trapping, wear or maintenance hazards.

#### Required action sequence
1. Establish the normal belt tracking envelope through the relevant W10 section.
2. Record belt, trough, support and available side-clearance geometry.
3. Investigate likely initiating contributors and distinguish return-side/carryback fines from coarse/mixed-product loss mechanisms where the evidence allows.
4. Review source-control opportunities before treating a local side barrier as the primary corrective solution.
5. Define the access/clean-out requirements imposed by the restricted tunnel geometry.
6. If a secondary barrier/rail remains justified, develop it outside the verified normal belt operating envelope and verify the structural attachment / maintenance arrangement.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- normal tracking envelope established;
- trough/belt/support/clearance geometry verified;
- initiating contributors investigated and corrected where practicable;
- any secondary barrier remains outside normal belt contact during representative operation;
- access, trapping, wear, clean-out and maintenance risks assessed;
- product accumulation within the restricted access route materially reduced without introducing a new belt-interaction hazard.

#### Evidence traceability
Evidence pool includes `W10-REF-002`, `W10-REF-010/-011`, with `W10-REF-006/-009` available for mechanism/design context.

#### Authority boundary
Acceptable belt clearance/tracking envelope, final barrier type/material/support and confined-space/access controls remain South32 / responsible-engineer decisions.

### 25.5 L3 — Quantitative Alignment / Tracking Verification

#### Engineering objective
Quantify and correct the supported L3 tail-end alignment/offset condition where required, while preserving the evidence boundary that significant solid/lump-product loss has **not** been established as a direct consequence of that condition.

#### Required action sequence
1. Establish fixed reference points at the L3 tail-end arrangement.
2. Measure the relevant pulley, belt and supporting-frame geometry in the as-found condition.
3. Inspect the associated adjustment, support, idler and tensioning condition.
4. Remove local accumulated material where it could influence the tracking assessment.
5. Observe live belt tracking under representative operation.
6. Correct any verified alignment/tracking defect through the applicable maintenance/engineering process.
7. Record the as-left geometry and operating belt position.
8. Observe whether the corrected condition changes the local fines/material accumulation pattern; do not retrospectively classify significant solid-product loss unless operating evidence supports it.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- tail-end geometry/alignment quantitatively established;
- any confirmed defect corrected or formally dispositioned;
- representative live tracking observed and recorded;
- as-left references retained;
- the close-out record maintains the distinction between the verified alignment concern and the currently unproven significant solid-product causal chain.

#### Evidence traceability
Key evidence includes `LoadoutStationL3-REF-001` for the supported tail-end offset/alignment concern, `LoadoutStationL3-REF-007` for fines/carryback contamination and `LoadoutStationL3-REF-008` as supporting lower-area accumulation evidence.

#### Authority boundary
Final alignment/tracking tolerance, adjustment disposition and operating acceptance remain South32 / responsible conveyor-engineering decisions.

## 26. Priority 3 — Water Containment and Structural Actions

### 26.1 Screens — Identify the Water-Escape Mechanism Before Modification

#### Engineering objective
Retain process water and entrained wet fines within the intended screening/chute process envelope by correcting the **verified escape mechanism**, rather than designing from an assumed cause.

#### Required action sequence
1. Complete a close safe-shutdown inspection of the screen/chute area.
2. Correlate the shutdown observations with the existing dynamic video evidence of active water escape.
3. Identify the actual escape path(s) and assess relevant seals, chute interfaces, screen movement/clearance, enclosure geometry and local maintenance condition.
4. Record the geometry required for a controlled containment modification.
5. Develop and implement the modification only after the loss mechanism is established.
6. Verify the modified system under representative operation.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- the actual water escape path/mechanism is identified;
- the modification addresses that verified mechanism;
- representative operation demonstrates that process water/wet fines are retained within the intended process envelope to the accepted operating standard;
- no new maintenance or access conflict is introduced.

#### Evidence traceability
Dynamic evidence includes video `Screen-REF-016/-017`, supported by the controlled screen photographic evidence set.

---

### 26.2 Screens — Structural Support Assessment

#### Engineering objective
Establish the structural condition and remaining capacity of the cut/notched/corroded support members and define the required repair or replacement scope independently of unverified assumptions about why the damage occurred.

#### Required action sequence
1. Appoint a competent structural authority to inspect the affected members/connections.
2. Record relevant member dimensions, cut/notch geometry, section loss, connection condition and other inputs required by the structural assessment.
3. Determine remaining capacity and required repair/replacement scope.
4. Where future equipment installation/maintenance clearance requires geometry changes, develop those changes through formal structural calculation and approval.
5. Complete and document the approved structural intervention.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- competent structural assessment complete;
- remaining capacity/status defined;
- repair/replacement requirement defined and implemented or formally scheduled/controlled as appropriate;
- any revised support geometry formally checked/approved;
- the final structural recommendation remains separate from unproven assumptions about the original reason for the cut-outs.

#### Evidence traceability
Key photographic evidence includes `Screen-REF-006/-010`, supported by `Screen-REF-004/-008` for wider deterioration/water context.

#### Authority boundary
Structural capacity, repair method, temporary operating restrictions and final acceptance are competent-structural-authority / South32 decisions.

### 26.3 L1 — Structural Condition and Belt-Clearance Verification

#### Engineering objective
Define and correct the documented L1 structural deterioration, reduce conditions that may accelerate further deterioration and verify that representative belt operation maintains suitable clearance from the affected support geometry.

#### Required action sequence
1. Complete a competent structural assessment of the affected concrete, reinforcement, steel members and connections.
2. Record dimensions/section loss and other information required for capacity assessment and repair design.
3. Record the existing wear-slot / contact geometry at the affected supports.
4. Verify belt tracking and clearance under representative operating conditions.
5. Do not infer continuous present belt contact from historic/intermittent wear marks unless direct verification establishes it.
6. Develop and implement the required structural repair/replacement.
7. Reduce recurring fines/moisture accumulation around repaired interfaces as part of the local corrosion-control strategy.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- structural condition and remaining capacity formally assessed;
- repair/replacement scope defined and completed/controlled by the competent authority;
- representative operation confirms acceptable belt tracking/clearance at the affected supports;
- the report/close-out record continues to distinguish verified historical/intermittent contact from any current operating contact;
- recurring fines/moisture accumulation around repaired interfaces is materially reduced or controlled.

#### Evidence traceability
Key evidence includes `LoadoutStationL1-REF-007`, `-003/-005`, `-008`, `-013`, with `-017` available as supporting context.

#### Authority boundary
Structural disposition, acceptable belt clearance and any operational restrictions remain South32 / competent structural and conveyor-engineering decisions.

## 27. Priority 4 — Extend Proven Controls Where Applicable

After the CV09 pilot and targeted local actions have produced verified results, complete a structured applicability review at other relevant discharge and vulnerable transport locations.

The purpose is to extend **proven functional requirements**, not to copy hardware automatically.

### Applicability review should consider
- actual local spillage/carryback mechanism;
- cleaner presence and maintenance condition;
- belt/material condition;
- chute/discharge geometry;
- available installation envelope;
- tracking/loading condition;
- route available for removed material to return to the process;
- safe inspection/maintenance access;
- guarding/trapping considerations;
- whether the proven functional solution can be adapted without creating a new local problem.

#### Functional close-out
A control should only be standardised after its functional result is proven and local applicability is verified. Successful CV09 performance justifies wider assessment of the cleaner/containment philosophy; it does **not** justify copying the same hardware unchanged to U4 or every conveyor.

## 28. Priority 5 — Operational and Housekeeping Governance

### 28.1 Trip / Restart Product Handling

#### Engineering / operational objective
Ensure product removed from loaded conveyors during trip/restart preparation is controlled, temporarily contained and returned to the process without creating unmanaged secondary deposits.

#### Required actions
- confirm the current written trip/restart unloading procedure;
- define authorised responsibility for unloading, temporary placement and recovery;
- identify/confirm controlled temporary laydown/containment arrangements where required;
- define how and when removed material is returned to the process;
- ensure the procedure distinguishes unavoidable event-driven clearing from recurring mechanically generated spillage.

#### Functional acceptance / close-out criteria
- procedure documented and approved;
- responsibilities explicit;
- temporary material placement controlled rather than ad hoc;
- recovery/reintroduction route defined;
- event-driven deposits identifiable separately from chronic source-generated spillage.

---

### 28.2 Housekeeping / Recovery

Housekeeping remains necessary to maintain safe access, recover spilled material and control consequences while engineering improvements are being developed and after unavoidable events.

It should not be treated as evidence that the initiating mechanism has been corrected.

#### Required actions
- confirm housekeeping/recovery responsibilities;
- define normal inspection/clearing frequency appropriate to the operating area;
- retain records where repeat accumulation is significant enough to support trend assessment;
- use recurring accumulation as feedback on the effectiveness of source-control measures;
- maintain safe access/escape routes, particularly in restricted areas such as W10.

#### Functional close-out
Housekeeping governance is established when responsibilities, frequency and recovery controls are defined and recurring accumulation records can be used to distinguish consequence management from source-control performance.

---

### 28.3 Programme Close-Out Rule

Part X defines the full close-out philosophy. For Part XI implementation, each action should retain the applicable evidence chain:

> **As-found evidence → verification / measurement → corrective design or control → implementation → representative operating proof → as-left record → monitoring / closure.**

A maintenance sign-off, installation completion or cleaned area is therefore an intermediate milestone unless it also demonstrates the intended functional outcome at the level appropriate to the action.

## Part XI Reconciliation Check

This V0.3 programme now closes the main evidence-to-action chain established in Parts IV–X:

- CV06 compromised cleaner condition → restore and verify cleaner function;
- CV09 cleaner/containment case → pilot and prove system-level fines-control function;
- U4 tracking/one-sided loss → quantify, correct and prove full-travel tracking;
- Sample Conveyor 3 → verify/control feed and tracking before secondary containment;
- Sample Conveyor 2 → verify/control feed/loading without presuming mistracking;
- W10 → investigate source contributors and only then develop secondary barrier if justified;
- L3 → quantify/correct alignment concern without promoting unproven solid-product causation;
- Screens water escape → identify verified mechanism before modification;
- Screens structural condition → competent assessment and approved repair disposition;
- L1 → competent structural assessment plus representative tracking/clearance verification;
- trip/restart handling → controlled procedural containment/recovery;
- housekeeping → consequence control and performance feedback;
- wider rollout → standardise functional requirements only after proof and local applicability review.

No new mechanism is introduced by the programme; the additions close action gaps already supported by the refined report body.
