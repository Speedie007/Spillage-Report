# South32 Spillage Audit Report — Hybrid Narrative Architecture

## Purpose
This document records the agreed direction for the final report form after the V0.1 review and context-alignment discussion.

The factual/engineering content developed in `Context/Running_Report_Contextual_Spillage_NotesV3.7.docx` is substantially aligned. The principal redesign requirement is **how the report communicates that content**.

## Governing Report Identity
The final document should be a **hybrid engineering audit report** with three layers:

1. **Formal shell** — controlled engineering-report components required for issue, governance and traceability.
2. **Narrative core** — a guided audit story explaining what is happening across the connected material-handling system and why the findings matter collectively.
3. **Technical evidence backbone** — photographs, evidence sheets, finding records, qualifications and action details that substantiate the narrative.

The report must remain professional and technically defensible, but it should not read like a repetitive component defect register.

## Formal Shell — Retain / Reuse
The following formal elements from the ISS sheave-report lineage remain suitable and should be retained or adapted:

- clean professional cover page;
- report title, report number, revision, issue date and issue status;
- client / site / project metadata;
- document-control / revision-history table;
- distribution list;
- prepared / reviewed / approved sign-off register;
- confidentiality / disclaimer / limitations where applicable;
- table of contents;
- list of figures and list of tables in the final controlled issue;
- inspection scope and limitations;
- definitions / terminology where they materially assist the reader;
- references / source documents;
- controlled appendices and evidence register.

These components provide formality and governance. They should **frame** the audit story rather than dictate the writing style of the body.

## Narrative Core — Intended Reader Experience
The report body should guide the South32 reader through the investigation in a logical sequence:

### A. Why the audit was undertaken
Explain the purpose in plain engineering language:
- spillage occurs at multiple locations;
- the audit was intended to determine whether the observed deposits arise from one common cause or multiple interacting mechanisms;
- the focus is on mechanism, containment and practical corrective action rather than exact tonnage quantification.

### B. Understand the system before the defects
Orient the reader to the material journey from the underground crusher through the inspected conveyor ecosystem, surface screening and loading station.

Where useful, include a simple process/topology overview so later photographs and findings can be interpreted in context.

### C. Explain the central audit principle
State clearly:

> The point at which spilled material is observed is not necessarily the point at which the initiating failure occurred.

Use carryback, water transport, mistracking and trip/restart unloading as examples.

### D. Build the three interacting spillage stories
Explain the observed mechanisms as connected themes rather than isolated defects:

1. **Fines / carryback and cleaner-containment performance**
2. **Coarse / combined-product transit loss and lateral containment**
3. **Water-related escape / wet fines / slurry and secondary consequences**

Event-driven trip/restart unloading should be explained as a separate operational mechanism that overlays the chronic conditions.

### E. Use case evidence to prove and refine the story
Use selected assets as case studies rather than treating every asset as an equal standalone chapter.

Examples:
- CV06 — mechanical cleaner failure / missing blades / displaced support bush.
- CV09 — cleaner removes material but removed fines are not adequately retained within the intended process envelope.
- W10 — coarse/mixed product has escaped the carrying envelope in a confined tunnel; exact initiating mechanism may remain unresolved, but the containment consequence is clear.
- Sample Conveyors 2/3 — narrow-belt loading, product size, feed control and local side containment; Sample 3 additionally has visually supported mistracking.
- U4 — local alignment/mistracking mechanism causing lateral product loss; custom cleaner geometry means generic standardisation is inappropriate.
- Screens — confirmed water loss of containment plus separate structural deterioration/damage issues.
- L1 — historical/intermittent belt-contact evidence plus separate structural deterioration.

Other inspected assets remain in the evidence record and should be used where they add topology, comparison, extent or support to the broader story.

### F. State the unified engineering control philosophy
The higher-order objective is:

> **Keep the combined product — fines and coarse/lump ore — within the intended process/material-handling envelope throughout transit, using the control mechanism appropriate to the failure mode and local geometry.**

This means:
- fines/carryback → clean effectively and keep removed material in the intended process stream;
- transfers → control trajectory / chute containment;
- coarse lateral losses → correct tracking/alignment and use location-specific side containment where justified;
- narrow sample conveyors → feed control first, tracking second, secondary side containment where appropriate;
- screening → retain product/process water within the designed screening/fines-handling process;
- event-driven unloading → controlled temporary placement and recovery procedure.

### G. Recommendations should follow the hierarchy of the story
Recommendations should be presented in layers rather than as one flat defect list:

1. **System-level containment strategy**
2. **Carryback / cleaner-containment pilot and standardisation philosophy**
3. **Coarse-product transit containment controls**
4. **Local alignment / tracking corrections**
5. **Water-containment and structural actions**
6. **Operational / housekeeping controls**
7. **Verification / measurement / design-development requirements**

## Writing Style — What “Softened” Means
“Softened” does **not** mean informal, casual or less technical.

It means:
- explain the engineering logic before presenting the evidence table;
- use short narrative paragraphs and bullets where they improve comprehension;
- introduce cause-and-effect transitions such as “This matters because…”, “The significance of this condition is…”, “Taken together, these observations indicate…”;
- avoid repeating the same clinical metadata and assessment wording after every photograph;
- make the reader understand the conclusion before asking them to process the detailed evidence.

The preferred tone is **formal engineering narrative**.

## Evidence Layout Principle
The body should not be forced into one-photo-per-page modules.

Use evidence according to the narrative need:
- hero photograph + supporting context images;
- two portrait images side-by-side;
- stacked landscape images;
- 2 x 2 context/sequence grids;
- grouped technical evidence sheets for one engineering story;
- appendices for useful record evidence that does not need to interrupt the main narrative.

Every image retains its canonical evidence ID and traceability.

## Important Balance
The final report should allow two types of reader to use it effectively:

### Management / Operational Reader
Should be able to read the narrative chapters and understand:
- what the systemic problem is;
- where the major local exceptions are;
- why the current condition persists;
- what control philosophy is recommended;
- what needs to happen next.

### Engineering / Maintenance Reader
Should be able to follow the evidence references, detailed case studies, asset-specific actions and appendices to understand:
- exactly what was observed;
- what is proven versus inferred;
- what physical change / inspection / measurement is required;
- what evidence supports the recommendation.

The report succeeds when both readers obtain what they need without forcing either one through the wrong level of detail.

## Development Rule
Do not rebuild the formal report directly from the current sheave-style V0.1 structure. First agree the narrative outline, then map the existing contextual findings and evidence into that outline. Reuse the strongest visual/control elements of the sheave-report lineage only where they serve the audit story.
