# REF-006 + REF-007 — principal lateral belt-offset pair

## Inspector interpretation

REF-006 and REF-007 are to be treated as complementary images showing the lateral belt-offset condition clearly.

### REF-006

- belt/product distribution is visibly asymmetric;
- a significant portion of the roller is exposed and not carrying the belt;
- this provides a clear physical reference that the belt is laterally displaced rather than centred;
- the image also shows uneven distribution of the transported product relative to the available carrying width.

### REF-007

- provides a stronger visual impression of the **extent/severity** of the lateral offset;
- the inspector estimates visually that the belt may be displaced by a large fraction of the roller/belt width, describing the impression as potentially around 40% or more;
- this percentage is **not measured** and must not be used as a quantitative report value.

## Evidence-controlled interpretation

The pair supports:

> **A visually substantial lateral belt-offset condition at Sample Conveyor 3, with exposed roller width and asymmetric belt position clearly evident in the captured photographs.**

The pair does not establish the exact offset dimension/percentage, whether the condition is continuous or load-dependent, or the mechanical cause.

For final report wording, prefer terms such as:

- **visually substantial lateral offset**;
- **a large portion of the roller remains exposed at the captured condition**; or
- **the belt is visibly displaced from the expected centred relationship to the roller arrangement**.

Avoid publishing `~40%` or any similar percentage unless later measurement provides a defensible dimensional basis.

# Engineering implication carried forward

The emerging evidence reinforces the same core relationship framework as Sample Conveyor 2:

> **feed/loading condition + product-size distribution + usable belt geometry + lateral belt position interact to determine whether the narrow Sample Conveyor can contain the transported product.**

The photographs support investigation of these relationships but do not establish one chronological initiating cause. Corrective-action sequencing must therefore remain diagnostic:

1. establish representative feed quantity/variation and product-size distribution;
2. quantify belt offset/tracking under representative loading;
3. identify and correct alignment/tracking/interference causes;
4. establish the usable carrying geometry after those corrections;
5. determine whether feed quantity/product size/loading distribution also requires control;
6. reassess residual containment duty; and
7. apply belt-compatible side-skirt/spill-seal containment where required.
