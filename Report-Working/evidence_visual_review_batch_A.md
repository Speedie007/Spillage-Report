# Visual Evidence Review - Batch A

## Scope
This review covers the first visual-evidence batch for the South32 Wessels Mine combined-product spillage report:

- CV06 - 5 photographs
- CV09 - 5 photographs and 3 videos
- U4 Conveyor - Tripper Car - 11 photographs and 1 video

The review compares the uploaded media against the current contextual narrative. Classification is based on what the media independently demonstrates, not only on the prior written description.

## Evidence Classification Key
- **Direct** - the image/video itself clearly shows the stated physical condition or event.
- **Supporting** - the image/video supports the finding or extent but does not independently prove the full causal mechanism.
- **Context only** - useful for equipment/location orientation or inspected-condition record.
- **Source-recorded observation** - retained where the inspector recorded the condition directly in the contextual notes/questionnaire, but the supplied visual alone does not fully prove it.

# 1. CV06

## 1.1 Overall visual conclusion
The visual evidence supports the contextual conclusion that CV06 had a non-functional or materially compromised belt-cleaning arrangement at the time of inspection. The strongest evidence is CV06-REF-004 and CV06-REF-005.

The photographs support two separate deficiencies:

1. the scraper support/bushing assembly is visibly displaced relative to its housing; and
2. the cleaner support beam/cross-shaft is present below the belt but scraper blade elements are not visible across the belt width.

The photographs do **not** establish why the support bush became displaced or why the blades were absent. The existing contextual statements that poor housing design is a probable contributor and that maintenance/replacement history is unknown therefore remain correctly qualified.

# 2. CV09

## 2.1 Overall visual conclusion
The CV09 evidence strongly supports the contextual finding that wet fines/carryback are being released into the surrounding plant area in the head/discharge region and that the existing cleaner arrangement is a key local mechanism.

The visual record directly confirms:
- wet/slurry-like accumulation on the floor beneath the conveyor/transfer area;
- a cleaner/scraper assembly immediately below the belt that is heavily coated with wet fines;
- the cleaner region is visibly open to the surrounding structure rather than enclosed within a complete local containment envelope in the supplied views;
- extensive fines coating on structural steel below the belt; and
- active droplets/wet material falling from the overhead conveyor/transfer region during CV09-REF-006 video.

The videos and photographs therefore materially strengthen the existing CV09 working concept. They support the need to capture material removed at the cleaner and direct it into the intended CV09 fines discharge path rather than allowing it to fall into the plant area.

The visual evidence does **not** by itself verify the detailed feasibility dimensions for the proposed chute extension, the final cleaner type, the number of cleaning stages, or the proposed spring/self-tensioning mechanism. Those remain engineering design concepts requiring detailed design and site measurement.

# 3. U4 Conveyor - Tripper Car

## 3.1 Overall visual conclusion
The U4 evidence strongly confirms the presence of severe/localised spillage and belt tracking displacement in the tripper-car area. The most useful visual evidence is UGU4-REF-006, -007, -009, -010, -011 and -012.

The visual record directly confirms:
- substantial accumulated fines/product adjacent to the tripper structure;
- lump/solid-product accumulation concentrated along one side of the conveyor area;
- visible lateral displacement/mistracking of the belt relative to the idler/support geometry; and
- a distinct/custom transverse cleaner/scraper arrangement that is materially different from the conventional CV06/CV09 cleaner arrangements.

A key qualification is required for the pulley-causation statement. UGU4-REF-008 and UGU4-REF-010 show the running tail-pulley/belt region and support the recorded alignment concern, but the supplied visuals do not provide a fixed survey reference or dimensional measurement sufficient to independently quantify the pulley misalignment relative to the tripper structure. Therefore:

- **Belt mistracking and associated one-side spillage are visually verified.**
- **Tail-pulley misalignment remains a source-recorded direct inspection observation and is visually supported, but is not independently quantified/proven by the photographs/video alone.**

This does not invalidate the contextual finding; it improves the evidence wording by separating what the photographs/video prove from what was directly observed on site by the inspector.

# 4. Batch A Comparison Against Contextual Notes

## 4.1 Findings strengthened by visual review
- CV06 displaced support bush: visually verified.
- CV06 missing cleaner blades: visually verified.
- CV09 wet-fines/slurry accumulation: visually verified.
- CV09 cleaner heavily contaminated with wet carryback and located in an open/non-contained local arrangement: visually verified.
- CV09 active wet fallout during operation: visually verified by video.
- U4 severe spillage accumulation: visually verified.
- U4 lateral belt mistracking: visually verified.
- U4 custom/different cleaner arrangement: visually verified.

## 4.2 Findings that remain source-recorded / engineering interpretation rather than visually proven
- CV06: poor housing design as the reason for the displaced bush remains a working assessment, not proven by the photograph.
- CV06: exact reason/history for missing blades remains unknown.
- CV09: exact final chute-extension geometry, spring/self-tensioning cleaner design and primary/secondary cleaner requirement remain conceptual engineering solutions.
- U4: tail-pulley misalignment relative to the tripper structure is visually supported but not independently quantified by the supplied images/video; the direct inspection observation remains the primary source for that statement.
- U4: the underlying reason the tail pulley is misaligned remains unresolved.

## 4.3 No material contradiction identified
No Batch A visual evidence materially contradicts the current contextual narrative. The principal refinement is evidentiary wording at U4: distinguish the visually verified mistracking/spillage from the inspector-recorded pulley misalignment that still requires quantitative alignment verification.
