# Visual Evidence Review - Batch C

## Scope
This review covers the third visual-evidence batch for the South32 Wessels Mine combined-product spillage report:

- Screens / Screen Chutes - 14 photographs and 3 videos
- Loading Station L1 - 18 photographs

The review compares the uploaded media against the current contextual narrative. Classification is based on what the media independently demonstrates. Where the contextual source identifies a structural member as load-bearing or records an inspection observation that cannot be proven from a still image alone, that distinction is retained.

## Evidence Classification Key
- **Direct** - the image/video itself clearly shows the stated physical condition or event.
- **Supporting** - the image/video supports the finding or extent but does not independently prove the full causal mechanism.
- **Context only** - useful for orientation, location or inspected-condition record.
- **Source-recorded observation** - an inspection observation retained from the questionnaires/contextual notes where the media supports but does not independently establish the complete proposition.

# 1. Screens / Screen Chutes

## 1.1 Overall visual conclusion
The screen evidence strongly confirms two separate conditions already identified in the contextual notes:

1. active loss of process water from the screen/chute area during operation; and
2. severe corrosion/deterioration together with multiple physical cut-outs/notches in structural support members around the screen installation.

The photographs directly show extensive corrosion, section loss and local openings/cut-outs in steel members. The videos directly show water spray, droplets and runoff escaping the equipment envelope and wetting surrounding steelwork/areas during operation.

The visual evidence does **not** establish the equipment-level source of the water loss, the reason the structural members were cut, or the load path/capacity of the affected members. The contextual source identifies the affected members as load-bearing; the media verifies the physical damage but structural significance still requires formal engineering assessment.

# 2. Loading Station L1

## 2.1 Overall visual conclusion
The L1 evidence strongly confirms extensive spillage/accumulation and serious structural deterioration in the tunnel/conveyor area. It also provides direct visual support for the previously recorded belt-to-structure contact history.

The visual record directly confirms:
- extensive dry fines and lump/solid-product accumulation on floors and surrounding structure;
- severe concrete deterioration/spalling with exposed reinforcement and corrosion staining in the tunnel/overhead structure;
- localised steel-section loss/perforation/corrosion at support members; and
- distinct smooth notches/wear slots in angular steel supports immediately adjacent to the running belt path.

Importantly, LoadoutStationL1-REF-007 also shows the belt running clear of the adjacent angular supports at the instant photographed. This is consistent with the contextual statement that the belt was centred/not in continuous contact during the latest inspection, while the wear slots record previous/intermittent contact.

The static photographs do not independently prove the full mechanical root cause of the earlier contact. Belt mistracking remains the probable cause based on the geometry and inspection record, but exact dynamic tracking behaviour requires live observation/measurement.

## 2.3 L1 evidence refinements
- The media directly supports **previous/intermittent belt-to-structure contact** because wear slots/notches are visible in supports immediately adjacent to the running belt path.
- REF-007 also shows **no visible contact at the instant photographed**, supporting the existing qualification that the belt was running clear/centred during the latest observed condition and that continuous contact should not be claimed.
- Belt mistracking remains the **probable** mechanism for prior contact, but the still photographs do not establish the complete dynamic root cause.
- REF-003 to -006 directly show substantial concrete deterioration/spalling and exposed reinforcement in the tunnel/overhead structure.
- REF-008 and -009 directly show severe steel section loss/perforation with corrosion. The degree of remaining structural capacity cannot be determined from photographs and requires engineering assessment.
- The photographs visually support a moisture/corrosion environment, but the exact proportion attributable to fines-retained moisture versus other water exposure cannot be quantified from the media alone.
- Extensive product accumulation is directly verified throughout the L1 area, supporting the existing extensive/widespread classification.
