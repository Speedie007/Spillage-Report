# Visual Evidence Review - Batch E

## Scope
This review covers the fifth visual-evidence batch for the South32 Wessels Mine combined-product spillage report:

- Loading Station L2 - 6 photographs and 1 video
- Loading Station L4 - 8 photographs
- Sample Conveyor 1 - 11 photographs

The review compares the uploaded media against the current contextual narrative. Classification is based on what the media independently demonstrates. Where the original inspection record identifies a material state or mechanism that is not independently visible in the still photographs, that distinction is retained.

## Evidence Classification Key
- **Direct** - the image/video itself clearly shows the stated physical condition.
- **Supporting** - the media supports the finding or extent but does not independently prove the complete mechanism.
- **Context only** - useful for orientation, equipment configuration or inspected-condition record.
- **Source-recorded observation** - an inspection observation retained from the questionnaires/contextual notes where the media does not independently establish the complete proposition.

# 3. Sample Conveyor 1

## 3.1 Overall visual conclusion
The Sample Conveyor 1 photographs strongly confirm extensive/widespread material accumulation throughout and beneath the installation. The evidence shows dry fines, mixed-size/lump product and product distributed onto access surfaces and structural members at multiple elevations.

The strongest visual characteristics are:
- material on the floor and around the base of the conveyor/structure;
- product on stairs and access grating;
- substantial adhered/dried material on horizontal steel members, including drip/run-like deposits;
- large fines and lump-product deposits on platforms and internal structural surfaces; and
- a very large coarse/lump-product accumulation above/within the structure in `LoadoutStationSample1-REF-011`.

The adhered deposits in `REF-005`, `REF-006` and `REF-007` are consistent with material that has at some stage been moist/sticky enough to adhere and form run/drip features. However, a static photograph cannot independently establish that the material was actively wet at the time of inspection or identify the exact water source. The existing "wet fines" classification therefore remains a source-recorded inspection observation, visually supported by adhered/dried deposit morphology but not independently proven as active wet material in this still set.

Likewise, the photographs strongly demonstrate distributed spillage/accumulation but do not independently prove that every deposit originated specifically from belt carryback. Carryback remains part of the source-recorded/mechanism assessment and must be distinguished from the directly visible consequence.

# 4. Batch E Comparison Against Contextual Notes

## 4.1 Findings strengthened by visual review
- L2 clean/no-significant-spillage inspected condition is visually supported by both photographs and the reviewed video sequence.
- L4 dry fines and lump/solid-product spillage is directly verified and remains principally localised to lower channels/structure.
- Sample Conveyor 1 extensive/widespread accumulation is strongly visually verified.
- Sample Conveyor 1 solid/lump-product spillage across access areas and structural members is directly verified.
- Sample Conveyor 1 has substantial adhered/dried deposits with run/drip morphology that visually support a history of sticky/moist material deposition.

## 4.2 Important evidentiary refinements
- **L2:** the clean inspection record is a condition at the time captured, not proof that spillage can never occur under other operating states.
- **L2 duplicate reference:** `REF-005.jpg` and `REF-005.mp4` must remain explicitly distinguished by media type/path.
- **L4:** photographs show accumulated local spillage but do not capture the exact instantaneous material-loss mechanism.
- **Sample Conveyor 1 wet fines:** the still photographs support prior moist/sticky material deposition but do not independently prove active wet fines at the moment photographed; retain wet-fines wording as source-recorded inspection information.
- **Sample Conveyor 1 carryback:** distributed deposits are visually proven, while attribution of all deposits specifically to carryback remains a mechanism assessment rather than something every still photograph independently proves.

## 4.3 No material contradiction identified
No Batch E visual evidence materially contradicts the current contextual narrative. The batch strengthens the contrast between the comparatively clean L2 condition, the localised L4 condition and the extensive/widespread Sample Conveyor 1 condition.
