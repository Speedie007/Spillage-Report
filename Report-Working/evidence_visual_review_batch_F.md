# Visual Evidence Review - Batch F

## Scope
This review covers the sixth and final visual-evidence batch for the South32 Wessels Mine combined-product spillage report:

- Underground Crusher - 5 photographs
- UC05 / UC04 / UC03 / UC02 / UC01 continuous conveyor system - 16 photographs
- U3 Conveyor - 8 photographs
- U5A Conveyor - 9 photographs
- U5B Conveyor - 6 photographs

The review compares the uploaded photographs against the current contextual narrative. Classification is based on what the media independently demonstrates. Where a mechanism such as belt carryback cannot be established from a still photograph alone, the distinction between direct physical evidence and the inspection-derived mechanism is retained.

## Evidence Classification Key
- **Direct** - the photograph itself clearly shows the stated physical condition.
- **Supporting** - the photograph supports the finding or extent but does not independently prove the complete mechanism.
- **Context only** - useful for location/orientation or inspected-condition record.
- **Source-recorded observation / mechanism** - retained from the inspection record where the photograph is consistent with the finding but cannot independently prove the operating mechanism.

# 1. Underground Crusher

## 1.1 Overall visual conclusion
The Crusher photographs directly confirm mixed fines and coarse/lump-product accumulation around and beneath the conveyor/crusher area. The condition is locally significant in individual views but is not shown as uniformly distributed through the full installation, consistent with the existing trace-to-moderate asset-level assessment.

Fine material beneath/adjacent to the belt and return-side structure is consistent with the recorded carryback mechanism, but the still photographs do not independently establish that every deposit originated specifically from carryback.

UGCrusher-REF-001 also shows standing water/puddling around the accumulated product. The visual evidence confirms water was present in that local area, but it does not identify the source or establish a crusher-specific water-loss mechanism.

## 1.2 Item review
- **UGCrusher-REF-001.JPG:** **Direct** - sizeable mixed lump/fines pile adjacent to the conveyor/crusher area with standing water/puddling around the deposit. High report value for overall condition; water source unconfirmed.

- **UGCrusher-REF-003.JPG:** **Direct / Supporting** - fines with scattered larger fragments directly beneath the belt/underside structure; supports return-side accumulation context.

- **UGCrusher-REF-005.JPG:** **Direct / Supporting** - product/fines retained on or behind upper structural members within the guarded conveyor/crusher area; supports distributed contamination beyond floor level.

# 2. UC05 / UC04 / UC03 / UC02 / UC01 Continuous Conveyor System

## 2.1 Overall visual conclusion
The 16-photograph set strongly confirms distributed dry-fines accumulation along the continuous underground conveyor route, with mixed coarse fragments present in some locations. Large local fines mounds are visible beneath and alongside the conveyor at multiple points, while other portions are comparatively clean.

This variation is consistent with the contextual record that part of the UC05-UC03 area had reportedly been cleared shortly before inspection. UGUC05_04_03_02_01-REF-007 directly shows a comparatively clean conveyor-side access route, but the photograph alone cannot prove when or why that area was cleaned; the recent-cleaning explanation remains source-recorded inspection information.

The visual evidence strongly supports the existing moderate, distributed asset-level classification. Some individual piles are locally large, but the photographic set also includes comparatively clear sections; the route should therefore not be reclassified as uniformly extensive/widespread based solely on the strongest close views.

The location of fine deposits directly beneath/adjacent to the belt and support structure is consistent with carryback redistribution, but the static photographs do not independently prove carryback as the sole origin of every deposit.

# 3. U3 Conveyor

## 3.1 Overall visual conclusion
The U3 photographs directly confirm localised-to-moderate fines/mixed-material accumulation around the conveyor installation. The strongest direct conveyor-related evidence is UGU3-REF-004, -005 and -007, which show fines and mixed material accumulated immediately beneath/adjacent to conveyor structures and access platforms.

UGU3-REF-002, -003, -006 and -008 also show coarse broken rock/material along the underground roadway adjacent to the conveyor. Because the roadway is an excavated underground environment, the still photographs alone cannot establish that every coarse fragment visible on the roadway originated from conveyor spillage. Those images are therefore treated as supporting/context evidence rather than proof that all visible rock is conveyed-product loss.

The existing localised-to-moderate classification remains appropriate. No Batch F photograph provides evidence relevant to the previously Unknown trip/restart clearing status.

# 5. U5B Conveyor

## 5.1 Overall visual conclusion
The U5B photographs confirm dry-fines accumulation at several localised points around the conveyor/return-side structure. Individual deposits are sizeable in some views, particularly UG5B-REF-001 and UG5B-REF-005, but the set does not show the same continuous/widespread longitudinal accumulation seen at U5A.

The existing trace-to-localised asset-level classification should therefore be retained, with the refinement that some **individual local deposits are substantial** even though the condition is not visually demonstrated as widespread along the full inspected route.

UG5B-REF-003 and -004 show fine material immediately beneath the return-belt/support region, strongly consistent with the recorded carryback mechanism. As elsewhere, static images do not prove carryback as the sole origin of every deposit.

# 6. Batch F Comparison Against Contextual Notes

## 6.1 Findings strengthened by visual review
- Crusher mixed coarse/fines accumulation is directly verified; local standing water is also visible, with source unresolved.
- UC05-UC01 distributed fines accumulation is strongly verified at multiple locations, together with mixed coarse material in some areas.
- The photographed UC route includes both heavily accumulated and comparatively clear sections, consistent with the source-recorded recent-clearing qualification.
- U3 local conveyor-adjacent fines/mixed accumulation is directly verified.
- U5A extensive/widespread fines accumulation is strongly visually verified.
- U5B localised fines accumulation is visually verified, with some individual local piles sizeable despite the non-widespread asset-level classification.

## 6.2 Important evidentiary refinements
- **Carryback terminology:** the physical fines deposits are directly visible; attribution of every deposit specifically to belt carryback remains a mechanism interpretation unless dynamic evidence is available.
- **U3 roadway rock:** coarse broken rock along the underground roadway cannot automatically be assigned to conveyor spillage from still photographs alone; strongest report evidence should favour deposits directly beneath/adjacent to the conveyor structure.
- **UC05-UC03 recent cleaning:** REF-007 directly shows a comparatively clear inspected condition, but the explanation that the area had been cleared shortly before inspection remains source-recorded information.
- **U5B extent:** retain trace-to-localised overall classification, but note that some individual local deposits are substantial.
- **Crusher water:** standing water is visually present at REF-001, but no source/cause should be assigned without separate evidence.

## 6.3 No material contradiction identified
No Batch F visual evidence materially contradicts the current contextual narrative. The final visual-review batch completes independent inspection of all repository evidence groups and further strengthens the distinction between directly visible material accumulation and inferred operating mechanisms.
